01

Why it works

They've trusted you and they've had a real win. What stands between that and a yes isn't information. It's a handful of beliefs: "this won't work for me", "I've tried things like this before", "I'm not ready".

Phase 3 takes those beliefs apart one at a time, in the open, before it ever mentions a price. By the time the offer arrives, the reasons not to have already been answered.

There's no need to soft-pedal any of it. You gave real value before you asked for anything, so the pitch is a service, not an interruption.

The framework: what happens in Phase 3

Opening the pitch

The close you use here is the one you picked in Chapter 3. Part 5 covers what each close involves.

The words

  • Broken Models: "You've tried [approach]. It fails because [the plain reason]."
  • The bridge: "Here's what [your Event Magnet™] doesn't do: [the next problem]. So you're left asking, [the question]. That's one step of [nine]. Here are the other [eight]."
  • The System Reveal: "[Your Magic Formula™] has [three] phases. [Phase one]: [Hell, why it exists, Heaven, a breath for each]."
  • Belief bust: "You think [the belief, in their words]. Here's what's actually true: [the bust]."
  • Proof: "[Name], [their situation]: [their number]. [Second name], [a different situation]: [their number]. Different [niches], same [outcome]."
  • The offer: "You've seen the what and the why. The way we do this together is [offer name], [what it is in one line]."
  • The price: "Get [offer] today for [price]. [The close instruction, said out loud]."

03

Engaging the room

Don't let the room fall silent just because you've started the pitch. A room that's still answering you is a room that's still with you.

  • Broken Models: "How many of you here have tried [approach]? Give me a YES."
  • The System Reveal: after each part of your Magic Formula™, "Type [one word] if you can see why that matters."
  • Two Identities: ask them to choose out loud. "Type WHOLE in the chat if you're someone who runs the whole thing."
  • Belief → Bust It: "How many of you have had that exact thought? Type ME." Then, after the bust, "Give me a YES if that changes it."
  • Proof: "Type their name if you can see yourself in that story."
  • Before the offer: "Type READY if you want to see how we do this together."

The last one matters most. When the room has asked to see the offer, you're answering a request, not interrupting them.

04

Right and wrong

Crossing into the pitch

✕ Not that

"Sorry, here comes the salesy part."

✓ Do this

"You've seen the what and the why. Let me show you how we do this together."

Apologise for the pitch and the room concludes it's something to be ashamed of. Own the transition, with no apology and no flinch.

Price and value

✕ Not that

The number first, then everything they get.

✓ Do this

Everything they get, then the anchor, then the number.

Say the price first and every good thing after it sounds like you're justifying it.

After the price

✕ Not that

Filling the silence with more explanation.

✓ Do this

Saying the number, giving the instruction, and letting the silence sit.

Broken Models

✕ Not that

Mocking what they've tried.

✓ Do this

Being fair to each approach and blaming the model. They've blamed themselves enough.

05

The steps

  1. List two or three Broken Models. What your room has already tried, and the plain reason each one fails.
  2. Write your bridge. What your Event Magnet™ doesn't solve, and the question that leaves.
  3. Write your System Reveal. Each part of your Magic Formula™ as Hell, why it exists, Heaven.
  4. Write the Two Identities. Keep it respectful on both sides.
  5. List their beliefs and bust each one. Write a blunt version and a kind version of your strongest bust, and pick one.
  6. Line up your proof. A name, a situation and a number for each. Only real ones.
  7. Write the offer line, the stack, the anchor and the bridge.
  8. Write the price line and your close instruction, with the numbered steps they'll see on screen.
  9. Choose your two story bits. One to break a belief, one just before the price.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Price before value. The most common mistake in the whole presentation.
  • Apologising for the pitch. It teaches the room to feel awkward about it.
  • Teaching inside the System Reveal. It shows what each part gets them. It never shows how to do it.
  • Reassuring when you should be proving. "It works for everyone" doesn't break a belief. A person like them does.
  • Inventing proof. Borrowed proof from a credible, named source is fine. Made-up proof never is.
  • Talking after the price. The silence is doing work. Let it.

07

Check your understanding

  1. What are the four beats that open the pitch? Broken Models, the bridge, the System Reveal, Two Identities.
  2. What comes first, the value or the price? The value, then the anchor, then the price.
  3. What do you do straight after you say the price? Give the close instruction and let the silence sit.
