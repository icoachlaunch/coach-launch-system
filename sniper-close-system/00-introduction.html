01

I Think Everybody Has Left

Matthew White, in his own words.

The first virtual event I ever ran, nobody came. I sat there on my own, staring at an empty room, wondering what on earth I'd done wrong.

So I tried again. This time people actually turned up, and I remember thinking this was it, this was the one where it all came together. Then, about 15 minutes in, a message popped up from my MC… "Hey Matthew, I think everybody has left."

Can you imagine what that feels like, when you've poured everything you know into a presentation and the room empties while you're still talking? I'd love to tell you I fixed it the following week. It took me about 14 virtual events to make my first sale.

Here's what I worked out the hard way. It was never my content. It was the sequence and the pitch… and that's the part nobody had taught me.

02

What you're about to build

By the end of this training you'll have your own Sniper Close™ presentation: a live event, written beat by beat, that takes a virtual room from "who is this person?" to a decision.

The promise of the system is simple. It's the closing system that turns your virtual room into 3–5 high-ticket clients, without dreading the money pitch or the awkward silence that follows it.

That promise rests on one idea you'll hear again and again in these chapters: closing a virtual room is a sequence, not a personality. Anyone with a proven offer can follow a sequence.

03

Who this is for

This is for you if you're a coach or an expert with an offer of $3K or more that has already sold, and you want to sell it to a room and not one call at a time.

It's also for you if you've run events before and one of these sounds familiar:

  1. The teaching trap. With no structure, your content takes over and the offer gets squeezed into the last few minutes.
  2. The fear of the money moment. The pitch arrives cold, so it feels awkward even to you.
  3. Thank you… then crickets. The room loved it, nobody bought, and the interested ones ended up back on your sales calls.

If you don't have an offer that has sold yet, start there. A perfect close on an unproven offer is a lot of effort pointed at the wrong thing.

06

How to use each chapter

Every chapter has the same shape: why it works, the framework, the right and wrong versions, the steps, the mistakes to avoid, three questions to check your understanding, and one action.

Read it, answer the three questions, do the action, then move on. Go in order. Every chapter is built on the one before it.

07

Mistakes to avoid before you start

  • Skipping to the builder. The builder is fast, but you'll make better calls inside it once you understand what each beat is for.
  • Rewriting your slides first. Slides come last. The sequence comes first.
  • Waiting until you feel ready. Nobody feels ready. Follow the order and let the presentation do the heavy lifting.

08

  2. What is the one idea the whole system rests on? Closing a virtual room is a sequence, not a personality.
  3. Why do the mechanisms come before the four phases? So you know what each beat is doing before you write it.
