This chapter in one minute
  1. Phase 4 has one job: the decision. Most of the room won't move the first time they hear the offer.
  2. You raise their objections yourself, before anyone types them in the chat.
  3. You show both futures honestly, then make the offer a second time.
  4. You end by handing over your Event Magnet™ you promised at the start, which closes the loop.

01

Why it works

When you make an offer for the first time, a few people act straight away. Most don't, and it isn't because they've decided against it. They're sitting with a practical worry: the time, the money, whether it'll work in their particular corner of the world.

If you stop after the first pitch, those people leave still holding that worry, and "I'll think about it" becomes "I never got round to it."

Phase 4 is for them. It answers the practical worries out loud, shows what staying put actually costs, and gives them a second, clear chance to say yes. A lot of the sales in a room come from this phase, which is why you never skip it.

Beliefs versus objections

Phase 3 dealt with what's going on inside them: "this won't work for me". Phase 4 deals with what's going on around them: the calendar, the bank account, what their partner will say. Keep the two apart and each one gets a proper answer.

The words

  • The Tour: "The minute you join, you'll [the first thing they do]. By [day or week], you'll [the next thing]. By [day or week], you'll [their first win]."
  • The Fork in the Road: "Keep [the path they're on, named], and let's be honest about what that costs: [their arithmetic, worked through]. Or take [the offer]: [the new picture], starting [when]."
  • The send-off: "Here's [your Event Magnet™], as promised. [The callback to your opening]. [The one line you want them repeating]."

03

Engaging the room

By Phase 4 the room knows how to answer you, so use it to bring the undecided back in.

  • Objection → Handle It: name the objection as a universal question. "How many of you here have thought, 'I just don't have the time right now'? Give me a YES." Then answer it, and ask, "Type YES if that helps."
  • The Tour: "Type WEEK ONE if you can picture yourself there."
  • The Fork in the Road: "Type the number of the path you're choosing."
  • The Re-Pitch: ask the people who've said yes to type IN, so the rest of the room can see others deciding.
  • Questions and Answers: invite the questions by name, and thank each person who asks.

04

Right and wrong

The Tour

✕ Not that

"You get twelve modules, a community and weekly calls."

✓ Do this

"The minute you join, you'll… By the end of week one, you'll…"

Every sentence starts with what they'll do or feel. A "you get" sentence is the value stack leaking back in.

The Fork in the Road

✕ Not that

"If you don't act now, you'll regret it."

✓ Do this

Where they'll be in 90 days on each path, with the cost of staying put worked out in numbers.

Price the "don't" side with arithmetic, not adjectives. And describe the path they're on with respect. It isn't a foolish choice. It's just the one that keeps them where they are.

Urgency

✕ Not that

A countdown that resets, or a limit you made up.

✓ Do this

A true reason: a group that starts on a date, a bonus that genuinely ends.

Made-up urgency destroys trust the moment someone notices it, and someone always notices it.

How many times you pitch

✕ Not that

Once, then straight to questions.

✓ Do this

Twice, with content between the two that earns the second.

05

The steps

  1. List the three to five practical objections your room has. Only the ones they really raise.
  2. Write your answer to each. Short and honest.
  3. Write the Tour. Their first two weeks, every sentence starting with "you'll".
  4. Write the Fork in the Road. Two versions, then pick one. Work the arithmetic through.
  5. Write down your true reason to decide now. If you haven't got one, don't invent one.
  6. Write the Re-Pitch. The offer, the close instruction, and what happens the moment they say yes.
  7. Prepare for questions. Note the three you're most likely to be asked and how each answer leads back to the offer.
  8. Write the send-off. Your Event Magnet™, the callback, the line.
  9. Choose your story bit for the biggest objection.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Pitching once. Two passes at the offer is the minimum.
  • Skipping the Fork in the Road. Without both futures in front of them, people default to "I'll think about it."
  • Raising objections they don't have. Every objection you introduce that wasn't there creates resistance that wasn't there.
  • A guarantee that's vague or made up. Say exactly what's guaranteed, or leave it out.
  • Forgetting to hand over your Event Magnet™. You promised it in the first five minutes. Breaking that promise at the end undoes the trust you spent the whole event building.

07

Check your understanding

  1. Who is Phase 4 for? Everyone who didn't move on the first pitch.
  2. What's the difference between Phase 3's beliefs and Phase 4's objections? Beliefs are inside them ("it won't work for me"). Objections are practical (time, money, their situation).
  3. What closes the loop you opened in Phase 1? Handing over your Event Magnet™ in the send-off.
