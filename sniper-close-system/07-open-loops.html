Open Loops

Read~5 min
DoOne action
Part2 · The Mechanisms
ThenThe Token
This chapter in one minute
  1. An open loop is a promise or a question you raise and deliberately leave unfinished.
  2. Its job is to keep people in the room to the end, and to keep them moving with you from one part to the next.
  3. Phase 1 of the Sniper Close Sequence is named for it: WHO · Open Loop.
  4. Every loop you open, you close. A loop left hanging is a broken promise.

01

Why it works

Think about the last series you watched late at night. The episode ended on a question, and you told yourself "just one more" even though you had to be up early. Nobody forced you. The unanswered question did it.

A virtual room is full of people who are one click away from leaving, and most of them decide in the first few minutes whether they'll stay. Good content doesn't hold them, because they don't know it's good yet. What holds them is something they want that they haven't got yet.

That's an open loop. You show them what's coming, you make them want it, and you tell them when they'll get it. Now staying is the easy choice and leaving costs them something.

02

The framework: three kinds of loop

LoopWhat you openWhen it closes
The big loopYour Event Magnet™, shown early. "Stay to the end and it's yours."At the very end, when you hand it over
The question loopThe question they've been asking themselves, said out loud and left unansweredWhen your presentation answers it
The small loopsA line at the end of one part that points at the next. "In a minute I'll show you why that happens."A few minutes later, in the next part

The big loop

This is the one that holds the whole event together. In Phase 1 you hold up your Event Magnet™ so they can see it, you tell them in one line what it does, and you tell them it's theirs at the end.

Two things make it work. They have to be able to see it, because people stay for a thing and not for a description of a thing. And you have to want them to have it, so the promise is real when you keep it.

Your Event Magnet™ is a critical piece of your presentation, so this training shows you what one looks like and how it works in the room. The builder that makes yours is part of the full system.

The question loop

You open your presentation with your $Million Promise™ and the question your room has been quietly asking. Then you don't answer it. You let it sit while you tell them who this is for, name the enemy and tell your $Million Moment™. By the time you answer it, they've been waiting for it.

The small loops

These are the joins between the parts of your presentation. Every time you finish one part, you point at the next one before you start it, so there's never a natural place to leave.

A callback is a small loop too. You plant a picture early and come back to it later, and the room gets the small pleasure of recognising it.

03

Engaging the room

A loop gets stronger when the room says out loud that they want it closed.

  • When you open the big loop: "Who wants this? Give me a YES in the chat."
  • Part-way through: "How many of you are still here for [your Event Magnet™]? Type STILL HERE."
  • Before a small loop closes: "Type READY if you want to see why that happens."
  • When you close the big loop: "Type GOT IT when it's in your hands."

Each one reminds them what they're waiting for, and puts their own words behind it.

04

Right and wrong

Opening the loop

✕ Not that

"We've got a lot of great content today, so stick around."

✓ Do this

"This is [your Event Magnet™]. [One line on what it does]. Stay to the end and it's yours."

The first is a vague promise. The second is a thing they can see and a time they'll get it.

Keeping it open

✕ Not that

Showing it in the first five minutes and never mentioning it again.

✓ Do this

Coming back to it at the start of each phase: "And remember, this is yours at the end."

Closing it

✕ Not that

Running out of time and saying "I'll email it to you."

✓ Do this

Handing it over in the room, as promised, and calling back to where you first showed it.

The joins

✕ Not that

"Okay, so that's that. Next slide."

✓ Do this

"That explains why it keeps happening. In a minute I'll show you the one thing that stops it."

05

The steps

  1. Write your big loop in one sentence. What it is, what it does, and "stay to the end and it's yours."
  2. Write the question you'll leave unanswered. The one your room has been asking themselves.
  3. Mark every join in your presentation. Wherever one part ends and the next begins, write one line that points forward.
  4. Mark where you'll remind them of the big loop. At least once in each of the four phases.
  5. Check every loop closes. Go through and tick each one off against the place it gets answered.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Closing the big loop early. Hand over your Event Magnet™ in Phase 1 and you've removed the reason to stay.
  • A loop you can't see. "Great content" isn't a loop. A thing they can picture is.
  • Opening it once and forgetting it. People join late and people drift. Remind them.
  • Never closing it. If you promised it, hand it over. A broken promise at the end undoes the trust you spent the whole event building.
  • Opening too many. A room can hold one big loop and a few small ones. Open a dozen and none of them matter.

07

Check your understanding

  1. What is an open loop? A promise or question you raise and deliberately leave unfinished, so people stay to see it closed.
  2. What is the big loop in your presentation? Your Event Magnet™: shown in Phase 1, handed over at the very end.
  3. What does a small loop do? It joins one part to the next, so there's never a natural place to leave.
