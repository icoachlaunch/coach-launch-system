This chapter in one minute
  1. There are four closes: The ASK™ Close, The JOIN™ Close, The PASS™ Close and The SEAL™ Close.
  2. Each one asks the room for a different size of yes, and each one belongs to a different kind of event.
  3. The letters of each name are the steps of its flow.
  4. You only need one for your next event. You picked it in Chapter 3.

01

Why it works

Think about how differently you'd treat someone you met an hour ago and someone you'd spent a whole weekend with. After an hour you might swap numbers. After the weekend you might go into business together.

The mistake most people make at the end of an event is using the same close no matter how long they've had the room. They ask a room they've known for a short while to make the kind of decision that needs a weekend, or they spend three days with people and then only ask them to book a chat.

The Four Closes fix that. Each one is sized to the trust the event has built, so the step you're asking for always feels like the natural next one.

The ASK™ Close

Ask · Share · Kickstart

This is where most people start, and it's the one this training takes furthest.

  1. Ask. At the end of your presentation, you invite the right people to apply. They fill in an application form on its own page and book a time.
  2. Share. The application call. They share where they are, and you share the path.
  3. Kickstart. The yes happens on the call, and it's followed by an onboarding call.

The call is a one-call close. It's a set of specific questions, asked in order, that lead the person to close themselves. You aren't convincing anyone. The presentation did the convincing. The call is where they say it out loud.

What it looks like from the room: nobody is asked for money. The step is small and it filters for you, because the people who apply are the ones worth talking to.

The JOIN™ Close

Jumpstart · Onboard · Implement · Next Level

  1. Jumpstart. They join in the room, and they get a fast first win.
  2. Onboard. An enrollment call, within days.
  3. Implement. The first month of the work.
  4. Next Level. The step up from there.

What it looks like from the room: this is your first one-to-many close. The offer is made once, to everyone, and people join while you're still on screen.

The PASS™ Close

Paid Audit or Strategy Session

PASS™ is a name, not a set of steps. The flow is: they book, they pay, you run the session, they get a written report, and the next offer is made from there.

  • An Audit finds the gaps.
  • A Strategy Session delivers an outcome.

What it looks like from the room: you're pitching from the stage, and what you're selling is a paid session with you. The people who take it have paid to sit down and look at their own situation, which is a very different conversation from a free call.

The SEAL™ Close

Secure · Engage · Activate · Launch

  1. Secure. The non-refundable deposit, taken from the stage.
  2. Engage. The onboarding call.
  3. Activate. The balance, paid before the program starts.
  4. Launch. The program starts.

What it looks like from the room: the full offer, made to a room that has spent days with you. They don't pay the whole amount on the spot. They secure their place.

What this chapter gives you, and what it doesn't

You now know what each close is, what the room does, and the order its flow runs in. That's enough to choose yours and to see where your presentation is heading.

The full design of each close is its own piece of work: the pages, the words, the questions on the call, the session itself, the terms of the deposit. That's part of the full system.

03

Engaging the room

The close is the one place people expect you to stop asking questions and start talking at them. Don't.

  • Before you name the step: "How many of you already know you want help with this? Give me a YES."
  • When you give the step: tell them exactly what to click, then "Type DONE when you've done it."
  • As people act: read their names out. A room that sees other people moving, moves.
  • For the ones still deciding: "Type QUESTION if something's holding you back." That's your re-pitch in Chapter 13.

05

The steps

  1. Go back to your sentence from Chapter 3. "My next event is a ______, and I'll close it with The ______ Close."
  2. Read the flow for your close above, letter by letter.
  3. Write what the room does, in one sentence a stranger could follow.
  4. Write what happens next for someone who says yes. Every close ends on a call.
  5. Write the one question you'll ask the room straight after you give the step.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing the biggest close because it's the biggest. The close fits the event. It isn't a measure of ambition.
  • Offering more than one. Two choices and the room chooses neither.
  • Leaving out what happens after the yes. People hesitate when they can't see the next step.
  • Going quiet at the close. Keep asking, keep reading names, keep the room moving.
