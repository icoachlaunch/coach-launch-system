Running the Virtual Room

Read~5 min
DoOne action
Part4 · The Delivery
ThenThe Four Closes
This chapter in one minute
  1. A virtual room has three jobs: presenting, running the chat, and running the tech. One person can't do all three well.
  2. Your MC opens the room, introduces you, keeps the chat alive and keeps the clock.
  3. A full rehearsal and a tech check take the fear out of the day.
  4. When something breaks, and it will, the room follows your lead. Stay calm and carry on.

01

Why it works

Think about a good restaurant. The chef isn't seating people, taking coats and fixing the till. Someone else does that, so the chef can cook.

When you present, run the chat and manage the tech all at once, each job gets a third of you. You miss the question in the chat because you're telling a story. You lose your place in the story because someone can't hear. And the room can tell, because your attention keeps leaving them.

The room should only ever get one thing from you: all of your attention. Everything else needs somewhere else to go.

There's a second reason. Some things land better when someone else says them. An MC can tell the room how good you are and what you normally charge. If you say it yourself, it's bragging. If they say it, it's an introduction.

02

The framework: three jobs

JobWhat it coversWho does it
PresentingThe four phases. Nothing elseYou
The chatWelcoming people, answering simple questions, dropping links, feeding you the questions worth answering, celebrating the people who actYour MC
The techLetting people in, sound and screen, the recording, fixing whatever breaksYour MC, or a third person on a bigger event

If it's truly just you, pick one helper for the day, even a friend, and give them the chat. It's the job that makes the biggest difference.

What your MC does

  • Opens the room. Greets people by name as they arrive and gets them typing before you appear.
  • Introduces you. Your real credibility and what you normally charge, so you never have to talk yourself up and the offer later feels small beside your value.
  • Runs the chat. Answers the easy questions so you don't have to stop, and passes you the ones worth answering out loud.
  • Feeds the energy. Types the first YES when you ask for one. A room responds faster when someone goes first.
  • Drops the link. When you give the step for your close, it's in the chat at that moment, and again in the re-pitch.
  • Names the people who act. "Welcome, Sarah!" as people take the step.
  • Keeps the clock. Holds the run sheet from Chapter 17 and signals you privately.

Before the day

A full rehearsal. Everyone who has a job, in the real room, running the real thing: the introduction, the screen shares, the handover, the link.

A tech check, twice. Once in the rehearsal and again shortly before you go live. Sound, camera, lighting, screen share, the link for your close, and the page it goes to.

A backup for the three things that fail. A second way to get online, a copy of your slides your MC can share if yours freezes, and the link for your close saved where both of you can reach it.

Opening the room

People who arrive to silence leave. Have your MC in the room before the start, welcoming people and asking an easy question so the chat is already moving when you begin.

When something breaks

Something will: your sound, a slide, the link. The room takes its cue from you. If you panic, it's a disaster. If you smile and say "give me ten seconds," it's nothing.

Your MC covers while you fix it: a question for the room, a quick recap, a reminder of what's coming at the end. Agree that in advance.

The difficult attendee

Now and then someone uses the chat to argue, or to sell their own thing. Your MC deals with it quietly, with a private message first and removal if it carries on. You never stop the presentation for it.

The recording

Decide before the event what happens to the recording, and say only what's true. If the replay isn't being given away, don't promise it in passing to keep people happy. A reason to be there live is part of what keeps the room with you.

03

Engaging the room

This chapter is where Chapter 6 gets its team.

  • Your MC goes first. When you ask for a YES, theirs is the first one in.
  • Your MC reads the room for you. A private note: "chat's slowed down" or "lots of questions about price."
  • You say the names, your MC types them. Between you, nobody who responds goes unnoticed.
  • Questions get collected, not lost. Your MC keeps a list so the Q&A in Phase 4 starts with the best ones.

04

Right and wrong

Who does what

✕ Not that

Presenting while scanning the chat and admitting latecomers.

✓ Do this

You present. Your MC has the chat and the door.

The introduction

✕ Not that

"Hi, I'm… and let me tell you a bit about my background."

✓ Do this

Your MC introduces you, and you open with your hook.

The link

✕ Not that

"I'll find the link in a second… hang on…"

✓ Do this

In the chat the moment you give the step, from your MC.

When it breaks

✕ Not that

"Oh no, this always happens, I'm so sorry, this is so embarrassing."

✓ Do this

"Give me ten seconds." Your MC asks the room a question while you fix it.

05

The steps

  1. Choose your MC and give them this chapter.
  2. Write their introduction of you with them: your credibility and what you normally charge.
  3. Give them the run sheet and agree your private signals.
  4. List the links they'll drop and exactly when.
  5. Agree what happens when something breaks: who covers, and with what.
  6. Run a full rehearsal in the real room.
  7. Do the tech check again shortly before you go live.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Doing it all yourself. The room gets a third of you.
  • No rehearsal. The first time you share your screen shouldn't be in front of the room.
  • An empty chat at the start. The first people in decide the tone for everyone after them.
  • Stopping for one difficult person. That's your MC's job, done quietly.
  • Apologising for the tech. Fix it, smile, carry on. They'll forget it if you do.

07

Check your understanding

  1. What are the three jobs in a virtual room? Presenting, the chat and the tech.
  2. Why does your MC introduce you? So you never have to talk yourself up, and your normal price is anchored early.
  3. What do you do when something breaks? Stay calm, tell the room you need a moment, and let your MC cover.
