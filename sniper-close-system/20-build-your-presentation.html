01

Why it works

Think about learning to cook from a book. You can read every recipe and understand all of it, and you still can't cook until you've made something. The first dish is never the best one. It's the one that turns reading into knowing.

Your presentation is the same. Everything in this training makes more sense once you've got your own draft in front of you: your hook, your pain, your story, your offer, in order.

The builders are there so you don't start from a blank page. They ask you the questions, one at a time, and put your answers in the right place. The thinking is yours. The structure is done for you.

The framework: three steps

All three are in the Sniper Close™ Launch Kit, in the contents on the left.

Step 1 · The workbook

If you've done the action at the end of each chapter, most of this is already written. Put it all in the workbook. The Script Builder asks for exactly these answers, so the more you've filled in, the faster it goes.

Step 2 · The Script Builder

It asks about your event and your close, your room, your story, the one piece you teach and your offer. Then it writes one phase at a time and waits for you to approve it before the next.

Every beat comes with three lines: what's on the slide, what you say, and what you ask the room.

It has ten story bits built in: the basics from the Story-Bit Library in Chapter 8. It picks the ones whose target matches what your room believes, tells you which it chose and why, and writes each one out in its six parts. If you have a true story of your own for that moment, yours wins.

It will never make up a story, a result or a number for you. Where you don't have something yet, it marks the gap and lists them all at the end.

Step 3 · The Slide Builder

Once you're happy with the script, the Slide Builder turns it into your deck. It doesn't write or change a word. Your notes sit behind each slide, and the question for the room sits at the bottom of it.

The one thing the kit doesn't build

Your Event Magnet™. The Script Builder asks what tool you have for your one piece. If you have one, it uses it. If you don't, it marks the place and carries on, and that's the piece to get in place before you go live.

After the builders

  1. Read your script out loud, once, before you change anything. You'll hear what needs fixing.
  2. Fill the gaps it listed.
  3. Write your run sheet (Chapter 16).
  4. Brief your MC (Chapter 17).
  5. Rehearse it in the real room.
  6. Run it. Then run it again. It gets better every time you do.

03

Engaging the room

The Script Builder puts a question for the room in every beat, so you won't have to remember to add them. When you read your draft, check two things:

  • Could nearly everyone in your room say yes to each question? If only a few could, change it.
  • Is there a word you want them typing? Choose it now, and use it at the moments that matter.

05

The steps

  1. Open your Sniper Close™ Launch Kit.
  2. Download the workbook and fill it in.
  3. Copy the Script Builder prompt into a new chat and answer its questions.
  4. Approve each phase as it's written.
  5. Save your finished script.
  6. Copy the Slide Builder prompt into a new chat and paste your script.
  7. Read the script out loud with the slides, once, before you change anything.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the workbook. The builder is only as good as what you bring to it.
  • Letting the AI invent. If it ever offers a story or a number you didn't give it, delete it.
  • Changing the order. Four phases, in order. Change the words as much as you like.
  • Editing the slides and not the script. Change the script first, then rebuild the slide, so they always match.
  • Not running it. A presentation nobody has heard isn't finished. It's untested.
