This chapter in one minute
  1. The Four Closes aren't four options to pick from. They're a sequence, and you climb it one close at a time.
  2. The order is ASK™ → JOIN™ → PASS™ → SEAL™.
  3. You add one new close at a time, and the earlier ones stay available to you.
  4. Inside your presentation, the close always sits in the same place: the end of Phase 3, and again in Phase 4.

01

Why it works

Think about learning to drive. Nobody starts on the motorway. You start in a quiet car park, then a back street, then a main road, and by the time you reach the motorway your hands already know what to do.

Each close asks more of you than the one before it. The ASK™ Close asks you to invite people to a call. The JOIN™ Close asks you to make an offer to a whole room for the first time. The PASS™ Close asks you to pitch from the stage. The SEAL™ Close asks you to hold a room for days and take deposits at the end of it.

If you start at the top, you're learning to present, to hold a room and to close all at once. If you climb, each close only adds one new thing, and everything underneath it is already working.

02

The framework: the ladder

RungCloseWhat's new at this rung
1The ASK™ CloseRunning the event at all, and inviting the right people to apply
2The JOIN™ CloseMaking an offer from the room: your first one-to-many close
3The PASS™ ClosePitching from the stage, with a paid session as the step
4The SEAL™ CloseThe deepest immersion, and the deposit close

Three rules of the ladder

1. One new close at a time. You run a close until it works, then you add the next. You earn your way up.

2. The earlier closes stay available. Moving up doesn't mean leaving one behind. Someone running The JOIN™ Close can still invite a person to an application call.

3. Every close ends on a call. An application call, an enrollment call, the session itself, or an onboarding call. Nobody says yes and is then left wondering what happens next.

How the close and the event move together

This is Immersion = Conversion from Chapter 2, seen from the other side. As you climb the ladder, the event gets longer, because a bigger yes needs more time with you. If you want a high-ticket decision from the room, the room needs the time to get there.

Where the close sits in your presentation

Whichever close you're running, it lands in the same two places.

WhereWhat happens
Phase 3 · WHYYour full Magic Formula™ opens the pitch, you clear the beliefs in the way, you prime the room that an offer is coming, you make the offer, and you give the one step. This is the Sniper Close
Phase 4 · WHENYou answer what's stopping the people who didn't move, and you make the offer again. Same close, same step

So the close is never a slide you bolt on at the end. Everything from the first minute has been leading to it: the open loop, the win from your Event Magnet™, the Token they can see, the stories that cleared the beliefs. The close is where all of it gets asked for.

The whole sequence, start to finish

  1. Before the event, you choose your close from your event.
  2. In Phases 1 and 2, you earn the right to make it.
  3. In Phase 3, you make the offer and give the step.
  4. In Phase 4, you make it again.
  5. After the event, the call that every close ends on.

What's held back

This training gives you the sequence and the presentation that leads into it. What each close needs behind it is its own build: the page they land on, what's said on the call, how the session runs, how the deposit and the balance are handled, and what follows the yes. That's part of the full system.

03

Engaging the room

The sequence only works if the room moves at each point you ask.

  • When you prime the offer: "Do you want me to show you how we do this together? Give me a YES." You've now got their permission to pitch.
  • When you give the step: one instruction, then "Type DONE."
  • In the re-pitch: "Type the one thing that's holding you back." Then answer those, out loud, by name.
  • At the send-off: "Type GOT IT when your Event Magnet™ is in your hands."

04

Right and wrong

Where you start

✕ Not that

A multi-day event and a deposit close as your very first event.

✓ Do this

The first rung, run until it works, then the next.

When it isn't working

✕ Not that

Jumping to a bigger close because the smaller one isn't converting.

✓ Do this

Fixing the presentation at the rung you're on. A close that doesn't work small won't work big.

The pitch

✕ Not that

The offer arriving out of nowhere in the last few minutes.

✓ Do this

The room primed, asked for a YES, and then shown the offer.

After the yes

✕ Not that

A payment or a form, and then silence.

✓ Do this

A clear next step: the call their close ends on, and how to book it.

05

The steps

  1. Mark the rung you're on. Be honest: it's the close you can run well today.
  2. Write the one new thing that rung asks of you.
  3. Find the two places your close lands in your own presentation: the end of Phase 3 and the re-pitch in Phase 4.
  4. Write the line that primes the room before your offer, and the YES you'll ask for.
  5. Write the call your close ends on, and how someone books it.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping rungs. Each one teaches you something the next one needs.
  • Treating the closes as a menu. They're a sequence. One per event.
  • Changing the close in the re-pitch. Phase 4 repeats the same step. A new offer at the end tells the room the first one wasn't real.
  • Thinking the close is the last ten minutes. It's the whole presentation. The last ten minutes is just where you ask.

07

Check your understanding

  1. What is the order of the ladder? ASK™, then JOIN™, then PASS™, then SEAL™.
  2. What happens to the earlier closes when you move up? They stay available.
  3. Where does the close sit in your presentation? At the end of Phase 3, and again in the re-pitch in Phase 4.
