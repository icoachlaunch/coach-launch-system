02

What the Sniper Close™ doesn't solve

The Sniper Close™ gives you the order and the presentation. It was never meant to do the rest, and it's better to say so plainly.

It doesn't build the offer your close is selling. A perfect close on the wrong offer is a lot of effort pointed at the wrong thing.

It doesn't fill the room you're closing. The best presentation in the world, given to nobody, closes nobody.

It doesn't make your Event Magnet™. You've seen what one looks like and how it works. The builder that makes yours is part of the full system.

It doesn't design what's behind your close. You know what each of the Four Closes is and the order its flow runs in. The pages, the call and what follows the yes are their own build.

So you're left holding a question, and it's the right one to be holding:

"I know how to close the room… but what am I closing them into, and how do I fill it?"

That's step four of nine. And the order is everything… perfect your close before your offer is right and you're pitching your heart out to the wrong thing.

Here are the other eight.

03

The whole system: The $100K Day Engine™

Three pillars, nine steps. Each step exists because of a problem you'll recognise.

Pillar 1 · Offer Matrix™

Before a single ticket is sold, you need a high-ticket offer the room can't say no to.

Step 1 · $Million Story™ Everyone in your market sounds the same, and cold traffic can't tell what they're buying. That's why this step exists. You get a brand story, message and promise that makes your ideal client stop and say "that's me."

Step 2 · Magic Formula™ With no signature system, you compete on price and stack bonuses to get a yes. That's why this step exists. You get the unique ingredients that make you irreplaceable and the competition almost irrelevant.

Step 3 · Red Diamond Offer™ An offer built for one-to-one calls, not a virtual room, means you undercharge and over-deliver. That's why this step exists. You get an offer so strong the only logical answer from your room is yes, and raising your prices becomes natural.

Pillar 2 · Money Magnet™

This is where the money gets made: before, during and after the event.

Step 4 · Sniper Close™ ✓ You are here The pitch doesn't land, so you end up back on the calls. That's why this step exists, and you've just done it.

Step 5 · Cash Flow Engine™ Registrations bring in no cash, so the ads never pay for themselves. That's why this step exists. You collect revenue from registrations before the event even starts.

Step 6 · Genie X Converter™ No-shows and non-buyers go cold, and every bit of chasing is done by hand. That's why this step exists. Your show-up, follow-up and nurture run on their own, and sales close while you sleep.

Pillar 3 · Client Flywheel™

Consistency, and the right buyers arriving ready to invest.

Step 7 · Goliath Content™ The content hamster wheel, with nothing that sounds like you at scale. That's why this step exists. You run a content flywheel that produces emails, posts and ads from one source.

Step 8 · Pixie Dust Social™ A small circle, and posting to strangers and other coaches who will never buy. That's why this step exists. You grow an audience of buyers, not onlookers.

Step 9 · Dragon Fire Ads™ Ads that burn money on freebie seekers and leave the room empty or broke. That's why this step exists. You build an ad machine that fills every event with buyers.

Why it's nine and not one

Look at where the Sniper Close™ sits. Three steps come before it, because the close needs something to sell: a message, a system and an offer. Five steps come after it, because the close needs a room, and a room needs filling again and again.

Every step feeds the next. That's why the sequence matters as much as the steps.

Two kinds of people finish this training

The people who stop at one tool. They take the sequence, tidy up their slides and run their next event. Some of them will do well with it. Most will find the presentation is better and the room is still half empty, or the offer still isn't landing, and they'll go looking for the next single piece.

The people who run the whole system. They see that the close was never the whole problem. They want the offer built for the room, the room filled, and the follow-up handled, in the right order, each step feeding the next.

Neither one is wrong. But only one of them is building something that works without them holding every part of it together by hand.
