This chapter in one minute
  1. Phase 1 has one job: trust. By the end of it the room should think "this person gets me, and I need to hear the rest."
  2. It runs from your MC's introduction to your $Million Moment™.
  3. You open a loop here that stays open until the very end.
  4. You don't teach anything yet. Teach before they trust you and nobody is listening.

01

Why it works

Picture someone joining your event. They've got one hand on the mouse, half an eye on their email, and about ninety seconds of patience before they decide whether you're worth staying for.

Nothing you teach in that state will land, however good it is. They don't know yet that you understand their problem, so they've no reason to believe you can solve it.

Phase 1 fixes that before anything else happens. It tells them this is about them, it shows them you've stood where they're standing, and it gives them a reason to stay to the end. Trust first, and everything after it gets heard.

The words

  • The hook: "How many of you [their pain, in their words]? … Here's what almost nobody tells you: [your $Million Promise™]. And by the end of today, you'll have [your Event Magnet™], [the result it gives]."
  • Who it's for: "This is for you if [your Golden Avatar™, concretely]. If you're [who it isn't for], I'd honestly rather you had your morning back."
  • The open loop: "This is [your Event Magnet™]. [One line on what it does]. Stay to the end and it's yours."
  • The pain: "Sound familiar? [Their problems, in their words]. And [the worst one], every single [week]."
  • The enemy: "You don't have a [the thing they blame themselves for] problem. You have a [the real enemy] problem."
  • Your $Million Moment™: "[When]. I'm [where, and who's there]. [The moment, with one line of what was actually said]. [What it cost]. That's where [your Magic Formula™] started."

03

Engaging the room

Phase 1 is where you train the room to answer you. If they sit back and watch for the first ten minutes, they'll sit back and watch when you make the offer.

  • In the hook: open on a universal question. "How many of you here have [their pain]? Give me a YES in the chat."
  • Who it's for: "Type ME in the chat if that's you."
  • The engagement story bit: this is where you set the contract out loud, that today is a two-way event and you'll be asking for their answers all the way through.
  • The pain: after each problem, "Type YES if you've felt that one."
  • After your $Million Moment™: "How many of you have had a moment like that?"

Wait for the answers, read a few names out, and thank them. People respond more once they've seen that responding gets noticed.

04

Right and wrong

The first ninety seconds

✕ Not that

"Hi everyone, before we start, a few quick housekeeping items…"

✓ Do this

"How many of you have a business that works but can't explain it in one sentence? Drop a YES."

The enemy

✕ Not that

"You just haven't been consistent enough."

✓ Do this

"You were taught to sell like a shopfront when you're actually a specialist."

The first version blames them. The second blames the model they were handed, and they've blamed themselves enough already.

Your $Million Moment™

✕ Not that

"I struggled for years before I figured it out."

✓ Do this

A scene: where you were, who was there, what was said, what it cost.

A summary loses a room that was just starting to lean in. The scene does the work.

05

The steps

  1. Write the hook twice. Two different versions, then keep the one nobody else could have said.
  2. Write who it's for and who it isn't. Saying who should leave is what makes "this is for you" believable.
  3. Decide what you'll hold up for the open loop. It has to be something they can see.
  4. Name the enemy. Write a blunt version and a kind version, and pick one.
  5. List their pain in their words. Use the phrases they actually say to you, word for word.
  6. Write your $Million Moment™ as a scene. A time, a place, one line of real dialogue, and what it cost you.
  7. Choose your two story bits. One for engagement early, one for belief after your $Million Moment™. Chapter 9 has the library.
  8. Mark your engagement points. At least one question for the room in every beat.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Opening with your credentials. That's the MC's job. Nobody cares who you are until they know it's about them.
  • Housekeeping first. You have ninety seconds. Don't spend them on where the mute button is.
  • Closing the loop early. If you hand over your Event Magnet™ in Phase 1, you've removed the reason to stay.
  • A story that's a CV. Years of experience is a fact. The night it all changed is a story.
  • Borrowing someone else's story as your own. It works until someone in the room has heard it before.

07

Check your understanding

  1. What is the one job of Phase 1? Trust. They should think "this person gets me, and I need to hear the rest."
  2. Who introduces you, and why? Your MC, so you never have to talk yourself up and your normal price is anchored early.
  3. When does the open loop close? At the very end, when you hand over your Event Magnet™.
