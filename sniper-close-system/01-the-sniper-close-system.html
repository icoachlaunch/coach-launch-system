01

Why it works

Most people build a presentation by asking "what should I teach?" That question fills the whole event with content and leaves the offer hanging off the end like an afterthought.

The Sniper Close™ System starts from a different question: what does this room need to believe, in what order, before saying yes feels like the obvious next step?

People don't decide all at once. First they need to feel that you understand them. Then they need to want what you've got. Then they need to believe it will work for them. Only then are they ready to decide. The four phases follow that order exactly, which is why the order never changes.

The principle to hold on to is this: the whole presentation is the pitch. Every phase sets up the close, so by the time you make the offer it isn't a surprise bolted on the end.

02

The framework: four phases

PhaseIts jobThe room is thinking
1 · WHO · Open LoopTrust"That's me. I need to hear the rest."
2 · WHAT · TokenDesire"I can see the dream, and I want what's in your hands."
3 · WHY · Sniper Close ★Belief"This will work for me."
4 · WHEN · Re-PitchThe decision"There's nothing left stopping me."

Phase 1 · WHO — Open Loop

Open a loop they can't leave until it closes.

  • The MC intro. Your MC opens the virtual room and introduces you as the expert, so you never have to talk yourself up.
  • Act 1 · Serendipity. Open with your $Million Promise™ and the question they've been asking themselves… and leave it unanswered.
  • Act 2 · Connection. Tell your $Million Moment™: you were exactly where they are, and here's the moment it changed.
  • The Enemy and the Guide. Name the old way that keeps failing them, then step in as the guide who's beaten it.
  • The Pain. Say their pain out loud, in their words, until they feel seen.

Phase 2 · WHAT — Token

Show them what's possible, one pain at a time. Then put something of huge value in their hands.

  • Act 3 · Opportunity. Reveal your Event Magnet™, the one piece of your Magic Formula™ you're teaching today: its name and its parts.
  • Pain → Dream × 3. Take three pains and show the dream on the other side of each one.
  • The Results. Show real clients who've already made the trip.
  • The Token. Hold up one thing of huge value that's part of your Red Diamond Offer™, something they can see and want.

Phase 3 · WHY — Sniper Close ★

This is where the room says yes.

  • Act 4 · Remove Risk. Take away the risk of saying yes, so the only thing left to decide is when.
  • Belief → Bust It × 4. Name the four beliefs holding them back and bust each one.
  • The Pitch Primer. Prime the room that an offer is coming, so the pitch never lands cold.
  • Act 5 · Execution. Make the offer and ask for the decision.

Phase 3 is also where your full Magic Formula™ goes on screen for the first time. It opens the pitch. Chapter 3 explains why, and Chapter 11 walks through it beat by beat.

Phase 4 · WHEN — Re-Pitch

Most of the room doesn't buy the first time they hear it.

  • Objection → Handle It × 3. Answer the three objections before they type them in the chat.
  • The Re-Pitch. Make the offer again, for everyone who needed one more pass.
  • Questions and Answers. Take their questions live, and let every answer sell the offer again.

What runs through all four phases: engagement

There's one thing that isn't a step on the page because it happens in every step: you keep asking the room to answer you. "How many of you here have…?" "Give me a YES in the chat." "Type this word if you agree."

Engagement is one of the most important factors in a good event. A room that's answering you is awake, it's agreeing with you out loud, and it's practising saying yes long before you make the offer. A silent room is watching television. Chapter 5 covers it in full, and every phase chapter shows you where the questions go.

The Close Sequence

The four phases end in one close, chosen to match your event. There are four: The ASK™ Close, The JOIN™ Close, The PASS™ Close and The SEAL™ Close. You'll pick yours in Chapter 2, and Part 5 covers what each one is.

03

Right and wrong

Same person, same offer, two different rooms.

The training. Tips from start to finish, slide after slide. No story, no engagement, a quiet room. In the last five minutes: "So, um, if anyone wants help, the link's in the chat." Two people click.

The Sniper Close™ presentation.

  • WHO: the promise, a show of hands, the story, the pain named in their words.
  • WHAT: one piece taught properly, the dream on the other side of three pains, real results.
  • WHY: the beliefs busted, the offer made cleanly.
  • WHEN: the objections handled, the offer made a second time.

The offer never changed. The presentation did.

04

Mistakes to avoid

  • Treating the four phases as a menu. They're a sequence. Running long means cutting depth, never a phase.
  • Thinking the close is the last five minutes. The close starts the moment your MC opens the room.
  • Pitching once. Two passes at the offer, with content between them that earns the second, is the minimum.

05

Check your understanding

  1. What are the four phases, in order, and what does each one earn? WHO earns trust, WHAT earns desire, WHY earns belief, WHEN earns the decision.
  2. Where does your full Magic Formula™ first appear? In Phase 3, opening the pitch. Phase 2 only reveals the one piece you're teaching.
  3. What do you cut when you're running long? Depth, never a phase or a beat.
