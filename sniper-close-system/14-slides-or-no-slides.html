Slides or No Slides

Read~5 min
DoOne action
Part4 · The Delivery

This chapter in one minute
  1. The sequence works with slides and without them. The four phases don't change.
  2. Slides are there for the room, not for you. They're never your notes.
  3. One idea per slide, a few words, and never the sentence you're about to say.
  4. Some moments are stronger with your face on screen and nothing else.

01

Why it works

Think about the last time someone read their slides to you. You'd finished reading the slide before they were halfway through it, and after that you were just waiting for them to catch up.

People can't read one thing and listen to another at the same time. When your slide is full of words, the room has to choose between you and the screen, and the screen usually wins. Now they're reading, not listening, and you've become the voiceover on your own presentation.

A good slide works the other way round. It shows one thing that makes what you're saying easier to hold on to: a few words, a picture, a number. The room glances at it, gets it, and comes back to you.

So the question isn't really "slides or no slides". It's "what does the room need to see at this moment to stay with me?"

Three rules for any slide

1. Cut everything that isn't the point. Decoration, extra logos, a second idea. If it doesn't help them understand this moment, it's competing with it.

2. Point at what matters. Make the one key word or number bigger or bolder than everything else. The eye should land in the right place without being told.

3. Don't put your script on the screen. A few words that anchor what you're saying help. The full sentence you're about to speak makes them read ahead and stop listening.

Running it without slides

You can run the whole sequence with a camera and your Event Magnet™. Some people are better this way, because there's nothing between them and the room.

If you go without slides, three things still have to be seen: your Event Magnet™, your full Magic Formula™, and the step for your close. Hold them up, share them on screen for that moment, or have your MC put them in the chat.

Mixing the two

Most people end up here. Slides for the teaching, the system and the offer, where the room needs to see something. Your face, full screen, for the hook, your story and the moments where you're speaking straight to them.

Switching between the two also resets the room's attention, which is useful when energy drops.

Your notes are somewhere else

Whatever you choose, your script lives in your notes and not on the slide. If you find yourself adding words to a slide so you won't forget them, that's a note. Move it.

03

Engaging the room

  • Put the question on the screen. When you ask the room for a response, show it: "Type YES if that's you." People answer more when they can see what to type.
  • Use the reveal. Show the first line, ask "what do you think comes next?", then show it.
  • Leave the screen for your face when you ask the questions that matter most. A question lands harder from a person than from a slide.
  • Keep the close on screen while you read out names and answer questions. Late deciders need to see the step.

04

Right and wrong

How much is on it

✕ Not that

Six bullet points, each one a full sentence.

✓ Do this

Four words and nothing else.

Reading

✕ Not that

Reading the slide out loud, word for word.

✓ Do this

Saying something the slide doesn't say, while the slide holds the one idea.

The story

✕ Not that

A slide of text behind you while you tell your story.

✓ Do this

Your face, or a single title card.

The close

✕ Not that

Flicking past the step and on to the next slide.

✓ Do this

The step numbered on screen, and left there.

05

The steps

  1. Finish your script first. Slides come last. The sequence comes first.
  2. Go through it beat by beat and write what the room needs to see at each one.
  3. Mark the beats where it's just your face.
  4. Cut every slide to one idea. If it has two, it's two slides.
  5. Check the three that must be seen: your Event Magnet™, your full Magic Formula™, and the step for your close.
  6. Move anything that's really a note into your notes.

06

Mistakes to avoid

  • Building slides before the script. You'll end up writing a presentation to fit the slides.
  • Using slides as a safety net. If you need the slide to know what to say next, you need more rehearsal, not more words on the slide.
  • Hiding behind them. A room buys from a person. Let them see you.
  • Too many for the time. Rushing through slides feels worse to the room than having fewer.
  • No picture of the tool. If they can't see your Event Magnet™, your open loop is a description.

07

Check your understanding

  1. Who are slides for? The room. Your notes live somewhere else.
  2. What are the three rules for a slide? Cut what isn't the point, point at what matters, and don't put your script on the screen.
  3. Which three things must the room see, with or without slides? Your Event Magnet™, your full Magic Formula™, and the step for your close.
