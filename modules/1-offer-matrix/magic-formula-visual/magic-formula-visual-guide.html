What Is the Magic Formula™ Visual?

It's your Magic Formula™ — three phases, nine steps — turned into a single graphic you can put in front of buyers.

You've already built the system. The Visual makes it something people can see in four seconds.

That matters more than it sounds. A system nobody can picture is a system nobody can repeat back to a friend.

What you get is one HTML file. Double-click it and it opens in any browser, animates once, and sits there looking like you paid a designer for it.

No software to buy. No account to make. No designer to brief.

✔ Why a picture beats a list

A list of nine steps reads like homework. The same nine steps in a graphic read like a method.

Same content. Completely different feeling — and the second one is what makes someone think "this person has actually worked this out."

Where you'll actually use it

On the sales page, so people can see what they're buying before they read a word.

In the ad, as the image that stops the scroll.

In your masterclass deck, on the slide where you reveal the system.

In the proposal, the onboarding pack, the pitch — anywhere someone needs to understand your method fast.

What the Visual Is Not

It's not where you build your system. The Magic Formula™ Builder does that. This tool renders a system that's already locked — it will not write one for you, and it will stop if you haven't got one.

It's not a new version of your system. Every word on the graphic comes from your locked Magic Formula™. Nothing gets rewritten, invented, or "improved."

It's not a Coach Launch-branded asset. The graphic carries your colour, your fonts, your mark, your words. Our name appears nowhere on it. It's yours.

It's not a slide deck. It's one graphic. If you want your presentation built, that's the Sniper Presentation™ Slides tool, further along the Formula.

The Three Styles

Same system, same words, three different looks. You pick one — and you can rebuild in another any time without redoing the interview.

Open the style gallery that ships with this tool to see all three side by side with real content in them.

Style 01

Blueprint

What it looks like. An engineering drawing. Light background, fine grid, hairline boxes, technical labels, and a title block in the corner like a real architectural sheet.

The signature. A dimension line runs across the top carrying your promise timeline — "14 days, start to finish." It's the one design where your timeline is drawn as a measurement, which is exactly what it is.

Who it's for. Work where being exact is the selling point. Attorneys, financial advisors, medical, technical consulting, anyone whose buyer is reassured by precision.

Where it works. Proposals, PDFs, printed handouts, slides. It's the only light style, so it's the one that prints properly.

Think twice if. Your brand is warm, playful, or emotional. Blueprint will make it feel clinical.

Style 02

Marquee

What it looks like. A broadcast title card. Dark frame, your system name set huge in caps, solid colour bars for each phase, and the steps on bright white cards underneath.

The signature. On load, a bar of your colour sweeps across the frame and each phase wipes into existence behind it. It's built to be watched, not just read.

Who it's for. Anyone who needs to stop a scroll. Bold personal brands, live events, anyone running ads.

Where it works. Ads, social posts, video thumbnails, the top of a registration page.

Think twice if. You sell quiet, expensive and understated. Marquee is the loudest of the three by a distance.

Style 03

Monolith

What it looks like. Three white slabs standing side by side on a dark ground, each with a coloured seam down its edge.

The signature. A line runs under all three slabs, and your $Million Promise™ sits beneath it. The three phases literally stand on the promise — which is what your system actually does.

Who it's for. High-ticket. Coaches and consultants whose price does the talking.

Where it works. Sales pages, pitch decks, your about page — anywhere it needs to look expensive.

Think twice if. You need maximum impact in a crowded feed. Monolith is deliberately calm.

✔ Not sure? Start with Monolith

It's the most forgiving of the three. It works for almost any niche, and it holds up no matter what colour your brand is.

You can always rebuild in another style later — it takes one sentence.

The Short Display Line

This is the part people rush, and it's the part that decides whether the graphic looks professional.

Your step outcomes were written for a document, where a full sentence is right.

On a graphic, each one gets a box about sixty characters wide — roughly eight words. Longer than that and the design starts to look like a wall of text.

Why the builder makes you approve every line

The graphic is a fixed size. Text that overruns its box doesn't wrap — it gets cut off, and it disappears without any warning.

So the builder shortens each line, shows you all nine side by side with your originals, and won't build until you've signed off. It never trims silently.

How to shorten a line without gutting it

Keep the noun and the verb. Cut the articles, the qualifiers, and the setup.

The test: could someone read it in one glance and know what they get?

Too long: "Know exactly what to do at every night waking — no guessing, no half-asleep forum scrolling."

Right: "Know what to do at every night waking."

Same promise. Half the words. Nothing important lost.

Too vague: "Better sleep for everyone."

Right: "A full night, on repeat."

Short isn't the goal. Short and specific is the goal.

Your System Mark

The mark at the top left is for the system, not your business.

That's a real distinction. Your business name already sits in the corner of the graphic. The mark up top stands for the method itself.

Plenty of coaches have both — a company logo, and a small mark that represents their signature method. If you only have one, use the one that suits the system.

If you haven't got a mark

Say so. The builder will make you a simple monogram from your system's initials in your brand colour.

It's clean, it's yours, and it stops a missing file from holding up the whole build.

✔ File types

SVG is best — it stays sharp at any size. PNG is fine.

Wide logos and square marks both fit; the slot sizes itself to whatever you give it.

The Hot Step, On the Graphic

Your Magic Formula™ flagged one step as the hot step — the hottest ingredient in the whole system, and the seed of your Event Magnet™.

On the graphic it's marked, so the eye goes there first.

This is doing more than decoration. When someone looks at your system, you want them landing on the piece that creates the "I need that" moment.

That's the step that later becomes your free lead magnet, and the one your ads will point at.

Exactly one

Only one step gets marked. If you flag three, nothing is flagged — the eye has nowhere to land.

The builder takes the hot step straight from your Magic Formula™ report. If it isn't flagged there, it asks. It never picks for you.

The Button

Bottom right of every style is a live button. Click it and it goes wherever you point it.

That's what turns the graphic from a diagram into an asset that does a job.

You give it three things

The line
A short question or nudge above the button. "Ready for full nights?"
The label
What the button says. Keep it under about thirty characters or it crowds the corner.
The link
Where it goes. A booking calendar, a checkout, an application, a waitlist — anything.

Write the label as the thing that happens next. "Book a free sleep call" beats "Learn more" every time, because one of them tells you what you're about to get and the other doesn't.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

1. Trying to fix the system while making the picture

You get halfway through and realise step 6 is weak. So you start rewriting it here.

It happens because seeing your system laid out visually is the first time you really look at it. That's genuinely useful — but this is the wrong place to act on it.

It costs you a graphic that no longer matches your locked Magic Formula™, your sales page, or anything else downstream.

The fix: note what's weak, finish the graphic, then go back to the Magic Formula™ Builder and fix it properly. Rebuilding the Visual afterwards takes minutes.

2. Waving through the short lines

The builder shows you nine shortened lines and you say "yep, fine" without reading them.

It happens because it feels like an admin step near the end.

It costs you the one thing everybody actually reads. These nine lines are the whole graphic — the names tell people what the steps are called, the lines tell them what they get.

The fix: read all nine out loud. If one doesn't sound like you, change it there and then.

3. Picking the style you like instead of the style for the job

Marquee looks the most impressive on screen, so it gets picked by default.

It happens because you're choosing while looking at all three, rather than thinking about where the graphic is going.

It costs you when a loud, dark graphic lands in a printed proposal, or a quiet one gets buried in a busy feed.

The fix: decide where it's going first, then pick. Feed and ads, Marquee. Print and proposals, Blueprint. Sales pages and decks, Monolith.

4. Flagging more than one hot step

Every step feels important, so two or three get marked.

It happens because you built all nine and you're proud of all nine.

It costs you the whole point of the mark. Highlight everything and you've highlighted nothing — the eye has nowhere to land.

The fix: one step. The one that creates the fastest "I need that" reaction in a stranger.

5. A brand colour nobody can read against

You give it a pale yellow or a very light grey, and half the text vanishes.

It happens because a colour that works as an accent on a website doesn't always work as a solid block behind white text.

It costs you legibility on the exact elements meant to stand out — the phase bars and the button.

The fix: use your darkest brand colour for this, or ask the builder to check the contrast. If your colour is very light, Blueprint is the style that handles it best.

6. Leaving the button pointing nowhere

The graphic gets built with a placeholder link, and it ships that way.

It happens because at build time you haven't decided where it should send people.

It costs you every click the graphic earns — and a dead button on a sales page looks broken.

The fix: decide the destination before you start. If you genuinely don't have one yet, point it at your main booking page and update it later.
