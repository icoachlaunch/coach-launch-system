What Is the Visual Style Guide?

Your Visual Style Guide is how your brand looks — decided once, held in one place, and read by everything you build after it.

It used to mean a document. A list of colours, a couple of fonts, some rules you hoped you'd remember.

Now it means something more useful: a Brand Kit.

A Brand Kit is a complete look, shipped as a real file. Your hero colour, your text tones, your two fonts, your corner style, your shadows, and three grounds to put them on — light, dark, and your brand colour.

All of it tuned to work together, so you never have to check whether a button reads on a band or whether two fonts fight.

Six Kits ship with this tool. You pick one.

And because it's a file and not a memo, every builder downstream reads it instead of asking you. Your Sniper Presentation™ Slides, your Cash Flow Engine™ funnel pages, your Magic Formula™ Visual, your ads and your posts all load the same look from the same place.

That's the shift. Consistency stops being something you have to remember and becomes something you can't avoid.

1
One Kit. One file. Everywhere. It's the single global place your design lives. Change it there and everything follows. Change it anywhere else and you've started to drift.

It matters because recognition is built on repetition.

When your slides, your pages and your posts share one colour and one pair of fonts, people learn to spot you. That recognition builds trust, and trust is what makes a stranger comfortable enough to buy.

When the look wanders — a different colour here, a random font there — you read as smaller, newer and less certain than you are.

And your look isn't picked at random. It comes from who you are.

That's why this is Foundation 2, built right after your Business Brand Profile. Your personality points at the Kit that fits, and the Builder holds you to it.

What the Visual Style Guide is NOT

It's not a logo. A logo is one small piece of it, and you don't need a fancy one to start — a clean wordmark works.

It's not artwork or a finished website. It's the look those things follow, held in a file a designer, a tool or an AI can read.

It's not six options you keep open. You choose one Kit, and the other five stop existing for your business.

It's not a theme you pick per project. A deck theme, a funnel theme and a social theme is three brands, not one — and the whole point of the Kit is to make that impossible.

It's not your Brand Voice — that's Foundation 3, how you sound. This is only how you look.

How You'll Build It

You build your look in seven moves, each one a decision you lock and reuse forever.

The Builder walks you through them one at a time, always tied back to your personality.

Here's the path: name the feeling your look should give, pick your Brand Kit (the big one), confirm the typography your Kit brings, document your logo or wordmark, define your imagery, see the components your Kit already built, and write your consistency rules.

Notice how much of that is confirming rather than deciding. That's deliberate.

The Kit carries the design decisions — the colour maths, the font pairing, the corners, the contrast. Your job is to pick the one that fits your business, then document the few things only you can know: your mark, your imagery, your rules.

It takes about fifteen minutes.

The rest of this guide teaches each move in full — what it is, why it matters, how to choose yours, and the trap that quietly makes brands look messy.

One feeling → one Brand Kit → everything you build, automatically.
You're choosing, not designing

Most people find this a relief. You don't need to know what a good font pairing is, or which text colour reads on gold. Someone already did that work, and it's in the file.

The one thing you can get wrong is picking for yourself instead of for your business. Pick the Kit that fits the personality in your Brand Profile — not the colour you personally like best.

The 7 Moves, In Full

1

Your Look in a Word — the feeling

Before you pick anything, you name the feeling your visuals should give — bold, warm, premium, playful, clean.

Why it matters. The feeling is the filter every other choice passes through. It's also what points you at the right Kit — a "warm and welcoming" brand and a "premium and calm" brand should never end up in the same one.

How to choose yours

Pull it straight from your personality. A Challenger brand feels bold and high-contrast; a Friend brand feels warm and welcoming; an Expert brand feels premium and calm. Pick the one lead feeling. A hint of a second is fine, but one has to drive.

✓ Do this

"Warm and welcoming." — one clear feeling that matches a Friend personality. Now there's a test every choice has to pass.

✕ Not that

"Premium but playful and minimal but bold." — four feelings fighting. No Kit can satisfy all of them.

The trap: a feeling that contradicts your personality. If they disagree, one of them is wrong — fix it here, before it picks the wrong Kit.

2

Your Brand Kit — the one choice that spreads

This is the big one. You pick one of the six Kits, and it carries your colours, your fonts, your buttons and your corners into everything you ever build.

Why it matters. It's the only choice in this guide that reaches everywhere. Every downstream builder reads it. Get this one right and the rest of your look takes care of itself; get it wrong and you'll be fighting your own brand for a year.

How to choose yours

Open the gallery. Flip through the six on the funnel page, the slide and the post. Then narrow to the two or three your feeling points at, and pick the one that looks like your business — not the one you'd personally decorate a room with.

Then there are two paths, and the Builder will ask which one you're on.

Path A — take the Kit as it is. Fastest, and guaranteed to hold together. Most people should do this. Your OVERRIDES line will say none.

Path B — you already have a brand colour. A logo you've used for years, a colour your audience knows you by. Keep it. You pick the closest Kit for its fonts and feel, swap in your own hero colour, and the Builder records that swap on your OVERRIDES line so every builder applies it.

✓ Do this

"Sovereign, but with my navy as the hero — I've built ten years of equity in it." One Kit, one deliberate swap, written down.

✕ Not that

"Sovereign for the slides, Meadow for the funnel — they're different audiences." That's two brands. The Builder will refuse it.

The contrast check — Path B only, every time

Your colour has to carry the text that sits on it, or it doesn't. Mid-brightness colours — golds, oranges, greens, cyans — can't carry white text. That's not a judgement on the colour, it's physics.

So when you swap a hero, the Builder checks it and sets the text on it to your dark tone if white would be faint. It'll look deliberate — think black type on a bright green button — and people will be able to read it.

The trap: asking for a seventh look, or tweaking every value until the Kit is unrecognisable. The six cover the ground. The flex is your hero colour — everything else stays as the Kit set it, because those settings are what keep your deck matching your funnel.

3

Typography — confirm, don't decide

Two fonts, two jobs. A display font for headings that carries personality, and a body font for paragraphs that has one job: be easy to read.

Why it matters. Fonts set tone before a single word is read, and more than two fonts is the fastest way to look amateur.

How to choose yours

You mostly don't. Your Kit already picked a pairing that works with its colours, both free on Google Fonts and already loaded — nothing to install.

It also sets the size ladder, so a headline on a slide and a headline on your funnel page are the same size relative to everything around them, even though one is on a projector and one is on a phone. That's the bit people usually get wrong by hand.

If you're genuinely attached to fonts you already use, you can swap them in and keep everything else from the Kit. The Builder records that on your OVERRIDES line too — and it'll check the fonts actually load on the web before it lets you.

✓ Do this

"Happy with the pairing." — locked in five seconds, and guaranteed to work with your colours.

✕ Not that

Three fonts, plus a script for paragraphs nobody can read, plus a desktop-only font that won't load on a web page.

The trap: swapping fonts because the Kit's feel unfamiliar on day one. Give it a week on real work first. Familiarity isn't the same as fit.

4

Logo & Wordmark — the mark and its rules

Your logo, or a clean wordmark — your name set in your Kit's display font — plus the simple rules for using it.

Why it matters. A consistent mark, used correctly, signals you're established. And you don't need a designed logo to start. A tidy wordmark in your Kit's font is a completely legitimate brand mark.

How to choose yours

If you have a logo you like, document its colours and check it works on your Kit's light and dark grounds. If you don't, start with a wordmark in your display font, in your hero or text colour.

Either way, set three rules: clear space (breathing room around it), minimum size (don't shrink it to a blur), and versions (one for light backgrounds, one for dark, so it never disappears).

✓ Do this

A clean wordmark in your Kit's display font, with clear-space and light/dark versions defined.

✕ Not that

Waiting months for the "perfect" logo while your brand looks different on every asset in the meantime.

The trap: stalling the whole brand on a logo. A simple wordmark used everywhere beats a great logo used inconsistently.

5

Imagery & Texture — the feel

The style of your photos and graphics, plus any simple recurring element — a shape, a line, a grid.

Why it matters. This is the one thing your Kit can't decide for you. Two brands on the same Kit can look nothing alike because of their imagery, and it's the most overlooked part of a look.

How to choose yours

Match the imagery to your feeling: bright and candid for warm brands, moody and minimal for premium ones, bold and graphic for high-energy ones. Then pick at most one recurring element that shows up across your stuff.

Your Kit helps here too. Any block can sit over a photo, and the Kit lays a wash over the image automatically so your text stays readable on light and dark alike.

✓ Do this

Bright, real, human photos on a warm Ember brand — plus one signature shape used sparingly.

✕ Not that

Generic stock handshakes on a Sovereign brand, with five different graphic styles competing.

The trap: imagery that fights the feeling. Cheap stock on a premium Kit quietly undercuts everything the Kit got right.

6

Components — already built

The small repeating pieces: buttons, headings, cards, callouts, lists, quotes, price blocks. Your Kit built all of them.

Why it matters. This is where the Kit pays off most. Every piece already matches your colours and fonts, so you never style a button again — you just use it.

What ships in every Kit

Buttons, solid and outline, on your hero colour with text that reads on it. Headings, lede and body on your size ladder. A highlighter stripe for one word in a headline. Cards and callouts. Tick and cross lists, and a before/after block. Stats, testimonials, price blocks and a value stack. Countdown timers, announcement bars, order bumps and an FAQ for funnel pages. And a photo layer that keeps text readable over any image.

And three grounds — light, dark, and your brand colour. Same blocks, three moods. The brand-coloured one is the loudest thing you own, so spend it on a peak: one or two bands on a page, not every second section.

✓ Do this

The Kit's standard set, used as shipped. One button style on every asset, without anyone deciding it twice.

✕ Not that

A new button shape and colour on every page because "this one needed something different."

The trap: restyling a component on one page. The moment you hand-pick a colour or a corner somewhere, that page starts drifting from everything else. If a component genuinely needs to change, it changes in the Kit — for everything — or not at all.

7

Consistency Rules — the guardrails

A short do/don't list that keeps you on brand when you're moving fast.

Why it matters. Rules you can recite are rules you'll actually follow. Five lines beat a fifty-page brand bible nobody opens.

How to choose yours

Start from the standard set and trim to what you'll remember: one Kit, everywhere. Never type a colour code anywhere except the Kit. Two fonts, never more. The logo gets clear space and never goes light-on-light. Images match the feeling. When in doubt, more white space, not more stuff.

The trap: writing rules so long you never read them again. Short and firm beats thorough and ignored.

Your BRAND KIT Block — the handle everything holds

When the Builder assembles your Visual Style Guide, the most important part isn't the colour table or the font names. It's three short lines at the top.

BRAND KIT: sovereign FILE: Reference/brand-kit/tokens/sovereign.css OVERRIDES: none

That block is what every downstream builder reads. When your Slides builder, your Funnel builder or your Magic Formula™ Visual asks what your brand looks like, you paste those three lines and it does the rest.

The first line names your Kit. One of the six, by its id.

The second line is the real file your look lives in — the one global place. It sits in the Reference folder of your $100K Day AI Workspace, and you put it there yourself, once: download your Kit's file from the gallery and save it into Reference/brand-kit/tokens/. A builder that can load files loads it; one that can't reads the values from it.

The third line records anything you deliberately kept from your existing brand. For most people it says none. If you kept your own hero colour or your own fonts, it lists exactly those — and nothing else.

BRAND KIT: sovereign FILE: Reference/brand-kit/tokens/sovereign.css OVERRIDES: --brand: #1b2a4a · --ink-on-brand: #ffffff

That's a Sovereign brand that kept its own navy as the hero, with white text confirmed readable on it. Every builder that reads this applies the navy and leaves the rest of Sovereign exactly as shipped.

Where it lives

The block sits at the end of your Visual Style Guide, and your Guide is saved as visual-style-guide.md in the Reference folder of your $100K Day AI Workspace. Your Kit's file lives beside it, in Reference/brand-kit/tokens/ — a folder that holds exactly one file.

There is exactly one of these blocks in your business. If you ever find two, one of them is wrong.

Three ways you'll use it

Running a builder. Paste the block when it asks. That's the whole hand-off. You never answer a colour question again.

Building a page yourself. Load three files, in this order — your Kit, the blocks, then the surface for the medium you're on — and use the blocks. They're already your colours.

Changing Your Kit Later

Your Kit isn't a tattoo. Businesses reposition, audiences shift, a rebrand happens. You're allowed to change it.

What you're not allowed to do is end up with two. Here's how to change it cleanly.

Keeping your own colour or fonts

This isn't really a change — it's the Path B swap from Move 2, and it happens inside the Builder. Your OVERRIDES line records exactly what you kept, the contrast check runs, and everything else stays as the Kit shipped it.

The rule: override the hero colour and its text colour, or the two fonts. Never the corners, the spacing, the shadows or the grounds. Those are what make a Kit a Kit, and they're what keep your deck matching your funnel.

Moving to a different Kit

Re-run the Builder's Kit step, pick the new one, and let it re-issue your Visual Style Guide with the new block.

Then the part people skip: the new block replaces the old one. In your Guide, in your Workspace, everywhere you pasted it. The old block goes.

Anything you already built on the old Kit gets rebuilt on the new one. That sounds heavy, and it isn't — a slide deck rebuilt in a new Kit is minutes, because the words and the structure are separate from the skin. That's the payoff of keeping the look in one file.

Editing a colour on one page

Don't. This is the one change that's never allowed, and it's the one that's most tempting at eleven at night.

The moment you hand-type a colour on one page, that page has left your brand. If a colour genuinely needs to change, it changes in the Kit — through the Builder — so it changes everywhere at once.

⚠ One Kit installed at a time

Replace, never add. Your Visual Style Guide carries one BRAND KIT block. Your Workspace holds one Kit. Every builder you run reads the same one.

The moment there are two, you're back to guessing which one is true — and so is every tool you use. That's the exact confusion the Kit exists to end.

The Hard Rules

These keep your look consistent and repeatable, and the Builder holds you to them.

One Kit, everywhere. Not one for slides and another for the funnel. One look is the entire point.

One Kit installed at a time. When it changes, it's replaced. There's never a second block alongside the first.

Never type a colour code outside your Kit. The Kit file is the only place a colour lives. A hand-picked colour on one page is the start of drift.

Six Kits, not seven. You choose from the six. You don't invent one, and you don't tweak one until it's unrecognisable.

The flex is your hero colour and your fonts. Those can be yours, recorded on the OVERRIDES line. Corners, spacing, shadows and grounds stay as the Kit set them.

Text on your hero must read. If you swap in your own colour, the contrast check runs. White on gold, cyan or green is never shipped.

Two fonts, never more. A characterful heading and a readable body. A third font is where brands get messy.

Pick for the business, not for you. The Kit matches your personality in the Brand Profile, not your personal favourite colour.

A mark with rules. A logo or a wordmark, with clear space, minimum size and light/dark versions.

Imagery matches the feeling. No cheap stock on a premium Kit, no moody minimalism on a warm one.

When in doubt, white space. More room, not more stuff. Space reads as premium and calm.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

Almost every messy-looking brand falls into one of these.

1. Two Kits for two jobs

What it looks like: Sovereign for the slides because "the room is high-ticket," Meadow for the funnel because "it's a warmer audience."

Why it happens: each asset feels like its own project with its own mood, so a different look feels justified each time.

Why it costs you: the person who saw your slides doesn't recognise your funnel page. Every touchpoint restarts trust from zero.

The fix: one Kit. If the mood needs to shift between assets, use the Kit's three grounds — light, dark, brand — not a second Kit.

2. Picking your favourite colour

What it looks like: a bold Challenger brand in Meadow, because the founder likes green.

Why it happens: the gallery is a row of looks, and it's natural to pick the one you'd want in your own house.

Why it costs you: the look and the message pull in opposite directions, and your audience feels the mismatch before they can name it.

The fix: go back to the personality in your Brand Profile and let it choose. The Builder will name the two or three that fit — pick from those.

3. Hand-typing a colour on one page

What it looks like: a slightly different red on the thank-you page because "it needed to pop more."

Why it happens: it's fast, it's late, and it's only one page.

Why it costs you: that page has left your brand, and the next one will too. Drift never happens all at once — it happens one reasonable exception at a time.

The fix: never type a colour code anywhere except the Kit. If a colour genuinely needs to change, change it in the Kit so it changes everywhere.

4. Skipping the contrast check on a swapped hero

What it looks like: white button text on a gold, orange, green or cyan hero you brought in from your old brand.

Why it happens: it looked fine on your screen, and white text on a coloured button is what buttons "usually" look like.

Why it costs you: people can't read your call to action — especially on a phone — so the button that's supposed to make you money doesn't.

The fix: let the Builder run the check every time you swap a hero. If white is faint, the text goes dark, and it gets recorded on the OVERRIDES line so every builder does the same.

5. Tweaking the Kit until it isn't one

What it looks like: new corners here, a different shadow there, a third font for "just the quotes" — until the Kit is a seventh look nobody else can reproduce.

Why it happens: every individual tweak feels small and reasonable.

Why it costs you: the Kit's whole value is that every builder produces the same look from the same file. A customised Kit produces a customised look exactly once, then drifts.

The fix: the flex is your hero colour and your fonts. Everything else stays as shipped. If you want a genuinely different look, pick a different Kit.

6. Keeping the old Kit "just in case"

What it looks like: a rebrand to Ink, with the old Crimson block still sitting in the Workspace and pasted into half the builders.

Why it happens: deleting the old one feels final, and the new one hasn't proved itself yet.

Why it costs you: half your assets come out in the old look, half in the new, and every tool has to guess which one you mean.

The fix: replace, never add. When the Kit changes, the old block goes — from your Guide, your Workspace and every builder — and anything built on it gets rebuilt.

7. Waiting on the "perfect" logo

What it looks like: the whole brand on hold until a logo is finished.

Why it happens: it feels like you can't have a real brand without a real logo.

Why it costs you: months of inconsistent-looking assets while you wait — when the Kit could have made them consistent today.

The fix: start with a wordmark in your Kit's display font. A consistent wordmark beats a great logo used late.

Is It Ready?

This foundation isn't scored out of 25 — a look is judged by whether it's consistent, legible and held in one place. So it passes a Ready Check: every item a clear yes.

  • You've named one clear feeling, and it matches your personality.
  • You've picked exactly one Brand Kit, from the six, for the business rather than for yourself.
  • Your BRAND KIT block is present, names the Kit by its id, and is the only one in your business.
  • Your OVERRIDES line says none, or lists exactly the hero colour and text colour or fonts you kept — nothing else.
  • If you swapped a hero colour, the contrast check ran and the text on it reads.
  • You have the Kit's two fonts, or two of your own that load on the web — never a third.
  • You have a documented mark — logo or wordmark — with clear-space and light/dark rules.
  • Your imagery style matches the feeling, with at most one recurring element.
  • Your components are the Kit's standard set, used as shipped.
  • You have a short do/don't list you could actually follow — and "never type a colour outside the Kit" is on it.
  • The consistency test: a non-designer could build ten assets on this and every one would look like the same brand.

All boxes checked? Your Visual Style Guide is locked — and every asset you make from here reads its look from one place.
