Competitive Landscape

Foundations · Foundation 5 of 5

A clear map of who else is in your space and what makes you different — so the AI can write sharper messaging and position your offer to stand out.

What Is Your Competitive Landscape Doc?

It's a short map of your market — who else serves your people, and why someone picks you.

This isn't about obsessing over competitors. It's about knowing the terrain so you can find your lane.

When the AI knows what else is out there, it can help you sound different on purpose — instead of blending in with everyone selling something similar.

What It Captures

Your market

The space you're in and who else plays here — the broad terrain.

Your key competitors

3–5 real competitors or alternatives. For each: what they offer, roughly what they charge, and what they do well.

How you're different

Your angle against them — not "I'm better," but different in a way that matters to your buyer.

The market gaps

What's missing — what the competitors get wrong, overlook, or ignore.

What your audience does instead

The big one: what your ideal client is doing right now instead of buying — DIY, free content, or nothing at all. Often the real competition.

How You'll Build It

The builder is a relaxed strategy chat — one question at a time.

If you can't name competitors off the top of your head, it helps you think it through ("What would your ideal client Google, or search on YouTube, instead of finding you?").

Fifteen minutes. Then you save it and the AI can position everything you build to stand out.

Is It Ready?

No score — it's a working map. It's done when:

  • Your market is named in a sentence.
  • You've listed 3–5 real competitors or alternatives with a note on each.
  • You've said how you're different — a real difference, not "I care more."
  • You've named what your audience does right now instead of buying from you.

Revisit it as you learn your market — the AI can help you update it anytime.

You can't stand out if you don't know what you're standing next to. Map the terrain once, and every message you build knows exactly how to be the obvious choice — instead of one more option that sounds like all the rest.
