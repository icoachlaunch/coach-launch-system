What Is Your Competitive Landscape?

It's the map of the ground you sell on: who else serves your people, what your buyer actually compares you to, where the gaps are, and the lane you hold against all of it.

Here's the thing nobody tells you about competition. Your buyer never compares you to nothing.

Before they say yes to you, they weigh you against the other coach, the course they bought last year, the free videos they've been watching for months — and the option of doing nothing at all. That whole set is your competition. Not just the people with a title like yours.

Map it honestly and every message you write knows exactly what it's standing next to. Skip it and you sound like one more option that sounds like all the rest.

4
Four kinds of alternative, and most coaches only ever name one. Direct competitors, adjacent solutions, DIY and free, and doing nothing. The last two are usually where the money actually goes.

It matters because "different" is what makes a buyer choose, and you can't be different on purpose from something you haven't named.

One rule runs the whole way through: different, not better. "Better" is a claim your buyer can't check and every competitor also makes. "Different" is something they can see.

What this document is NOT

It's not your positioning. Your lane and your stance are set in your Business Brand Profile. This document reads them and tests them against the field — it never rewrites them.

It's not your pricing. Your three levels and their price points live in Products & Pricing. This document only records where those prices sit against what the field charges.

It's not your buyer. Who they are, their day, their objections — that's The Golden Avatar™. This document records what the market offers them and what they've already tried.

It's not the copy. Your SCORE™ Card and your Enrollment Doc write the finished lines about why you're different. This document hands them the raw material, labelled as raw material.

It's not a research project. Twenty minutes, nine questions, one document — and anything you don't know yet gets marked, not guessed.

How You'll Build It

You build it in six moves. The Builder walks you through them one question at a time, tells you why each question matters, and shows you an example.

  1. Your market. The space you're in, in a line, the way a buyer would say it.
  2. The alternatives. Three to five things your buyer weighs you against — at least one that isn't a coach — and for each: what it offers, roughly what it costs, what it does well, where it falls short.
  3. What your buyer does instead. The real competition, and why it feels reasonable to them.
  4. The gaps. What the whole field gets wrong, overlooks or ignores.
  5. Your edge. Your stance, pointed at each alternative — different, not better. Then where your three levels sit on price against the field.
  6. The before-you pattern. What people have usually tried before they find you, and why it didn't stick.

Nine questions. About twenty minutes. Then the Builder runs a Ready Check and hands you the document.

"I don't know their price" is a real answer

Most people know their craft far better than the field around it. The Builder never pushes you to invent a competitor's price, a weakness you haven't seen, or an edge that isn't true yet.

A guessed price is written down and marked UNVERIFIED, with a note on what to check. A gap you can't see yet is marked none yet. Both pass the Ready Check. A map with honest blanks beats a map full of guesses.

The Six Moves, In Full

Each move is one or two questions. Here's what each one is for, how to answer it well, and the trap that catches people.

1

Your Market — the space, in a line

What it is. The aisle your buyer is standing in when they find you — said the way they'd say it, not the way a brochure would.

Why it matters. The alternatives only make sense inside the space. Name the space and the field sorts itself out.

How to find yours

Ask what your buyer would type into a search box the night the problem gets loud. That phrase is usually your market.

✓ Do this

"Sleep coaching for parents of babies and toddlers — the field is other sleep consultants, a couple of big-name courses, the baby books, and a lot of free content."

✕ Not that

"Personal development." That's a shelf with a thousand products on it, and your buyer isn't standing at it.

The trap: answering with your stance. "I'm the gentle sleep coach" is your edge, and it comes in Move 5. First, the space itself.

2

The Alternatives — what your buyer weighs you against

What it is. Three to five options your buyer considers instead of you, with four things recorded for each: what it offers, roughly what it costs, what it genuinely does well, and where it falls short.

Why it matters. These are the things your messaging stands next to whether you name them or not. Named, you can be different from them on purpose. And the "does well" column keeps you honest — a rival with no strengths is a rival you've imagined.

How to find yours

Start with the two or three names your buyers mention. Then add at least one that isn't a coach: the course, the app, the free channel, or the do-nothing option. If you can't name any, ask what your buyer would Google or search on YouTube instead of finding you.

✓ Do this

"The online course — a self-paced video library with a community, priced like a mid-range course. Does well: thorough, and you can start tonight. Falls short: nobody checks whether you actually did it."

✕ Not that

"Two other coaches. They're not as good as me." No strengths, no price, no detail — and the edge leaking in where the facts should be.

The trap: guessing a price and writing it as fact. The Builder keeps your guess and marks it UNVERIFIED with a note to check their page. A guess dressed as a fact ends up in your messaging.

3

What Your Buyer Does Instead — the real competition

What it is. What your ideal buyer is actually doing about the problem today, instead of buying from you — and why that feels reasonable to them.

Why it matters. Most of the people who should buy from you aren't choosing a rival. They're doing it themselves, watching free content, or doing nothing. That's who you're really up against, and your messaging has to speak to it.

How to find yours

Describe the Tuesday. Not "they don't take it seriously" — what do they actually do? Search at two in the morning? Buy another book? Try a new trick every night?

✓ Do this

"Searching 'baby won't sleep' at two in the morning and trying a new trick every night. It feels reasonable because every trick works for someone, and none of them cost anything."

✕ Not that

"They're lazy." A judgement, not a behaviour — and nothing you can write a message to.

The trap: only naming the buyers who choose a rival. Ask about the ones who don't buy from anyone. They're the bigger group.

4

The Gaps — what the whole field overlooks

What it is. Looking at all the alternatives at once, what the field gets wrong, overlooks or ignores — what your buyer keeps needing that nobody is giving them.

Why it matters. The gaps are where your edge lives and where your content comes from. Everything the field ignores is something you can own.

How to find yours

A real gap is something your buyer would feel. If they wouldn't notice it, it's a note, not a gap. And if the gap is a feature you sell, dig for the need underneath it — speed, accountability, being seen.

✓ Do this

"Everyone sells the method. Nobody deals with the fact that the parent is too tired to follow any method consistently."

✕ Not that

"Their branding is weak." You'd notice. Your buyer wouldn't.

The trap: listing your features as gaps. "Nobody offers voice-message access" is a feature. The gap is what the buyer gets from it.

5

Your Edge — your stance, tested against the field

What it is. Your stance from the Business Brand Profile, pointed at each alternative in turn: against this one, what do you do, believe or refuse to do that they don't — that your buyer would notice? Then, if you've built Products & Pricing, where each of your three levels sits on price against the field, and why.

Why it matters. "Better" is a claim your buyer can't check. "Different" is something they can see, and it's why they choose. Your stance is the edge; this move just aims it.

How to find yours

Read your stance back, then take the alternatives one at a time. The edge against the course is rarely the same as the edge against doing nothing. One line each.

✓ Do this

"Against the online course: I check — every week, someone looks at what you actually did. Against the free videos: one method, in order, instead of a new trick every night. Against doing nothing: a plan that takes less energy than another bad night."

✕ Not that

"I'm more experienced and I genuinely care." Every alternative says that too. The Builder won't write it down.

The trap: rewriting your stance here because the field exposed it. Good — that's the test working. But the stance lives in your Profile. Update it there; this document reads the new one.

6

The Before-You Pattern — what they tried, and why it didn't stick

What it is. What the people who end up working with you usually tried first, and why it didn't stick. Recorded as raw material — the Builder doesn't write the copy here.

Why it matters. This is the pattern your $Million Story™ names, your Golden Avatar™ records as failed attempts, and your SCORE™ Card turns into the block that says why your system works when the usual fix didn't. Capture it once, plainly, and those tools don't have to ask you again.

How to find yours

Two halves, and the second matters more. What did they try? And what did it fail to give them? If the reason is "they didn't try hard enough", dig: what did the thing they tried fail to provide?

✓ Do this

"A course they never finished, then a cheaper coach who gave them a plan and disappeared. It didn't stick because nobody was watching whether they did it."

✕ Not that

"They tried a few things." No names, no reason — nothing a story can be built from.

The trap: polishing it into copy. This is the ore, not the ring. Your SCORE™ Card does the polishing.

A Worked Example

One coach, start to finish. Illustrative — the shape of the answers, not answers to copy.

A sleep coach for parents of babies and toddlers, solo, with her Profile and her Products & Pricing built.

Market: sleep coaching for parents of under-threes. Other sleep consultants, two big-name courses, the baby books, and a great deal of free content.

Alternatives: a well-known online course (thorough, start tonight; nobody checks if you did it; price UNVERIFIED — to check); two other consultants with a similar promise (warm, personal; one method for everyone); the free videos (free, endless; a new trick every night, no sequence); and doing nothing (costs nothing; another bad night).

What her buyer does instead: searches at two in the morning and tries a new trick every night, because every trick works for someone and none of them cost anything.

The gaps: everyone sells the method; nobody deals with a parent too tired to follow one consistently. Nobody checks.

Her edge: her stance from the Profile — "there are gentler routes that work, and the hard way is only faster on paper" — aimed at each: against the course, she checks every week; against the free videos, one method in order; against doing nothing, a plan that takes less energy than another bad night. Her membership sits above the course and below the other consultants, because it has a person in it and doesn't pretend to be 1-on-1.

Before they find her: a course never finished, then a cheaper consultant who gave a plan and disappeared. It didn't stick because nobody was watching.

Twenty minutes, one UNVERIFIED price to check, and a map her $Million Story™, Golden Avatar™ and SCORE™ Card can all read.

The Hard Rules

These keep your map honest and useful to every tool after it. The Builder holds you to them.

Different, not better. "More experienced", "higher quality" and "I care more" are never recorded as an edge. If nothing different lands against an alternative, the document says none yet.

Three to five alternatives, at least one that isn't a coach. The course, the app, the free channel, or doing nothing. That's usually where the money goes.

Every alternative gets its strengths. A rival with nothing going for it is a rival you've imagined, and your buyer knows it.

Nothing invented. No competitor, price, weakness, statistic or trend you didn't bring. A guessed price is kept and marked UNVERIFIED.

No real names the Builder supplies. You name the alternatives. The Builder never names a company, person or product for you.

Your stance is tested here, set in the Profile. If the field exposes it, you go back to the Business Brand Profile and sharpen it there.

Your prices are placed here, set in Products & Pricing. Above, alongside or below — with a reason — and never changed in this document.

The before-you pattern is raw material. It's labelled that way in the document. Your SCORE™ Card writes the finished block.

Nothing from another document. No avatar, no copy, no rewritten positioning, no pricing decisions. Each Foundation owns its own questions.

A marked unknown is fine. UNVERIFIED, none yet, and "build Products & Pricing first" are all real answers and all pass.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

Almost every landscape that doesn't help falls into one of these.

1. "I don't have competitors"

What it looks like: the alternatives section is empty, because nobody does exactly what you do.

Why it happens: you're thinking of competitors as people with your title and your method.

Why it costs you: your buyer is still weighing you against something — the free videos, the course, doing nothing — and your messaging is silent about all of it.

The fix: ask who's getting the money today, and what your buyer does about the problem right now. That's the field. There is always a field.

2. Better, not different

What it looks like: "I'm more experienced, more personal, and I genuinely care about results."

Why it happens: it's true, and it feels like the honest answer.

Why it costs you: every alternative says exactly the same thing, so your buyer can't use it to choose — and the only thing left to compare is price.

The fix: aim your stance at each alternative and name something the buyer could see. "I check every week" is different. "I care" is a claim.

3. Guessing a price and writing it as fact

What it looks like: "The course is about a thousand dollars" — from memory, from a year ago, or from a guess.

Why it happens: an empty cell feels worse than a wrong one.

Why it costs you: the number ends up in your messaging and your price positioning, and a buyer who knows the real price stops trusting the rest.

The fix: keep the guess, mark it UNVERIFIED, and check their page. The Builder does this for you — it never fills the gap itself.

4. Only naming direct competitors

What it looks like: three other coaches, and nothing else.

Why it happens: they're the ones you see at events and in your feed.

Why it costs you: most of your lost buyers never hired a coach at all. They bought a course, watched free content, or did nothing — and your landscape has nothing to say to them.

The fix: at least one alternative from the other three kinds. Ask what your buyer tries before they'd ever think of hiring someone.

5. Rewriting your stance here

What it looks like: the field exposes a weak stance, so you write a new one into this document.

Why it happens: the test worked, and fixing it on the spot feels efficient.

Why it costs you: now your Profile says one thing and your landscape says another, and your Brand Voice and your $Million Story™ were built on the old one.

The fix: go back to the Business Brand Profile, sharpen the stance there, and re-test it here. One source, read by everything.

6. Building the buyer here

What it looks like: the "what they do instead" answer turns into a full portrait — age, income, a day in the life, a list of objections.

Why it happens: once you start describing your buyer it's hard to stop.

Why it costs you: that's The Golden Avatar™, and it asks those questions properly with its own validation. Answering them here means answering them twice, differently.

The fix: here, record the behaviour and the reason. Save the person for Step 1.

7. Polishing the before-you pattern into copy

What it looks like: the last answer comes out as a finished paragraph of marketing — the villain named, the twist landed.

Why it happens: it's the most story-shaped question in the Foundations, and it's tempting to write the story.

Why it costs you: your SCORE™ Card is built to write that block in the right place in the arc. A polished version here gets copied in raw, in the wrong order.

The fix: ore, not the ring. What did they try, and why didn't it stick. Plain words. The SCORE™ Card does the rest.

Is It Ready?

This foundation isn't scored out of 25 — it's the truth about the field, written plainly. So it passes a Ready Check: every item a clear yes.

  • Your market is named in a line, the way a buyer would say it.
