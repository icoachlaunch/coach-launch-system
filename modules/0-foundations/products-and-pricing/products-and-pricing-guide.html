What Is Your Products & Pricing Doc?

It's the plain record of what you sell — laid out as three levels a client can climb, each one named and priced by you.

Most coaches start with one offer and a price they're not quite sure about.

That's fine as a start. It's a problem as a business, because one offer means every month starts from zero. A client buys once, gets a result, and leaves — and you go and find the next one.

This document fixes the shape. You lay out three levels: a membership at the bottom, a faster option in the middle, and closer access at the top. Each one a step up in price. Each one a step closer to you.

That's the ascension model. A client can start small, get a win, and climb — and the people who finish the upper levels fall back into the membership instead of walking away.

What this document is NOT

It's not your Money Model. The Money Model is Step 3 of the Engine — it picks your Accelerator phase, chooses which level is your core offer, runs the maths and locks the price. This document sets the price points the Money Model starts from.

It's not your offer. The value stack, the bonuses, the guarantee and the sales copy are your Red Diamond Offer™ Enrollment Doc. This is the list of what exists, not the pitch for it.

It's not a pricing strategy session. The Builder teaches you the model and tells you where prices usually land. It never sets a number for you, and it never moves one you've chosen.

It's not a business plan. Twenty minutes, thirteen questions, one document.

How You'll Build It

You build it in five moves. The Builder walks you through them one question at a time, tells you why each question matters, and shows you an example.

  1. What you sell today. You list everything, rough prices and all. The Builder places each thing on its level — or tells you it's a front-end product that sits below the ladder.
  2. Level 1 · your membership. What's in it, what it's called, and the annual-first price with the monthly worked back.
  3. Level 2 · your accelerator. Done-for-you or a 3-day event, what it's called, how long it runs, and its price point.
  4. Level 3 · your proximity offer. 1-on-1, retreat or mastermind, what it's called, how many seats, how long it runs, and its price point.
  5. Your revenue goal. One real number with a date, for the Money Model to work with.

Thirteen questions. About twenty minutes. Then the Builder runs a Ready Check and hands you the document.

"I don't have that yet" is a real answer

Every client defines all three levels here — including the ones they don't sell yet. A level you haven't built is named, shaped and marked Planned. A price you haven't settled is marked TBD — set in the Money Model. A number you've guessed is marked UNVERIFIED.

A ladder with a Planned rung is a plan. A ladder with a missing rung is a gap nobody can build on. The Builder never pushes you to invent a price, and a marked unknown passes the Ready Check.

The Three Levels, In Full

Three levels, three fixed forms. You name each one and set each price. The forms don't change, because every tool downstream reads this document knowing exactly what a level means.

Level 3Proximity

1-on-1, a retreat, or a mastermind. Direct access to you, and a room of people already doing it. Seats are capped, because your time is the thing being sold.

Usually $20,000 and up — some run to $100,000.

Level 2Acceleration

Done-for-you, or a 3-day accelerator. The same outcome, faster — because you do more of it for them, or they build it with you in three days.

Usually $10,000 to $15,000 and up.

Level 1Confidence

Your membership. The trainings, the system, the community and lower-level coaching — a weekly coaching call is the classic shape. Sold annual first, monthly for those who can't.

Usually around $5,000 a year, with the monthly worked back from it.

Level 1

Confidence — your membership

What it is. The foundation of your training and your coaching. Your trainings, your system, your community, and a lower level of coaching — most often a weekly group call. It's where a client gets confident that the system works, and confident in working with you.

Why it matters. It's the floor of the whole business. It's what fills the room at your events, it's what a new client can say yes to, and it's what the upper levels fall back into when their term ends. Without it, every other sale ends when it ends.

How to shape yours

Keep it to training, community and group coaching. Enough for a result; light enough that it doesn't compete with the levels above it.

Price it annual first. The year is the price you lead with. The monthly exists for the people who can't pay for the year, and you always show both.

✓ Do this

A training library, a weekly group coaching call, a private community — $5,000 a year, or about $556 a month for those who need it.

✕ Not that

A library of recordings with no calls and no community. That's a course. Nobody falls back into a course.

The trap: putting everything in it. If the membership includes 1-on-1 access and done-for-you pieces, you've priced Level 3 work at Level 1 — and there's nothing left for anyone to climb to.

Level 2

Acceleration — faster

What it is. One of two forms. Done-for-you, where you or your team do the work for them. Or a 3-day accelerator — a bootcamp-style event where they build the thing with you in three days. Either way the value is speed: the same outcome, sooner.

Why it matters. It's the middle of the ladder — usually $10,000 to $15,000 and up — and it's where a lot of your cash comes from. It also gives the membership somewhere to send people who want to move faster than a weekly call allows.

How to shape yours

Pick the form honestly. If you and your team deliver, it's done-for-you. If you'd rather get a room of people building together, it's the 3-day accelerator.

Give it a term — three months, three days plus ninety days of support, six months. And decide the fallback: when it ends, they continue in your membership.

✓ Do this

"A 3-day build, then ninety days of weekly check-ins — then they roll into the membership." A term, a fallback, a clear reason it's faster.

✕ Not that

"The membership, but with two calls a week." That's more of Level 1, not a faster route to the result.

The trap: an accelerator with no end. If it never ends, it's a membership with a bigger price — and there's no moment where the client falls back into the real one.

Level 3

Proximity — closer

What it is. One of three forms: 1-on-1, a retreat, or a mastermind. Direct access to you, and to a small group of people who are already getting the result. The top of the ladder.

Why it matters. It's where your best clients want to be, and it's priced like it — $20,000 and up, as much as $100,000. Because it's your time, it's capped, and the cap is part of what makes it worth the price.

How to shape yours

Pick the form, set the number of seats you can genuinely hold, give it a term, and decide the fallback — the membership, same as Level 2.

✓ Do this

"A mastermind, eight seats, twelve months, two in-person days a quarter and a monthly call with me — then the membership."

✕ Not that

"Unlimited 1-on-1, as many as sign up." Proximity means your time. Without a cap it isn't proximity, and it isn't sustainable.

The trap: fixed deliverables with no access. If the client gets a set of outputs and never gets near you, that's Level 2 wearing a Level 3 price tag.

The rule

The Fallback — what makes the ladder compound

The membership is the foundation, and both upper levels fall back onto it.

Your accelerator runs for its term — say three months. When it ends, the client's next step is the membership. Same for the mastermind or the 1-on-1 work when its year is up.

So a $10,000 sale doesn't end at $10,000. It turns into recurring revenue, and the business grows on top of itself instead of starting from zero every month.

The Builder records this as one line on each upper level: Then: continues in [your membership]. If you want a different fallback, you can have it — the Builder will flag it once and record yours.

The Annual-First Maths

Your membership has two prices, always shown together. The year is the one you lead with. The year carries a saving on twelve months of the monthly.

25% is the standard. You can choose your own saving — the Builder asks, records it, and uses it in the maths. What matters is that the year is clearly the better deal, so serious people pay up front.

Annual = Monthly × 12 × (1 − saving)
Monthly = Annual ÷ (12 × (1 − saving))

Start from whichever number you hold. If you know what the year should cost, the Builder works the monthly back. If you've only ever thought in monthly, give it that and it works the year forward.

You start withAt the standard 25%Both prices shown
$5,000 a year$5,000 ÷ (12 × 0.75)$5,000 a year, or about $556 a month
$6,000 a year$6,000 ÷ (12 × 0.75)$6,000 a year, or about $667 a month
$500 a month$500 × 12 × 0.75$4,500 a year, or $500 a month

Round either number to something clean if you like — the Builder will offer, and the decision is yours.

Why annual first

The year front-loads twelve months of cash and removes the month-two decision entirely. Lead with the monthly and you've built a subscription people cancel; lead with the year and you've built a high-ticket offer paid over time.

What You Sell Today — the map

Before the Builder teaches you the levels, it asks for everything already on your shelf. Then it places each thing.

A recurring offer with ongoing coaching and community sits at Level 1. A fixed-term offer where speed is the value — done-for-you, or a multi-day build — sits at Level 2. Direct-access work — 1-on-1, a retreat, a mastermind — sits at Level 3.

Anything that fits none of those — a low-ticket course, a book, a single workshop ticket — is recorded under Other — below the ladder. It's a front-end product, not a level. The Builder never invents a fourth rung to hold it.

Then the gaps show. Whatever level has nothing on it is the one you shape next and mark Planned. Most coaches find they already have one level without knowing it, and the other two are the plan.

Your Revenue Goal

Your Business Brand Profile keeps the picture of where you're headed. This document keeps the number.

Give it in whatever shape you hold it — monthly recurring, a yearly total, or a number of clients — and give it a date. A goal with a date is a plan; a goal without one is a wish.

If it's a number pulled from the air, say so. The Builder keeps it and marks it UNVERIFIED, and your Money Model will show you what it takes to get there.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

Almost every ladder that doesn't compound falls into one of these.

1. A course instead of a membership

What it looks like: Level 1 is a library of recordings. No calls, no community.

Why it happens: recordings are easy to make and feel like they scale.

Why it costs you: nobody falls back into a course. When the accelerator ends there's nothing with a person in it to continue into, and the ladder stops compounding.

The fix: add the coaching layer — a weekly group call is the classic shape — and the community. Training alone is a product; training plus coaching plus community is a membership.

2. Everything in the membership

What it looks like: 1-on-1 access, done-for-you pieces and unlimited calls, all inside the $5,000 membership.

Why it happens: generosity, and the worry that a lean membership won't sell.

Why it costs you: you've priced Level 3 work at Level 1, so there's nothing left to climb to — and you've filled your calendar at the cheapest price on the ladder.

The fix: keep Level 1 to training, community and group coaching. The closer access is what people climb for, and it's priced accordingly.

3. Leading with the monthly

What it looks like: "$497 a month" on the page, with the yearly nowhere or buried.

Why it happens: the monthly looks easier to say yes to.

Why it costs you: you've built a subscription people cancel in month two, instead of a high-ticket offer paid over time. The cash that should land up front arrives in dribs.

The fix: annual first, every time, with the saving clear. Show the monthly beside it for the people who genuinely can't pay for the year.

4. An accelerator that never ends

What it looks like: "Ongoing done-for-you support" at $12,000, with no term.

Why it happens: ending something feels like losing the client.

Why it costs you: with no end there's no fallback, so the $12,000 is the whole relationship — and you're delivering Level 2 work forever at a one-off price.

The fix: give it a term — three months, six, a year — and make the membership what continues. The sale becomes the start of recurring revenue, not the end of it.

5. A fourth rung for the $47 product

What it looks like: "Level 0: the ebook" or "Level 1.5: the mini-course".

Why it happens: you've got it, so it feels like it should be on the ladder.

Why it costs you: every tool downstream reads three levels. A fourth confuses the Enrollment Doc, the funnel and the presentation, and makes the ladder look like a shop.

The fix: record it under Other — below the ladder. It can still exist. It just isn't a level.

6. Pricing from fear

What it looks like: a membership at $1,200 a year, an accelerator at $3,000, because "my people can't afford more."

Why it happens: the price feels like a judgement on you, so you lower it before anyone can object.

Why it costs you: the maths won't clear in the Money Model, and you'll be delivering high-ticket work on low-ticket revenue.

The fix: the Builder tells you the usual band and flags a number well under it, once. You can keep your number — but hear the flag, and let the Money Model show you what it does to the maths before you commit.

7. Deciding your phase here

What it looks like: "I'm in Growth, so my core offer is the membership" — before the Magic Formula™ or the Money Model exist.

Why it happens: the phases are on the roadmap, and it's tempting to place yourself early.

Why it costs you: the phase is decided properly in the Money Model, from where you actually are. Guessing it here puts a guess at the root of every price decision after it.

The fix: lay out the three levels and their price points here. Let the Money Model pick the phase and the core offer when it has the Magic Formula™ to work with.
