What Is a Program Module?

A Program Module is a one-page tool your members fill in and walk away holding — built for one step of your Magic Formula™. Nine steps, nine modules. That's the backbone of your program.

You already know the shape. Your Event Magnet™ is one step of your Magic Formula™, given away free: a one-page tool, incomplete on purpose, that ends on a paid next step.

A Program Module is the same kind of tool. But it's built for every step, for the people who've already paid.

It teaches the step in full. It produces the step's outcome. It hands the member to the next module. And its promise still sells.

One step × one page × one session = one thing they hold

This builder writes the Module Specs. A spec is everything that will be on the page — the panels, the rows, the result, the done-when list — plus the lesson that goes with it for your call or your video.

It works with you one module at a time. You confirm each one before the next.

9
modules, in order — one per step, each named for its step. Every one fills in within 20 to 60 minutes and ends with something the member can hold up.

What a Program Module is NOT

It's not a lesson summary. A page they read is a lesson. A page they fill in is a tool.

It's not your slide deck. The slides teach. The module is what the teaching produces.

It's not an offer page. There's no price, no booking link and no second button on a module. The selling happens around it, not on it.

And it's not the artwork. This step writes the content. The finished pages in your brand are step 2.

The Three Problems This Solves

Most programs don't fail because the coach can't teach.

They fail because the member finishes a lesson with nothing in their hands. Here's what that looks like.

1. The pile of videos

Your program is forty recordings in a members' area. Everyone starts. Almost nobody finishes.

Watching isn't doing. A module turns each step into a page they fill in — and a filled-in page is a step done.

2. Every lesson teaches the whole system

Module 1 tries to explain all nine steps. The member leaves inspired and stuck.

A module teaches one step, produces that step's outcome, and names the next one. Nothing more. That's how people finish.

3. The free tool is better than the paid one

Your Event Magnet™ is a sharp one-page tool. Then someone pays you — and gets slides.

The paid version should be the same kind of tool, nine times over, complete. Your modules are the proof that paying was the right call.

▲ The real truth — your members didn't pay for more to read

They paid to walk out with the thing done.

A module is a promise kept on a single page: do this, in this session, and you'll be holding this when you're finished.

Anatomy of a Module Spec

Every spec has two parts. Part A is the page. Part B is the teach.

The headings are a contract — step 2 reads them to build the artwork — so they're never renamed, reordered or dropped. An empty one says [none].

The Promise — Five Options, You Pick One

Every module gets its own promise. It's your $Million Promise™, scaled down to what this one step delivers.

[specific result] in [time] — without [obstacle]

It's the subtitle on the page, and it's the line that sells the module before anyone fills it in. Never an instruction ("Use this worksheet to…"). Always a result.

Why five

The Builder writes five that lead with different things, so the choice is real. It marks the strongest ★. You pick one by letter, edit one, or take the ★.

Leads withExample shape (fictional)
AThe result the step produces"Turn what you know into one priced offer in 45 minutes — without guessing your numbers."
BThe first win they'll feel fast"Book your first sales calls this week — without cold-pitching strangers."
CThe obstacle it removes"…in 40 minutes — without pushy closing."
DThe dream outcome they walk away with"Know your first clients by name in 40 minutes — without building an audience first."
EWho they become"Step into the expert you already are in 30 minutes — without another course."

The four you don't pick aren't wasted. They stay in the spec as ready-made email, ad and post hooks.

⚠ The obstacle is theirs, not invented

The "without" comes from your own documents — the fear your members actually name in your SCORE™ Card, your Golden Avatar™, your $Million Promise™.

No two modules share a promise. And a module never promises money the step can't produce on its own.

The Teach — Five Acts, No Pitch

Part B is a lesson. And a lesson holds attention the same way a sales message does: people notice, they trust, they believe, they weigh it up, they act.

So the teach runs the five acts of your SCORE™ Framework, in order — in education mode. The how is taught in full, and the last act is an action, never a pitch.

ActThe member thinks…Where it lives
1 · Serendipity"This lesson is about my problem, right now"B1 the three struggles + the first line of B2
2 · Connection"This person has been where I'm stuck"B2 one line from your story — only if it fits this step
3 · Opportunity"So that's how it's done"B3 three teaching sections, the how in full
4 · Reality"I can do this — and here's what it costs not to"B5 the belief to break, with real proof
5 · Execution"The next step is obvious"B6 the one action · B4 done when

The test for every line

Could another coach in your space have written it? If yes, it needs your scene, your words or your numbers.

The Builder scores every act on exactly that question before it shows you the spec. Anything generic gets rewritten first.

▲ The bridge line is the only sell — and it comes after the action

No price, scarcity or offer anywhere inside the lesson. The member does the one action first.

Then one line names your next-level offer, exactly as your documents name it. That's the whole sell. It works because it comes last.

Done When — The Finish Line You Can See

Every module ends with three or four tick items. They're how a member — or you, on a call — knows the step is actually finished.

The rule: observable. Something you could check without asking how they feel.

✓ Observable

"5 conversations held this week"

✕ A feeling

"Feel clearer about my outreach"

Done when is also where the real-world actions live. The page plans and records them — the money collected, the posts published, the calls held. The ticks confirm they happened.

That's what keeps a page to one session. The work on the page takes 20 to 60 minutes. The work in the world takes as long as it takes — and gets ticked off when it's done.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

Almost every program that doesn't get finished fails in one of these ways.

1. Writing the modules before the Magic Formula™ is locked

What it looks like: Nine modules on nine step names you're still changing.

Why it happens: The program feels urgent, and the Formula feels finished enough.

Why it costs you: Every renamed step is a rebuilt module — and a page in a member's hands with the old name on it.

The fix: Lock the Magic Formula™ first. The module's name is the step's name, and it never changes on its own.

2. A module that's really a lesson

What it looks like: A page of paragraphs with a few lines to write on at the bottom.

Why it happens: Teaching is comfortable. Designing something to fill in takes a different kind of thought.

Why it costs you: Nobody walks out of a lesson summary holding anything. Check 3 fails.

The fix: One teaching line per panel. The rest is rows to fill. The teaching lives in Part B, on the call.

3. Teaching the whole system inside Module 1

What it looks like: Module 1 explains all nine steps, and the member leaves inspired and stuck.

Why it happens: You're proud of the system, and you want them to see it all.

Why it costs you: Overview isn't progress. Module 1 should produce Step 1's outcome — nothing more.

The fix: One step per module. The whole system lives in A5, as a map, with "you are here" on it.

4. Putting the offer on the page

What it looks like: A price, a booking link, or a "book your call" button in the closing bar.

Why it happens: It feels like a missed chance not to sell while they're engaged.

Why it costs you: A member mid-task who meets a price stops being a member and starts being a prospect again.

The fix: One plain Next button on the page. The next-level offer is sold in the bridge line on your call and the email angle in B6b.

5. Done-when items you can't see

What it looks like: "Feel more confident about my pricing."

Why it happens: Outcomes feel like feelings, and feelings are easy to write.

Why it costs you: Neither of you can tell when the step is finished. The module never closes.

The fix: Something countable or checkable. "Three prices written on the card." "Five conversations held." Take them from the step's outcome.

6. Filling a gap instead of flagging it

What it looks like: A member quote you don't have. A rate you never measured. A feature your program doesn't offer.

Why it happens: A blank looks unfinished, and a plausible line looks done.

Why it costs you: It's a false claim in your own program, with your name on it.

The fix: [MISSING CONTENT — …], naming exactly what's missing. Supply it later; replace the flag; bump the version. A blank the member fills in beats a number you made up.

7. Starting the artwork before the content is confirmed

What it looks like: A beautiful page for Module 1 — and a row name you now want to change.

Why it happens: The pages are the exciting part.

Why it costs you: Every content change now has to be made twice — in the spec and on the page — or the two drift apart.

The fix: Confirm all nine specs here first. Then step 2 builds from them, and a content change always goes back into the spec.
