Step Promises — Your Secret Weapon

Your $Million Promise™ covers the whole transformation. That's perfect for Section 1.

But when you make a video about Step 4, the big promise is too big. You need a promise for just that step.

What it is

A step promise is the pitch for one step. What this step gets them, how much or how fast, and the one pitfall it saves them from.

The step's Core Currency + a Measurable Metric or Timeline + one Painful Pitfall it avoids

You'll recognize the parts. They're the same elements as your $Million Promise™ — just scoped down to one step.

There's no audience callout. By the time someone's watching content about Step 4, they already know it's for them.

Why it matters

Every title in a section is built on its step promise. A vague step promise means 21 vague titles.

A sharp one means every Roadmap, Stone and hook in that section points at one clear result.

How to find yours

Start with the step's outcome from your Magic Formula™. That's the currency.

Then ask: how will they know it worked — a number, a timeframe? And what's the one mistake this step stops them making? The Builder drafts all nine for you to sharpen.

✓ Specific

"Plan your whole week in 30 minutes on a Sunday — without a to-do list that's longer by Tuesday."

✕ Generic

"Get organized and become more productive."

(Illustrative only — a made-up productivity coach for overwhelmed founders.)

⚠ The trap: rewording your big promise nine times

If your step promise could sit on top of any step, it's your $Million Promise™ in disguise.

Each step promise is about what THIS step delivers. And no two steps should share the same pitfall.

Step Struggles — The Raw Material

A step struggle is the friction your avatar hits when they try one step on their own.

It's not a big-picture pitfall. It's the thing that happened to them last Tuesday.

Why it matters

Step struggles power your Connection Stones and a third of your hooks. They're how your content makes someone feel seen.

A struggle that's too vague makes them scroll past. A struggle that's exactly right makes them think how did they know?

How to find yours

Look in your Golden Avatar™ — their pains, their limiting beliefs, and the exact phrases they use. Then look at the problem your SCORE™ Card names for that phase.

Bring it down to one step. What do they try? What goes wrong? What do they say to themselves when it does? You need one to three per step.

✓ An experience

"They batch a month of posts on Sunday, then run out by Wednesday."

✕ A category

"They struggle with consistency."

▲ Use their words, not yours

Your avatar doesn't say "I lack a content strategy." They say "I never know what to post."

Write the struggle the way they'd say it to a friend. That's the language your titles will be written in.

Piece 3 · Slingshot Hooks™

1515 per section · 150 in all · 3 per SCORE™ act

A Slingshot Hook™ is an opening. It's the first 5 to 20 seconds of a video, an email subject line, or the first line of a post or an ad.

You don't write new content for a hook. You tape it onto a piece you already have, and the body stays exactly the same.

Each SCORE™ act gets three hooks, so every section has fifteen. Each hook has a template. The Builder fills it from your locked work — and only your locked work.

⚠ A hook never carries a number you didn't confirm

Plenty of hook formulas come with numbers baked in — "over 300 clients," "in just 14 days," "$5,000 for $2,500."

Ours don't. Every number in a hook comes from your $Million Promise™, your Magic Formula™ or your SCORE™ Card. If there's no real proof for a Proof Hook yet, it gets a flag — never a fake.

The Title Rules

Every one of the 210 titles has to be market-ready. Good enough to use as a video title, a subject line or a hook — without another edit.

The Builder checks every title against these seven rules before you see it.

1. Specific beats general — and specific means true. Use your real numbers, your step names, your avatar's phrases. No confirmed number? Use a concrete detail — a time, a moment, a phrase — never an invented stat.

✓ True and specific

"The Sunday-Night Email You Keep Not Sending"

✕ Vague

"How to Write Better Emails"

2. Open a gap. Hint at the answer. Don't hand it over in the title.

3. Their words, not yours. "Stop chasing clients in your DMs" beats "Optimize your client acquisition strategy" every time.

4. One idea per title. The act or the hook picks the angle. Never cram two.

5. Break the pattern. If any AI could have written it, rewrite it.

6. Short enough to text. Aim for 6 to 12 words. Feeds cut long titles off.

7. Match the act or the hook. A Myth Hook debunks. A Proof Hook proves. A title that forgets its job is just a headline.

⚠ What makes a title sound like AI

Starting with "How to" (fine once in a while — never three in a row). Words like unlock, master, elevate, leverage, supercharge, game-changer.

Vague benefits like "next level." Any number you can't back up. And the big one — the niche-swap test: if you could swap in a different audience and the title still works, it's too generic.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

Almost every map that doesn't get used fails in one of these ways.

1. Building the map before the SCORE™ Card is finished

What it looks like: A half-built SCORE™ Card, and a map full of titles that could belong to anyone.

Why it happens: Content feels urgent, and the SCORE™ Card feels like homework.

Why it costs you: The map has no message of its own. Feed it a thin source and you get 210 thin titles — and you stop using the map in a week.

The fix: Finish all 19 SCORE™ blocks first. Then map it.

2. Step promises that are just the big promise, reworded

What it looks like: Nine step promises that all say roughly the same thing.

Why it happens: The big promise is comfortable, and scoping it down to one step takes real thought.

Why it costs you: Nine sections collapse into one. Your content repeats itself, and your audience notices.

The fix: Start each step promise from that step's outcome in your Magic Formula™. Different outcome, different promise, different pitfall.

3. Struggles that are categories, not experiences

What it looks like: "They struggle with time management."

Why it happens: Categories feel safe. A specific struggle feels like it might not apply to everyone.

Why it costs you: Nobody stops scrolling for a category. They stop for the exact thing that happened to them.

The fix: Name the moment. What did they try, what went wrong, and what did they say to themselves? Pull it from your Golden Avatar™.

4. Putting fake numbers in titles

What it looks like: "How 300 Coaches Doubled Their Revenue in 14 Days" — when you've never counted.

Why it happens: Numbers make titles stronger, and a lot of hook formulas come with numbers already in them.

Why it costs you: It's a false claim with your name on it. One audience member who checks, and the trust is gone.

The fix: Use your real numbers — your promise's metric and timeline, your nine steps, your confirmed client proof. No real number? Use a concrete detail instead. It's just as specific, and it's true.

5. Trying to make all 210

What it looks like: You finish the map, feel the weight of 210 titles, and make nothing.

Why it happens: A big list looks like a big to-do list.
