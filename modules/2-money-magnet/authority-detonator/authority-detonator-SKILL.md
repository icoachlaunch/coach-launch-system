# The Authority Detonator™ Builder — V4

Coach Launch · The $100K Day Engine™ · Money Magnet™ · Step 5 · Cash Flow Engine™

This skill writes the scripts for the videos a client records for their Cash Flow Engine™ funnel —
everything after someone is in. The operator (Matthew or the Coach Launch team) runs it; the client records
what it writes. The content comes from the client's own folders; the shape comes from the SCORE™ Framework;
what gets sold is always the dream outcome.

**Sibling skill:** the Ticket Sales Detonator™ (`cl-ticket-sales-detonator`) writes the one video that
comes *before* the sale on a paid ticket page. This skill writes everything after it, and in a paid funnel
it reads that script first.

## 0 · Load the foundation first

1. **Load `cl-score-framework`** and read its reference once. The SCORE™ Framework is the *shape*: five
   acts, in order, one big idea, the proof map, one action, the scoring rubric.
2. **The SCORE™ Card is the source of truth for content.** The Framework decides where things go; the
   Card (and the client's other assets) decide what is said.
3. Read **`references/card-map.md`** — which Card block and which asset feeds which part of each script.

This skill's own workflow and delivery format win over the Framework skill's default delivery (no act
labels in the client's document). The Framework's rules win for the copy itself.

## 1 · Two branches, six jobs

**Which branch?** Ask one thing: **how did they get their seat?**
- **A free seat** (free registration) → the **free branch**. Nothing before these videos has built any
  authority, so they build it: the host's story, the tool tour, the full case.
- **A paid ticket** (any ticket page, any phase) → the **paid branch**. The Ticket Sales Detonator™ video
  has already told the story, shown the tool and made the case, and they've just paid. These videos don't
  repeat it: they're short, already-sold scripts.

The phase doesn't decide the branch — the entry does. If the Money Model and the funnel build disagree,
don't block: go by the page they actually land on, and note the mismatch once in the chat.

One video, one page, one job. Each run writes one job (the two confirmation videos are written as a pair).

### Free branch — a free seat

| Job | Page | Length | The one action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Show-Up Detonator** | Ask Campaign page (straight after a free registration, Phase 1) | **5–12 min** | Show up live. The tool is the bribe: it's handed over on the day. |
| **B · VIP Detonator** | VIP upsell page after a free registration | **5–12 min** | Upgrade to VIP (the button under the video). |

Sections: **`references/detonator-sections.md`**.

### Paid branch — after a paid ticket

| Job | Page | Length | The one action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D · VIP upsell** | VIP upsell page, straight after the ticket | **6–7.5 min** (950–1,150 words) | Add the VIP to the order. |
| **E · Downsell** | The page after a "no" to an upsell (the VIP downsell; a PASS or Upsell 2 downsell if the funnel has one) | **~1 min** (150–250 words) | Take the easier yes. |
| **F · PASS / Upsell 2** | The PASS upsell or Upsell 2 page | **6–7.5 min** (950–1,150 words) | Add it to the order. |

Sections: D and F → **`references/paid-upsell-sections.md`** · E → **`references/downsell.md`**.

### Both branches

| Job | Page | Length | The one action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C · Confirmation videos** | VIP-confirmation · seat-confirmed | **Buyer 1–2 min · No-upgrade 2–4 min (cap 4)** | Buyer: the next steps. No-upgrade: next steps, then one last chance at VIP. |

Sections: **`references/confirmation-videos.md`**.

**Length is set by the job.** A free-branch Detonator runs as long as its job needs: never under 5
minutes, never over 12 (about 750–1,800 words at 150 a minute). The paid-branch upsells run 950–1,150
words; the downsell about a minute. When in doubt, write shorter — the operator prefers tight, punchy copy.

Phase 1 has no VIP, so no VIP Detonator and no confirmation video: the Show-Up Detonator is the whole job.
A funnel that sends everyone to one thank-you page → write both confirmation scripts and tell the operator
the funnel needs a buyer / no-upgrade split.

## 2 · The rules that make it work

### Every job

- **Sell the dream outcome — the tool is only the vehicle.** The viewer isn't buying a worksheet, a
  workbook or a session. They're buying what it does for them: the client's $Million Promise™ (S-1) and the
  life in R-3 / R-4 — more clients saying yes, the business growing, the identity they want. Every promise
  line leads with the outcome; every tool piece, VIP component and next step is tied to what it gets them.
  Weak: "I'm going to show you what you'll be holding on the day." Strong: "We're going to show you how,
  with this one tool, you get more clients saying yes and grow your business faster." Name the gap between
  where they are and the outcome sharply, aimed at the situation, never insulting the viewer. If a line
  says what something *is* without saying what it *does for them*, rewrite it.
- **Trust the client's folders.** Everything in the client's folders is the client's own information and
  counts as verified — including lines an older asset tagged `[VERIFY]` or `UNVERIFIED`. Use it. Include
  every credential and result the assets carry; the client can cut, the script doesn't pre-cut. What the
  operator confirms in chat is true; don't ask them to re-confirm claim by claim. The one check that stays:
  a claim the **tool or offer physically can't deliver**, or a source that doesn't exist ("research shows"
  with no research). Flag it once, in one line, with a true alternative, then follow the operator's call.
- **Fill proof gaps with real research.** When the system has no client results yet, fill the proof slots
  with **real, sourced industry data**: run a web search for studies that back the core argument and say it
  out loud ("A study of nearly 1,000 [profession] found that…"). Give the sources in the chat. Never invent
  a study, a number or a year the source doesn't publish. "In our experience…" is fine for the host's own
  observation; "research shows…" needs a real source. Use `[PROOF NEEDED]` only when no credible data exists.
- **Voice: mix I, we, they.** A script that says "I" all the way through devalues the host. "I" is for the
  host's own story and credentials only. "We" is for the event, the team and the method ("we'll be showing
  you", "we fill it in together"). "They / them" is for the people the host helps and for describing the
  two groups. Beyond that, the script is in the client's Brand Voice (words, rhythm); without one, the way
  their Card and Golden Avatar™ read. Coach Launch names carry ™; the acts don't.
- **Evergreen: no event date, time, time zone or weekday.** Events run every week; a dated video goes
  stale. Say "on the day", "when we're live", "the day after". The script points to where the time lives:
  "the time's on this page and in your inbox." Event length only if it's fixed. Details inside the
  client's story and Card ("In 2019", "Tuesdays off") don't date the video; keep them.
- **Numbers are numerals.** $24.6 million, 27 people, 2.5 hours — read in one glance off a screen.
- **Nothing is handed over before the day.** Not the tool, not the workbook, nothing. Everything is
  released on the day; that's the reason to show up. The script says "this is what you'll get when you
  show up" and, on confirmation videos, "everything else gets released on the day". Never "it's in your
  inbox", "download it", "print it and bring it". The host's printed copy is for the camera.
- **Show, never teach.** The host holds the tool up and points at its pieces; the event does the how. No
  maths, no worked examples, no teaching headlines in the video.
- **One big idea, one action.** The video ends on one action, said out loud, pointing at the button
  ("right under this video"), ending on an instruction. Other moves (calendar, community) are *what
  happens next*, not extra asks.
- **Sells the day or the offer on this page — never the core offer.** No programme price, no close. The
  Card's E-5 is read so the script never pitches it. Never say "upsell" or "downsell" on camera.
- **Guarantees, price and scarcity.** Every VIP, PASS and Upsell 2 has a guarantee: the client's own if
  they have one, otherwise the Coach Launch default (10 times the value or a full refund — wording in
  `references/detonator-sections.md`, Section 6B) — say once in the chat that the default was used, so the
  client's checkout terms match it. The past price comes from the operator ("normally $X"); if there isn't
  one, there's no "normally" line. Deadlines, caps, countdowns and "only on this page" only if they're
  true; a placeholder deadline on a page → ask, and flag it to come off if it isn't real. **No early-bird
  pricing and no price-rise tiers, ever** (Matthew, 2026-11-08). Trial terms are stated plainly.
- **Recordings.** A free event never gives a recording away or promises one; recordings sit in the VIP.
  The Show-Up video sells *live* and doesn't mention a replay.
- **Spoken, not written.** Short sentences, one idea each, contractions. No bullets or headings inside the
  script. If a sentence needs a second read, rewrite it.
- **The document is client-facing only.** Title line, spoken sections (timing markers +
  `[STAGE DIRECTIONS]`), shot notes. No act labels, branch, placement, flags, score or Ready Check — those
  go in the chat.
- **One client's material stays with that client.** Never carry another client's wording, results or
  assets into a script — or into this skill.
- **Names.** The client's locked names exactly; when the operator names the tool or an offer differently
  from the assets, the operator's name wins. Only Coach Launch names that exist (`references/card-map.md`).

### The paid branch (D, E, F — and C after a paid ticket)

- **They're already sold — no full pitch.** They paid for the ticket a minute ago, after a 5–8 minute
  video that told the host's story, showed the tool and made the case. They don't need it again; they need
  the next problem, the offer and a reason to say yes now. No hook-and-story opening, no tool tour, no
  pains toured on the tool. The host's story comes back as **one line** at most. Proof is a line or two.
  Every extra minute is a buyer who closes the tab before the button.
- **Read the Ticket Sales Detonator™ script first — it's required.** Take from it: the big idea and its
  line (said back once, as a callback), the three struggles, the voice, and every proof piece it already
  used (so none is reused). Never contradict it, and never lift a line out of its story — a line that
  worked inside a scene doesn't work on its own. No ticket script → stop: "The paid branch reads the
  ticket video so it never repeats it. Let's build the Ticket Sales Detonator™ first." If the operator
  confirms the ticket page has no video, build from the SCORE™ Card and say so in the chat.
- **Never assume an earlier purchase.** F never assumes they bought the VIP. E only plays for someone who
  just said no to its upsell. Each script stands on its own.
- **Proof matches what's sold.** VIP proof is about people who took the VIP (or the host's own result with
  it); PASS proof is about the PASS. Never pass a ticket result or a coaching result off as an upsell
  result. If a result came with coaching, say so.
- **One proof map across the whole funnel.** No result is used twice across the ticket video, the upsells,
  the downsells and the confirmation videos — except one callback to the ticket video's client story.
- **The downsell rule — set by the price of the upsell it follows** (Matthew, 2026-11-09):
  - **Upsell $100 or more → the same offer, as a payment plan.** Nothing taken out, same access. Said
    exactly as "[N] payments of $[amount]". Work the maths first: never call it "the same price" or "the
    same $[price], split up" unless N × amount equals the upsell price exactly. A plan that totals more is
    fine — just never disguised.
  - **Upsell under $100 → a lower price, with items removed** (for example, the recordings only). Name
    exactly what's in and what's out. What's removed comes from the offer or the operator — never invented.
    Never sell the cut-down version as the full thing.

## 4 · The workflow

One step per turn, waiting for the operator — unless they ask for the whole draft at once (or no one is
there to approve); then run every step, note assumptions in the chat, and deliver.

**Step 1 · Read and lock the foundation.** Read every asset in full — in the paid branch, the ticket
script and any approved funnel scripts first. Echo back, briefly: the branch and why · phase and event ·
the tool (the name you'll use, its pieces, hero, map, live exercise) · the Card blocks you'll use · who's
watching and their likeliest objections · the voice · the offer on this page. Paid branch: the big idea's
line you'll call back and the proof already used. Name any proof gap now and say you'll fill it with
sourced research.

**Step 2 · Lock the job and the facts.** Ask only what the assets don't say — never the date or time:
- all jobs: the event name as on the page · the tool's name on the day (if it differs from the spec) · the
  host's name · the one thing to bring (the operator's choice stands — if it doesn't feed the live exercise,
  use it as the "why" anchor, e.g. their 12-month goal)
- A: what's on the Ask Campaign page under the video
- B and D: the past VIP price ("normally $X"), if there is one · the client's own guarantee, if any · does
  the offer genuinely close, cap or sit only on this page? · any trial (terms) · is "VIPs win" true in
  their experience? · the decline link
- E: which upsell it follows and its price · $100+: the number of payments and the amount · under $100:
  the downsell price and exactly what's removed
- F: PASS or Upsell 2 · its components, values, price, guarantee · is it genuinely only on this page? ·
  does it have a downsell?
- C: separate buyer / no-upgrade pages? what's on each (calendar, email, community, WhatsApp, +1)? does the
  VIP stay open on the no-upgrade page? Paid branch: which offers sit in the funnel, and where does a
  payment-plan VIP buyer land? (They bought the VIP → the VIP-confirmation page.)

Then set **the brief** in four lines and get it approved:
1. **Mode:** marketing. **Format:** full video script (A, B, D, F) or short video script (C, E).
2. **Temperature:** just registered (A, B: warm — weight on Opportunity and Reality) · just bought (D, E,
   F: hot — weight on Opportunity, Reality and Execution) · just finished the upsells (C: hot — weight on
   Reality and Execution).
3. **The big idea**, one sentence, built on the dream outcome (paid branch: the ticket script's, carried on).
4. **Target length**, and why.

**Paid-branch order:** D → the VIP downsell (E) → F → its downsell (E), if any → the confirmation pair
(C) last, because it reads everything before it. Each is approved before the next is written; an approved
script is never silently changed by a later one.

**Step 3 onward · Write it, section by section** from the job's reference file (see §1). For each section:
write it from the mapped blocks, read it aloud in your head, show it with its rough timing and one line on
what it drew from. **Give a choice of openings** — A and B: at least four, one per opening style
(question · debunk · symbol · mirror · promise · chance moment · real statistic), drawn from the Card's
S-blocks; D and F: two or three versions of the opening part; C and E: two or three opening lines. Each is
labelled with its style and one line on when it fits. No style is the default. Say which you'd pick and
why, and ask the operator which to use — **or whether to split-test** two or three. For a split test,
write each chosen opening as a complete first section that hands off to the same second section (see
`references/detonator-sections.md`, Section 1).

**When the operator rewrites a section:** adopt it verbatim — fix only spelling, grammar and punctuation.
Raise at most one or two claims the tool or offer can't support, each with a true alternative. If they say
"keep mine", keep it and log it as an operator call. Never re-expand a section they've tightened.

**Assemble** every approved section verbatim, in order, with `[start – end]` timing markers, then the shot
notes. Split long output after Section 3 rather than compressing.

**Check and score — in the chat only.** Run **`references/ready-check.md`**: runtime, Ready Check, SCORE™
score per act with the floor rule (any act at 2 or below doesn't ship). List operator calls separately —
they aren't failures to fix. Give the research sources used. If the funnel pages contradict the video
(download steps, "print it and bring it", an early-bird line, a fake countdown, a payment-plan VIP buyer
routed to the no-VIP page, a page note saying "no selling, 60–90 sec" where the video sells), list the page
edits for the Cash Flow Engine™ Funnel Builder.

**Deliver.** Follow the client's folder convention (`CONTEXT.md`, READMEs). Default:
`Client Engine/Money Magnet/cash-flow-engine/` (top level), as `authority-detonator-show-up.md` ·
`authority-detonator-vip.md` · `authority-detonator-vip-upsell.md` · `authority-detonator-vip-downsell.md` ·
`authority-detonator-pass.md` / `authority-detonator-upsell-2.md` (+ `-downsell`) ·
`confirmation-video-vip-buyer.md` · `confirmation-video-no-upgrade.md`. One canonical file per job — never
a `-v2`; split-test openings live inside that file. If the session is attached to the client's claude.ai
Project, save each script there too. Then the recording copy as a **Google Doc or Word (.docx)** — ask
which — with the same words: title, spoken sections, shot notes; nothing internal. Then stop.

**Keep it matched to the source.** When an asset changes — the ticket script included — re-run the
sections that draw on it. Never edit the script on its own.

## 5 · Boundaries

- Writes scripts and shot notes only — not the page copy (Cash Flow Engine™ Funnel Builder), the emails
  (Genie X Converter™), the event (Sniper Presentation™), the slides or the edit.
- Asked for the paid ticket page video → "That's the Ticket Sales Detonator™ (`cl-ticket-sales-detonator`)
  — it sells the ticket; this skill takes over once they've bought."
- Asked to teach the tool in the video → "That's the event's job. If the video teaches it, there's no
  reason to turn up."
- Asked to put the date in → "The date lives on the page and in the email, so the video never goes stale."
- Asked to send the tool early → "Everything's released on the day. That's why they come."
- Asked for a free-branch Detonator under 5 minutes → "Under 5 minutes there isn't enough context to build
  authority."
- Asked to retell the story in a paid-branch video → "They heard it a minute ago, on the ticket page. One
  line brings it back; the full story loses them before the button."
- Asked for an early-bird or a price that goes up → "We don't run early-bird pricing. Urgency only when
  it's real."
- Asked to pitch the core offer → "Not here. The offer is made at the event."
- Never invent a URL; in the script, point at where the button is.
