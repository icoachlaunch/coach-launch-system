# The Ticket Sales Detonator™ Builder — V1.1

Coach Launch · The $100K Day Engine™ · Money Magnet™ · Step 5 · Cash Flow Engine™

This skill writes the script for the video on a client's **paid ticket page** — the page where a stranger
decides whether to buy a seat at their live event for under $100. The operator (Matthew or the Coach
Launch team) runs it; the client records what it writes. The content comes from the client's own folders;
the shape comes from the SCORE™ Framework; what gets sold is the dream outcome, and the ticket is the
first step to it.

**Sibling skill:** the Authority Detonator™ (`cl-authority-detonator`) writes everything *after* someone
is in — for a free seat (its free branch) and after a paid ticket (its paid branch: the VIP upsell, the
downsell, PASS / Upsell 2 and the confirmation videos). The Ticket Sales Detonator™ writes the one video
that comes *before* the sale on a paid ticket page. Different viewer, different job, different structure.
**This script comes first:** the Detonator's paid branch reads it, so it can carry the big idea on without
retelling the story.

## 0 · Load the foundation first

1. **Load `cl-score-framework`** and read its reference once. The SCORE™ Framework is the *shape*: five
   acts, in order, one big idea, the proof map, one action, the scoring rubric.
2. **The SCORE™ Card is the source of truth for content.** The Framework decides where things go; the
   Card (and the client's other assets) decide what is said.
3. Read **`references/card-map.md`** — which Card block and which asset feeds which moment.

This skill's own workflow and delivery format win over the Framework skill's default delivery (no act
labels in the client's document). The Framework's rules win for the copy itself.

## 1 · The job

| | |
|---|---|
| **Page** | Any paid ticket page — the paid registration page of any phase, or any page selling a seat to a live event |
| **Ticket** | **Under $100.** The exact price the client sells at |
| **Viewer** | Cold or lukewarm — often straight from an ad. They don't know the host yet and haven't bought anything |
| **Length** | **5–8 minutes** — about 750–1,200 words at 150 a minute. Target ~1,050 |
| **The one action** | Buy the ticket — the button right under this video |
| **Format** | Script only. Talking head, slides, or both — the script is the script. Stage directions stay format-neutral |

**Out of scope — stop and say so:**
- **A free event** → "That's the Authority Detonator™ — a free seat needs a show-up video, not a sales video."
- **A ticket of $100 or more** → "The Ticket Sales Detonator™ is built for tickets under $100. A higher
  price needs a longer case than 8 minutes." Stop.
- **The VIP upsell, downsell, PASS / Upsell 2 or confirmation video** → `cl-authority-detonator`, its
  paid branch (it reads this script first).

## 2 · The structure — five acts, two loops, one big idea

Full shapes and templates: **`references/ticket-sections.md`**.

| # | Section | Act | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Hook** | Serendipity | ~10% | The opening line (a choice of styles) · the call-out · the promise · one result in two lines |
| 2 | **The Problem + My Story** | Connection | ~25% | Three "why" questions · **Loop A opens** (the one thing) · the host's story as a scene (**Loop B opens**) · the hardest number |
| 3 | **The Real Reason + The Day** | Opportunity | ~30% | One client's story as a scene · the twist · the real reason, the big idea said in full (**Loop A closes**) · the event and the tool revealed (**Loop B closes**) · what the tool does for them · a second proof |
| 4 | **The Picture** | Reality | ~15% | What staying put costs · "this won't work for me" answered · three "Imagine…" lines · who they become · the big idea landed |
| 5 | **The Ticket** | Execution | ~20% | The day in one breath · the anchor → the price → the honest reason → the guarantee · the strongest proof, last · two paths · one action · the big idea, last |

**The big idea and its line.** One big idea (from the SCORE™ Card — usually the S-4 debunk turned into the
truth), said as a line of 3–8 words. It is hinted in Section 1 (never the answer), said in full for the
first time when Loop A closes, then repeated word for word at the reveal, the recap and the close — 4 to 6
times in all. More sounds mechanical.

**The two loops.** Loop A ("the one thing most [who] never think about") opens in Section 2 and closes at
the real reason in Section 3. Loop B ("until I noticed…") opens inside the host's story and closes when the
event and the tool are revealed. Both close before the price. Never open a loop you don't close.

**Every section ends pulling into the next** — a line the viewer can't leave on.

## 3 · The rules that make it work

- **Sell the dream outcome — the ticket is only the door.** The viewer isn't buying 90 minutes on Zoom or
  a worksheet. They're buying what the day starts for them: the client's $Million Promise™ (S-1) and the
  life in R-3 / R-4. Every promise line leads with the outcome. If a line says what something *is* without
  saying what it *does for them*, rewrite it.
- **The stories are scenes.** The host's story (C-2) runs about 15% of the runtime: where they were →
  what they tried → the low point, felt → "until I noticed…" → the result. One client's story (O-3) is
  told the same way, shorter: who they were, a real day, what they'd tried, the moment they nearly quit,
  the one thing, life now. Details and feelings come only from the client's folders — sharpen the words,
  never add a detail. Thin story → ask one question in Step 2; never pad.
- **Show, never teach.** The tool is revealed and its pieces named by what they do for the viewer; the
  event does the how. No maths, no worked examples, no teaching headlines. The Magic Formula™ may be named
  once with its map line ("step 3 of 9"); never its steps.
- **Nothing is handed over before the day.** "This is what you'll get when you show up." Never "it's in
  your inbox", "download it", "print it". The tool is the reason to be there live.
- **The price is spoken — in this order, always:** the anchor (what the day is worth or what it replaces)
  → the exact ticket price → the honest reason it's priced that way → the guarantee. Use the client's
  exact price; never change it.
- **The anchor is never invented.** It comes from the client's material — the Money Model, what the client
  has charged for this before, what the day replaces (a course, a consultant, months of trial and error).
  Nothing there → ask the operator once (Step 2). No answer → use "what it replaces" in plain words, never a
  dollar value. No made-up "value".
- **The guarantee — always.** The client's own ticket guarantee, or the Coach Launch default:
  "Show up, do the work with us, and if you don't feel you got 10 times the value of your ticket, email us
  and we'll refund every cent." Say once in the chat that the default was used, so the client's checkout
  terms match it. The guarantee is about the money back, never a promised result.
- **Real urgency only. No early-bird pricing.** No "early-bird $X until … then $Y", no price-rise tiers,
  no countdown that isn't real (Matthew, 2026-11-08). A genuine seat cap (because the event is delivered
  live) is fine when the operator confirms it. Nothing real → no urgency.
- **Proof matches what they're buying.** A result that came from the client's coaching or done-for-you
  work is said as exactly that ("one of our coaching clients", "when we worked with Jess"). Never let a
  line imply the ticket alone produced it. Ask in Step 2 when it isn't clear.
- **One action. No VIP, no replay, no core offer.** The video sells the ticket and nothing else. Never
  mention the VIP (its own video plays right after the purchase), never promise a recording (recordings
  normally sit in the VIP — unless the operator confirms the ticket itself includes one), never pitch the
  core offer (the Card's E-5 is read, never used). Never say "upsell".
- **The story is told once per funnel — here.** The buyer watches this video, buys, and lands on the VIP
  page a minute later. This video owns the host's full story, the tool and the case. The Authority
  Detonator™'s paid branch reads this script and carries it on without retelling it — so write it to be
  read: one big idea and its line, the three struggles named, and every proof piece easy to spot (the
  paid branch won't reuse them).
- **Trust the client's folders.** Everything in them is the client's own information and counts as
  verified — including lines an older asset tagged `[VERIFY]` or `UNVERIFIED`. Include every credential
  and result; the client can cut. The one check that stays: a claim the event or tool **physically can't
  deliver**, or a source that doesn't exist. Flag it once, with a true alternative, then follow the
  operator's call.
- **Fill proof gaps with real research.** No client results yet → real, sourced industry data that backs
  the core argument ("A study of nearly 1,000 [profession] found that…"), sources in the chat. Never invent
  a study, a number or a year. `[PROOF NEEDED]` only when no credible data exists.
- **Regulated niches.** Health, weight, money, investing, legal, medical: results are what real people got
  ("Jess lost 4 kilos in 9 days"), never what the viewer *will* get. "Imagine…" lines stay imagine.
- **Voice: mix I, we, they.** "I" for the host's own story and credentials. "We" for the event, the team
  and the method. "They / them" for the people the host helps. Beyond that, the client's Brand Voice;
  without one, the way their Card and Golden Avatar™ read.
- **Evergreen: no event date, time, time zone or weekday.** Tickets sell every week; a dated video goes
  stale. "On the day", "when we're live". The date is on the page, under the video. Event length only if
  it's fixed. Details inside the client's story ("In 2019") don't date the video; keep them.
- **Numbers are numerals.** $27, $24.6 million, 27 people, 90 minutes.
- **Spoken, not written — in the client's Brand Voice.** Contractions, no bullets or headings inside the
  script, and if a sentence needs a second read, rewrite it. **Sentence length, rhythm and signature moves
  come from the Brand Voice, not from this skill:** if it says full 15–30-word sentences, never clipped
  (Coach Launch's does), write full sentences. Use its signature moves where they fit (e.g. "I get it…" before
  the twist, one earthy picture per section, threes, one CAPS word per beat, its peak phrase) and never its
  banned words. (First live run, 2026-11-09: a draft drifted generic until a voice pass.)
- **Length follows the voice.** A full-sentence voice runs longer; if the script lands over 8 minutes on
  paper, say so with the count and let the operator choose to trim or keep — a speaker's own pace often
  brings it inside.
- **The document is client-facing only.** Title line, spoken sections with timing markers and
  `[STAGE DIRECTIONS]`, short recording notes. No act labels, loops, flags, score or Ready Check — those go
  in the chat.
- **One client's material stays with that client.** Never carry another client's wording, results or
  assets into a script — or into this skill.
- **Names.** The client's locked names exactly; when the operator names the tool or event differently from
  the assets, the operator wins. Only Coach Launch names that exist (`references/card-map.md`).

## 5 · The workflow

One step per turn, waiting for the operator — unless they ask for the whole draft at once (or no one is
there to approve); then run every step, note assumptions in the chat, and deliver.

**Step 1 · Read and lock the foundation.** Read every asset in full. Echo back, briefly: the event and its
format · the ticket price · the tool (the name you'll use, its pieces, hero, map) · the host's story in
two lines and the client story you'll use · the Card blocks you'll use · who's watching and the belief
that stops them · the voice. Name any proof gap now and say you'll fill it with sourced research.

**Step 2 · Lock the facts.** Ask only what the assets don't say — never the date or time:
- the event name as on the page · the tool's name on the day (if it differs from the spec) · the host's name
- the exact ticket price (under $100 — else stop) and **the honest reason it's priced that way**
- **the anchor** — what the day is worth or replaces, if the assets don't say
- **the guarantee** — the client's own, or the Coach Launch default
- anything genuinely limited (a seat cap because it's live)? If not, no urgency
- does the ticket include a recording? (default: no — don't mention one)
- for each result you'll use: did it come from the event/tool itself, or from working with the client?

Then set **the brief** in five lines and get it approved:
1. **Mode:** marketing. **Format:** full video script.
2. **Temperature:** cold — weight on Serendipity and Connection; they need to trust the host before the price.
3. **The big idea**, one sentence, built on the dream outcome, and **its line** (3–8 words).
4. **The two stories:** the host's turning point and the one client story, one line each.
5. **Target length**, and why.

**Step 3 onward · Write it, section by section** from `references/ticket-sections.md`. For each section:
write it from the mapped blocks, read it aloud in your head, show it with its rough timing and one line on
what it drew from. **Give a choice of openings** — at least four, one per style (question · debunk ·
symbol · mirror · promise · chance moment · real statistic), drawn from the Card's S-blocks and the Golden
Avatar™, each labelled with its style and one line on when it fits. Say which you'd pick for cold traffic
and why, and ask the operator which to use — **or whether to split-test** two or three (each written as a
complete Section 1 that hands off to the same Section 2).

**When the operator rewrites a section:** adopt it verbatim — fix only spelling, grammar and punctuation.
Raise at most one or two claims the event or tool can't support, each with a true alternative. If they say
"keep mine", keep it and log it as an operator call. Never re-expand a section they've tightened.

**Assemble** every approved section verbatim, in order, with `[start – end]` timing markers, then the
recording notes. Split long output after Section 3 rather than compressing.

**Check and score — in the chat only.** Run **`references/ready-check.md`**: runtime, the loop and
big-idea check, the Ready Check, the SCORE™ score per act with the floor rule (any act at 2 or below
doesn't ship). List operator calls separately. Give the research sources used. If the ticket page
contradicts the video (an early-bird line, a fake countdown, a different price, a download step, a
promised replay), list the page edits for the Cash Flow Engine™ Funnel Builder. If the funnel's upsell
scripts were written before this one, say they need a re-run so they carry this script on.

**Deliver.** Follow the client's folder convention (`CONTEXT.md`, READMEs). Default:
`Client Engine/Money Magnet/cash-flow-engine/ticket-sales-detonator.md` (top level). One canonical file —
never a `-v2`; split-test openings live inside it. If the session is attached to the client's claude.ai
Project, save it there too. Then the recording copy as a **Google Doc or Word (.docx)** — ask which — with
the same words: title, spoken sections, recording notes; nothing internal. Then stop.

**Keep it matched to the source.** When an asset or the ticket price changes, re-run the sections that
draw on it. Never edit the script on its own.

## 6 · Boundaries

- Writes the script and recording notes only — not the page copy (Cash Flow Engine™ Funnel Builder), the
  emails (Genie X Converter™), the ads (Dragon Fire Ads™), the event (Sniper Presentation™), slides or the edit.
- Asked to teach the tool in the video → "That's the event's job. If the video teaches it, there's no
  reason to buy the ticket."
- Asked to put the date in → "The date lives on the page, so the video never goes stale."
- Asked for an early-bird or a price that goes up → "We don't run early-bird pricing. Urgency only when
  it's real — a seat cap because it's live, for example."
- Asked to mention the VIP → "The VIP gets its own video, straight after they buy. This one has one job."
- Asked for under 5 minutes → "A cold viewer needs the story and the reason before the price. Under 5
  minutes, there isn't room for both."
- Asked to pitch the core offer → "Not here. The offer is made at the event."
- Never invent a URL; in the script, point at where the button is.
