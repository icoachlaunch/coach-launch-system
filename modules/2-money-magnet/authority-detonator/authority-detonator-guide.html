What Is the Authority Detonator™?

Someone sees your Event Magnet™. They want it. So they register for your event to get it.

They land on the next page. Their seat's saved. The tool they registered for is waiting for them on the day. And right now, they have no reason at all to stay on that page.

Except your video starts playing.

That's your Authority Detonator™. A five-to-twelve-minute video where they meet you, hear the real problem said out loud, see that you've been there, and watch you hold up the very tool they'll get when they show up — pointing at exactly what it's going to break through for them on the day. Then why they need to be in the room for it, live.

Your Event Magnet™ is the fuse. This video lights it.

Here's the simple truth it fixes. A registration is a name on a list. Most people who register for a free event never turn up — the week gets busy, the email gets buried, the moment passes.

This video is the difference between a name on a list and a person in a seat.

What it's NOT

It's not a sales pitch. The offer gets made at the event — that's your Sniper Presentation™ and The Sniper Close™.

It's not the event. You don't teach the tool here. You don't reveal your nine steps here. That happens live, in the room.

It's not a thank-you clip. A minute of "thanks for registering, see you there" doesn't build authority and doesn't get anyone in the room.

And it's not dated. No day, no time. Your events run again and again — the page and the email carry the time, so the video never goes stale.

And it never hands the tool over early. The Event Magnet™ is given on the day, in the room. The video says so: "this is what you'll get when you show up." That's the show-up lever.

▲ Why it lives in the Cash Flow Engine™

Because it's a video on a Cash Flow Engine™ page. The page and the video get built in the same step, so they always say the same thing.

It's also the first piece of content most people ever see from you that isn't an ad or a post — and it's the one that decides whether they show up.

Where It Lives — The Two Pages

Your Cash Flow Engine™ funnel has two places this video can play. Each one gives the video a different job.

The Thank-You Page

The confirmation page right after they register. In Launch it's the page with the survey. In Execute it's the seat-confirmed page. In Growth and Mastery it's the thank-you page after the ticket.

The job: show up live.

The ask: three moves — the confirmation email and the calendar, one thing written down and brought, the page's own step.

Length: 5–8 minutes.

The VIP Upsell Page

The page between registration and confirmation that carries your VIP upgrade. Exists from Execute on — there's no VIP in Launch.

The job: upgrade to VIP.

The ask: the full VIP case, straight from your Cash Flow Max™ Blueprint — the two kinds of people in the room, what's in it, the honest terms, the price flip, a clean way to say no.

Length: 8–12 minutes.

Every one of those pages already has a video slot above the fold. The date and the time sit on the page under the video — the video never says them.

How Long Should It Be?

Five to twelve minutes. Where it plays sets the band.

Why not sixty seconds? Because a minute says "thanks, see you there." It doesn't build authority, and it doesn't give anyone a reason to clear their afternoon.

Authority takes minutes. They need to hear the real problem, see your proof, and see the tool in your hand. That's five minutes at the floor.

Why does the VIP video run longer? Because it has a case to make. It names the two kinds of people in the room, what's inside, the honest terms, what it costs, and gives them a clean way to say no. A real case takes longer than a welcome — and it's worth every second.

✕ Too short

"Hey, thanks so much for registering! I can't wait to see you Thursday. It's going to be amazing. See you there!" — nice, forgettable, dated by next week, and nobody's calendar changed.

✓ The right length

They heard the real problem named. They know you've been there. They saw the tool in your hand and the piece that breaks each of their exact problems. The calendar button got pressed.

How You'll Build It

The Builder does the heavy lifting. You make the calls. It runs in three phases.

Phase 1 · Lock the foundation and pick the page

The Builder reads your locked assets and shows you exactly what it's working with — your event, your tool and its pieces, your three problems, your proof, your voice.

Then you pick the page: Thank-You or VIP. That sets the job, the ask and the length. Then it collects the facts the script needs — the event name, the platform, what's actually under the video on your page, what they should bring. Never the date.

Phase 2 · Six sections, one at a time

Hook + Welcome. The Problem + Who I Am. Meet the Tool. The Three Problems, On the Tool. Why Live (or How We Make This Even Better). Action.

Each one comes with the teaching behind it and a draft written from your assets. You read it aloud, you say what's off, it gets fixed. Then you say "next."

Phase 3 · Assemble, check, keep

The Builder runs a runtime check against your band and a ten-point Ready Check — in the chat. Then your document is just the script, with its timing markers and stage directions, and the shot notes. Nothing internal goes in it.

Then it saves the master copy as a file — authority-detonator-thank-you.md or authority-detonator-vip.md — and hands you the script as a Google Doc or a Word document to record from.

The content already exists. The Builder reshapes it for one person talking to one person, on camera — with the tool in their hand.

The Six Sections

Every Authority Detonator™ follows the same spine. Problem, you, the tool, the three problems on the tool, why live, action. What changes between the two placements is what Sections 5 and 6 say.

For each section you'll see what it does, how long it runs, where it draws from, and what it sounds like. Three rules run through all six: no dates, numbers as numerals, and nothing that needs a second read.

1

Hook + Welcome

30–60 sec

The most important ten seconds you'll record. Someone just registered. Nothing is holding them on this page. Your first line has to make them stay.

The way you do that is by calling them out. Not "hey everyone." Who they ARE, in the words they'd use. When someone hears their own situation in the first line, they lean in. "That's me."

Then you acknowledge what just happened ("you're in, your confirmation email's on its way"), you stop them closing the page, and you say your $Million Promise™ once, out loud.

Draws from — your Golden Avatar™ (who), your SCORE™ Card's S-1 (the promise), your event name. Not the date.

Thank-You Page — the shape

"If you're a [avatar] who [their situation] — you're in. Your seat at [event] is saved, and your confirmation email's already on its way. Give it 5 to 10 minutes. And your [tool]? That's what you get when you show up.

But stop — before you close this page. Stick with me for the next few minutes, because I'm going to show you exactly what's going to happen on the day, what you're going to walk out holding, and the one thing I need you to do before you leave this page.

Because here's what [event] is really about. [Your $Million Promise™, spoken.] And on the day, live, I'm going to show you how."

VIP Upsell Page — the shape

"[Avatar callout] — you're in. Your seat at [event] is confirmed, and your confirmation email's on its way. But stop — before you close this page. Stick with me, because I'm going to show you something that makes this [event] 10X more valuable — and separates the people who watch from the sidelines from the people who actually get the result.

One question first. [Your VIP's promise, as a question.] Give me 10 minutes and I'll show you what that is, who it's for, and why I built it. If it's not for you, no hard feelings — there's a link under this video that takes you straight to your confirmation page."

What kills it: "Hey guys, welcome!" (nobody feels called out) · "Thanks so much for registering!" (grateful, not compelling) · "In this video I'm going to talk about…" (passive) · no avatar at all · a date.

2

The Problem + Who I Am

60–90 sec

The main problem, then you. In that order, and for a reason.

If you open with your credentials, they think "good for you." If you open with the problem they're living — said better than they'd say it — and then name the real cause underneath it, they think "this person gets it." NOW your story means something.

The shape: the main problem as they live it (a scene, not a category) → the root-cause line ("you don't have a marketing problem, you have a referral problem") → your $Million Moment™ in two spoken lines → your credibility in numbers you can stand behind → one real client result → "so I built something to fix it." That last line is the door into the tool.

Numbers stay numbers. $24.6 million. $1,002 in ads. 27 people. The script is read off a screen next to the lens — a numeral lands in one glance, a spelled-out number trips the tongue.

Draws from — your SCORE™ Card's C-1 framing (the root cause), C-2, C-3 and O-3. Nothing else. No result that isn't yours.

Both placements — the shape

"Here's the thing. [The main problem, as a moment they recognise.] [The root-cause line: you don't have a [surface] problem, you have a [real] problem.]

I know, because [your $Million Moment™ in two spoken lines]. [Your credibility, as numerals — only what's in C-3.] [One real result: who, where they started, how long, what happened.]

So I built something to fix it."

✕ Too much

A five-minute story about your journey, your failures, your breakthrough, every credential you've ever earned, and three testimonials. This isn't a keynote. They came for the tool.

✓ Just enough

The problem named, the cause named, the moment it changed for you, one number you can prove, one result, and the door: "so I built something to fix it."

No client result yet? Your own turnaround is the proof. The Builder flags the spot with [PROOF NEEDED] so you add one real result before you record — and it never makes one up.

3

Meet the Tool

30–45 sec

Thirty seconds, and it's the pivot of the whole video.

You've named the problem. You've shown you've been there. Now you hold up the thing you built to fix it. Printed. In your hand.

The words are already written: your Event Magnet™'s title is a promise — "this is how we [result], in [time], without [obstacle]." You say it, you say what the page is in one line, and you say the one line this whole video exists for: "this is what you'll get when you show up." Not "it's in your inbox." Not "print it and bring it." They get it in the room — that's why they come.

You don't explain it yet. You don't point at anything yet. You show it, you promise it, you stop.

Draws from — your Event Magnet™ Spec's title (the promise), its one-liner, and nothing else.

Both placements — the shape

[HOLD UP THE PRINTED TOOL]

"Let me show you the [tool]. This is how we [result] — in [time] — without [obstacle]. [One line on what the page is: one page, you [what they do on it], and it [what it produces].]

And this is what you'll get when you show up."

4

The Three Problems, On the Tool — Show, Don't Tell

120–180 sec

The heart of the video. And the part that's different from every welcome video you've ever seen.

You've got the tool in your hand. Now you walk them across it, one problem at a time. Three problems — one for each phase of your system, or the three struggles printed on the face of your tool. Each one goes like this: "You know how [the problem, as a moment they recognise]?" Then you point. "Here's where we address that." Then the curiosity line. Then the next one.

Show, don't tell. Point at the piece. Name the problem it breaks. Say the day will show how. Never the how.

Here's why. People believe what they see. A page in a hand is proof the tool exists, proof the day is real, and the single biggest curiosity lever in the whole video. You're not describing the event. You're showing them the thing they'll be filling in — and pointing at exactly what each piece breaks through.

How to present the tool on camera

  1. Hold it. The printed tool, in hand, in frame, at chest height. Not a slide. Not a screen share. A thing. Your copy — the registrant gets theirs on the day.
  2. Open each problem as a moment. "You know how the treatment's going well, you think about asking, and the moment passes?" A scene, not a category. If there's no moment you can picture, rewrite it.
  3. Point. One finger, one piece. "Here's where we address that." Name the piece by where it sits — "this piece right here," "you see this section?" — never by what's inside it.
  4. The curiosity line. Once per problem: "In the masterclass we'll be teaching [headline] — and on this piece I'll be showing you how to [overcome it]." The event shows how. The video never does.
  5. Three times. "And then there's this one…" "And you know how…?" Same shape, next piece.
  6. The hero. The result panel — "and this box right here is the number you walk out with." Turn the page so the camera sees the box. Don't explain the maths.
  7. The map. "It's step [X] of 9. On the day, I walk you through the whole map." Not one step more.
  8. The hinge. "In the masterclass I'll be giving you this tool to work through with me. Live. We're not going to talk ABOUT it — we fill in YOUR version. How cool is that?" The most important sentence in the video: the show-up promise and the demo rule in one line.
  9. The open question (thank-you page). The one thing the tool deliberately doesn't solve. That question is the reason to be in the room. On the VIP page you end on the hinge instead — the next section opens with "how we make this even better."

Draws from — your SCORE™ Card's C-1 (or the struggles on the tool's face), your Event Magnet™ Spec's Part A (the pieces, the hero, the map) and Part B (the headlines, the live exercise, the walk-away, the open question).

Both placements — the shape

"So — you know how [problem 1, as a moment]? [POINT AT THE PIECE] Here's where we address that. In the masterclass we'll be teaching [headline] — and on this piece I'll be showing you how to [overcome it].

And then there's this one. You know how [problem 2]? [POINT] Here's where that goes away. We'll be teaching [headline] — and on this piece I'll be showing you how to [overcome it].

And you know how [problem 3]? [POINT] You see this section? We cover that here. [What the day does with it.]

[TURN THE PAGE TO CAMERA] And this box, right here — [the hero]. That's [the answer it produces]. That's what you walk out of the day holding.

[POINT AT THE MAP] This one page is step [X] of 9 in [system]. On the day, I walk you through the whole map. And here's the exciting part. In the masterclass, I'll be giving you this tool to work through with me. Live. We're not going to talk ABOUT it — we fill in YOUR [tool]. How cool is that?

One thing it doesn't do, though. [The question the tool leaves open.] That's what the room is for."

What kills it: pointing at the tool before you've introduced it (that's why Section 3 comes first). Explaining a piece instead of pointing at it. Listing the nine steps. Describing the tool instead of holding it. If they've had the event, they don't need the event.

5

Why Live · or · How We Make This Even Better

45–210 sec

The bridge between excitement and action. This is where the two placements go their own way.

Thank-You Page — Why Live

Here's the honest situation. Your funnel has a replay page, and they'll find out. So "you have to be there" isn't enough — they'll decide to catch it later, and later never comes.

What works is the truth: the replay can't do the things that matter. It can't fill in your tool with you. It can't take your question. It can't hand you the bribe. The replay gives them the information. Being live gives them the result.

No invented urgency. If your SCORE™ Card's E-4 scarcity is real, you use it, plainly. If it's not, you don't claim a deadline the page doesn't enforce. Real reasons are stronger anyway.

Draws from — B3's do-it-now moment, your bribe (Launch), the +1 (VIPs), E-4 only if it's genuine.

Thank-You Page — the shape

"Now — I have to be straight with you about the day. I know there's a replay. Here's why I want you in the room.

On the day, we fill in [tool] together. I'll ask you to [the live action], right then, and I'll read what you put in the chat. You can't do that with a recording. [If there's a bribe: And if you're there live, you get [the bribe]. That's live only.]

The replay gives you the information. The room gives you [what they hold]. That's the difference."

VIP Upsell Page — How We Make This Even Better

This is where the case gets made, and it opens with one line and an intro most people skip. The line: "Now let me tell you how we make this even better." You've just said "how cool is that" — the VIP is the next thing up, not a change of subject.

Then the day after: when the event ends, general admission is on their own, and that's where most people stall. Then the truth about the day — it's a firehose, we move fast — and the two kinds of people in every room. The watchers: notes, nods, inspired… and next week they try to rebuild it from memory, on their own, with none of the tools. And the builders: the system open in front of them, following along, leaving with it live. The results between those two groups aren't close.

THEN the VIP intro: "So on this page there's a VIP option — and what I've found over the years is the people who go VIP are the ones who go faster. That's exactly why it exists." Now the stack has a reason.

The stack goes in three buckets, in this order. BUILD — the tools they build with. ACCESS — the time with you: the day-after session, the priority support. KEEP — the arsenal they keep: methods, recordings, templates. Every component from your Cash Flow Max™ Blueprint lands in one bucket, in your Blueprint's words, with its value. Spoken, not listed.

And the honest terms. If your VIP includes a trial of anything, the script says it plainly: free for how long, what it costs after, how to cancel, what they keep if they do. "I hate sneaky trials." Your audience does too.

Draws from — your Cash Flow Max™ Blueprint's components and value stack, sorted into the three buckets. The trial terms you confirm. Nothing else.

VIP Upsell Page — the shape

"Now let me tell you how we make this even better. When [event] ends and general admission logs off, everyone with a general seat is on their own… Because I have to be straight with you about the day. It's a firehose. We move fast, we build fast — and there are always two kinds of people in the room. Watchers on the sideline… And builders… The results between those two groups aren't close.

So on this page there's a VIP option. And what I've found over the years is the people who go VIP are the ones who go faster. That's exactly why the VIP exists. And today, it's [free / $X]. So let me walk you through exactly what you're getting.

First — the build. [The tools they build with.] [If there's a trial: And let me be 100% clear, because I hate sneaky trials: it's free for [N] days, then [price] a month. Cancel anytime — [how] — and you still keep [what they keep].]

Second — access. [The day-after session. The priority support.] Third — the arsenal you keep. [The methods, the recordings, the templates. If general admission gets no replay, say so.]"

6

Action

45–180 sec

The ask. Where most people go too soft or too hard.

Thank-You Page — three moves

On this page the ask is free, which is exactly why people fumble it. "See you there!" is not an ask.

The ask is three moves, said out loud, in order, with your face on them. One: find the confirmation email and add the event to your calendar — right now, before life gets in the way. Two: write down one thing and bring it on the day — your current price, your current headline, whatever the live exercise needs. The tool itself is waiting in the room, and you say so. Three: the page's own step — the community, the survey, the +1, whatever's actually under this video.

And then, because the video is evergreen, you don't say the date. You say where it is: "the time's on this page and in your inbox." Then you say you'll see them live.

Draws from — whatever's actually on your page. The Builder asks you to tick what's there, and names nothing else.

Thank-You Page — the shape

"So here's exactly what to do right now. Three things — they take a minute.

One: find your confirmation email — it's in your inbox in the next few minutes — and add [event] to your calendar. Right now, before life gets in the way. Two: write down [the one thing]. Bring it on the day. Your [tool] is waiting for you in the room — that's where we build YOUR version, live. Three: [the page's own step].

The time's on this page and in your inbox. Come live. Your [tool] is waiting. I'll see you in the room."

VIP Upsell Page — the full close

Too soft: "if you want, you can upgrade below." Too hard: a countdown-and-pressure close that makes someone who just registered feel like they've walked into a timeshare pitch. Neither. The close runs a full sequence, because the ask has to survive the viewer thinking about it.

  1. The price flip. Add it up — the stack total. What people paid for VIP at past events, if that's real. Today's price.
  2. The proof. "VIPs win" — they show up, they build in the room, they ask their questions first — only if it's true for you. The Builder asks.
  3. Two objections, swept in one line each. "Not techy? That's the entire point of the AI." "No time? This IS the time-saver — we build it during the event."
  4. The identity close. "Upgrading isn't really about the bonuses. It's about the version of you that shows up on the day." Then the honest line: "I can't guarantee your results. That part's always up to you."
  5. Three moves. One: the upgrade button under this video (and the cap, if you really cap it). Two: the confirmation email and the calendar. Three: bring one thing on the day.
  6. The clean no, then the question. "If VIP's not for you, that's completely fine — there's a link under the button that says no thanks." Then: "You're going to be in the room either way. The only question is… watcher, or builder? Upgrade now. I'll see you backstage."

Scarcity: only if it's real. If your page genuinely closes the offer when they leave, say so once, plainly. If you really cap VIP, say so. If not, don't claim it. A fake deadline costs you more trust than it buys.

Draws from — your Cash Flow Max™ Blueprint's stack total, anchor, price and guarantee. Your Golden Avatar™'s objections. The page's own decline link. The claims you confirm.

  • It's evergreen. No dates, no times, no weekdays. Your events run again and again. A date in the video means re-recording it every time — so it never gets re-recorded. The page and the email carry the time; the script points there.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

This is a short video with a simple structure. But the same handful of mistakes shows up constantly. Here's what to watch for.

1. Putting the date in the video

What it looks like: "I'll see you Thursday at 10." Recorded once. Played for six months.

Why it happens: It feels specific and warm in the moment.

Why it costs you: The event runs every week. The video is wrong by the second week, and nobody re-records it — so it stays wrong, and the viewer notices.

The fix: "The time's on this page and in your inbox." The page changes the date. The video never says it.

2. Pointing at the tool before you've introduced it

What it looks like: "You know how the ask feels awkward? [points at a page the viewer has never seen] Here's where we fix that."

Why it happens: You know the page so well you forget they don't.

Why it costs you: A finger on an unexplained page is noise. The curiosity lever only works once they know what the thing is and what it promises.

The fix: Section 3 first. Hold it up, name it, speak its title as the promise, "this is what you'll get when you show up." Then point.

3. Teaching the tool in the video

What it looks like: You walk them through the Scorecard, box by box, "so they get the most out of it."

Why it happens: It feels generous. And you know the tool so well it's hard not to.

Why it costs you: They've now had the event. Why would they turn up for it?

The fix: Point at the piece, name the problem, say "on this piece I'll be showing you how to…" — and stop. The event shows how.

4. Skipping the problems

What it looks like: You go straight from "you're in" to the tool and the hero box. It feels upbeat.

Why it happens: The problems feel negative — like you're bringing people down right after they registered.

Why it costs you: The tour has nothing to point at. "Here's where we address that" — address what?

The fix: The problems aren't negative. They're validating. When someone hears their exact Sunday night described back to them, they feel seen. The main one in Section 2, three on the tool in Section 4, every time.

5. The five-minute biography

What it looks like: Your journey, your failures, your breakthrough, every credential, three testimonials.

Why it happens: You think authority comes from the résumé.

Why it costs you: They came for the tool, not a keynote. They leave before you hold it up.

The fix: Two lines on the moment it changed, one number you can prove, one result, and the door: "so I built something to fix it." Authority comes from naming their problem, not from listing yours.

6. Jumping straight to the VIP bonuses

What it looks like: "How cool is that. Now, the VIP includes…" — the stack, with no reason for it to exist.

Why it happens: The bonuses feel like the pitch.

Why it costs you: A stack with no story is a price list. The viewer compares, instead of deciding.

The fix: "Now let me tell you how we make this even better." The day after. The firehose. The two kinds of people in the room. "The people who go VIP are the ones who go faster — that's exactly why it exists." Then build, access, keep.

7. Fake urgency, hidden terms

What it looks like: "Seats are limited." "You'll only see this once." A 14-day trial with no mention of what happens on day 15.

Why it happens: It's what every other video does.

Why it costs you: They find out. And then nothing else you say in any video is true either.

The fix: Real reasons. The replay can't fill in your tool with you. The cap is real or it's not said. The trial's terms, plainly: how long, what after, how to cancel, what they keep. "I hate sneaky trials."

8. Reading it like a document

What it looks like: Your SCORE™ Card is written copy. Read on camera as-is, it sounds stiff. "Twenty-four point six million dollars" trips the tongue.

Why it happens: The words are right, so you don't change them.

Why it costs you: People watch videos. They don't read them. A written sentence spoken aloud sounds like someone reading.

The fix: Read every line out loud before you record. Fix anything you'd never say. Numerals on the page. "You're going to," not "you will." Talk to one person, like a friend over coffee.

9. Giving the tool away at registration

What it looks like: "Your Scorecard is attached to this email. Print it and bring it on the day."

Why it happens: It feels generous, and it's what every other funnel does.

Why it costs you: They've got the thing. The event just became optional.

The fix: Hold it up on camera. Point at the piece that breaks their problem. Then: "this is what you'll get when you show up." The tool is the reason to be in the room — keep it there.

Recording Your Authority Detonator™

The script is done. Now you get on camera. Here's what actually matters for a video that fills a room.

  1. Print the tool — your copy. Your Event Magnet™, on paper, in your hand from Section 3 on. If it's a one-pager, print it large. A page in a hand beats a page on a screen. The registrant gets theirs on the day.
  2. Hold it still, then point. In Section 3, let the camera see the whole page before you point at anything. In Section 4, one finger, one piece, one line. Pause after each so the camera sees it. When you say "this box, right here," turn the page so the camera sees the box.
  3. Keep the setup simple. Good light on your face, decent sound, a clean background. A phone is fine. A cheap mic is fine.
  4. One take, if you can. Imperfect and real beats polished and stiff. A pause is fine. An "um" is fine. It should feel like you sat down and talked to one person.
  5. Look at the lens, not the screen. Put the Google Doc or Word version right next to the camera — the spoken sections come first, clean type, numerals.
  6. Point where the button is. You're recording for a page with the button under the video. In Section 6, point down and say "right under this video." It works every time.
  7. No date. If you say one by habit, re-take the line. The time's on the page and in the email.
  8. Put it above the fold. On the page, the video is the first thing they see. Autoplay if the page allows it, or a very obvious play button. The calendar button sits right under it.
