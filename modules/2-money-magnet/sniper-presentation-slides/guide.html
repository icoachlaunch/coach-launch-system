What This Is (and Why It Matters)

Sniper Presentation™ Slides takes the Script you already built and approved in Part 2 and turns it into a full slide deck. Your words, your brand, in minutes — presented from your own laptop.

Here's the shift. Most people treat slides as a design project. You open PowerPoint, fight with templates, drag boxes around for hours, and end up with a deck you use once. Next launch, you start over from a blank file. Nothing carries forward.

You don't have a slide problem. You have a template problem.

Slides are just HTML. And once the design lives in twenty reusable molds instead of in one disposable file, your slide work finally compounds. A new event is a new Script through the same molds. A rebrand is one block of colour and type. Neither one is a design project any more.

✦ The one that makes this special

Your Sniper Presentation™ Script from Step 4 already comes as slides in disguise. Every beat has a SLIDE line (what's on screen) and a SPEAKER SCRIPT line (what you say). Sniper Presentation™ Slides reads that script and turns each beat into a real slide — and it keeps your speaker script as private presenter notes only you see. You're not designing from scratch. You're converting a script that's already in slide form.

This is Part 3 of Step 4 · The Sniper Close™. Part 2 builds your Sniper Presentation™ — the live pitch, script and all. Part 3 (this) turns that script into the slides you stand up and deliver.

Three pieces you keep, plus two things you bring.

Why four files, not one

Your words live in the Script. The structure lives in the molds. The look lives in the themes. Your deck is the three of them assembled — and because they're separate, you can change any one without touching the others.

And two things you bring:

A

Your approved Script

Your Sniper Presentation™ Script from Step 4 · Part 2 — approved. That approval is what makes the file safe to build from. Not approved yet, or still carrying unverified figures? The Builder stops and sends you back to finish it. That's the point, not a bug.

B

Your brand

Your Business Brand Profile + Visual Style Guide. This is what makes the deck yours instead of generic — your colors, your fonts, your presenter mark. Every mold reads from one token block, so your whole deck rebrands in one move.

The Rule That Protects Your Script

This tool never writes or changes a single word of your copy. Every word on every slide comes out of your Script, exactly as you approved it.

That matters more than it sounds. Your Sniper Presentation™ Script was built, coached, and put through an 8-check Ready Check in Part 2. If a slide tool quietly reworded a headline to make it fit, your approved script would no longer be approved — and nobody would know.

So the line is hard. The Builder's only freedom is where a line breaks. It never tightens a phrase, fixes a typo, reorders a sentence, or trims a list to make it fit.

When copy doesn't fit

It stops and shows you exactly what overflows — "slide 22 has nine value-stack rows, the mold holds eight" — and you decide what to cut, in your Script. Then it rebuilds that slide. It will not choose which of your points to drop.

Want a line changed? Change it in the Script (or re-run Part 2), tell the Builder what moved, and it rebuilds the affected slides. Your copy stays yours.

The Big Upgrade: A Real Presenter View

Most slide tools make you choose: put your talk track ON the slide (so the room reads it and tunes you out), or keep it somewhere else and lose your place live. Sniper Presentation™ Slides solves both, because your Sniper Presentation™ Script already separates the two.

✓ On the slide (audience)

One idea. A headline, a visual anchor, minimal words — in your brand. The room looks at it for a second and looks back at you.

✗ Not on the slide

Your full talk track. A slide crammed with everything you plan to say is a teleprompter, not a slide — and the room reads ahead instead of listening.

So your SPEAKER SCRIPT rides along as presenter notes — hidden from the audience, there for you. In the Slide Viewer you get:

Timer

Run sheet + timer

Press T to start a timer, and open the ☰ run sheet to see every slide grouped by phase — so you pace the room and never lose the thread.

✔ Why this matters for the close

A Sniper Presentation™ builds trust, then desire, then belief, then the decision. That only works if the room is watching YOU, not reading your script off the wall. Clean slides for them, full script for you — that's how the room closes itself.

Where your deck differs from this

Three things change the table, and the Builder reads all three from your Script rather than asking you.

Price first, deposit second

On a Deposit Close the full price always leads. Lead with the deposit and it reads as a trick; lead with the price and it reads as a courtesy. The Builder will not reorder those two slides.

A 3-Day Challenge runs the same beats in the same order, split across three days with a day-open and a day-recap around each block — Day 1 is WHO, Day 2 is WHAT, Day 3 is WHY and WHEN. That comes out around 40 slides.

A VIP Hour adds an accent divider — at the front if you run it before the event, at the back if you run it after. The VIP Hour never carries the offer.

✦ Why the surfaces alternate

Look down the surface column: it never runs more than two of the same in a row, and the accent colour is spent only four times — the Big Shift, the Confidence Check, the Price, and Urgency. That rhythm is deliberate. A deck that's all one shade goes flat about fifteen minutes in, and when everything is emphasised nothing is.

Story-bit slides are styled as a change of pace on purpose — dark, one line of text, the story itself in your presenter notes. The room leans in because the screen suddenly stopped competing with you.

Pick Your Look — Six Themes

Your Script decides what the deck says. The plan table decides what it does. The theme decides what it feels like — and that's the one choice the Builder can't make for you, because only you know the room you're standing in front of.

All six themes run the same twenty molds and the same plan. What changes is colour, type, and corner treatment. A serif on warm paper says something very different from electric cyan on slate, even with identical words on the slide.

Crimson

the default

The Coach Launch house look. Confident, modern, high contrast — Montserrat headlines over Lato, generous corners, the signature crimson.

Fits: anyone who doesn't want to think about it.

Ink

editorial restraint

A serif headline on warm paper, square corners, one deep oxblood accent used sparingly. Reads like a considered document rather than a pitch.

Fits: consultants, advisors, authors — anyone selling judgement rather than energy.

Ember

warm and approachable

Terracotta on a cream canvas, soft rounded corners, a friendly geometric headline. Feels like a conversation, not a sales page.

Fits: life, health and relationship coaches — where trust matters more than swagger.

Sovereign

navy and gold

A high serif, near-square corners, gold as a precious accent rather than a highlighter. Your accent slides go navy, not gold — so the money slides read expensive instead of loud.

Fits: high-ticket finance, wealth, legacy and executive work.

Voltage

dark throughout

A slate deck with an electric cyan accent and a technical grotesque headline. The whole presentation runs dark, which reads sharp on a big screen and stops a bright room washing you out.

Fits: technical, operations, agency and B2B audiences.

Meadow

bright and optimistic

A clean green on white with a rounded modern headline and open spacing. High energy without shouting.

Fits: fitness, wellness, nutrition — and any audience that skews younger.
✦ See them before you choose

Open coach-launch-themes.html in your browser. Every theme is rendered on three real slides — a light one, a dark one, and an accent one — so you're comparing actual slides, not colour swatches. Pick the one that looks like the business you're selling.

Then your own colours go over the top

A theme is the starting point, not the finish. If your Visual Style Guide has your own hero colour and fonts, the Builder lays them over whichever theme you picked. You keep that theme's proportions, corners and character — and you get your palette.

So pick on feel. The colour can follow you. And if you haven't built your Brand Foundations yet, the theme ships exactly as designed, which is a perfectly good place to start.

Changing your mind is cheap

Every one of the twenty molds reads its colours and fonts from a single block of tokens. Switching theme rewrites that one block in every slide and nothing else — so "rebuild it in Sovereign" is a couple of minutes, and every headline stays exactly as you approved it. Build in one theme, present in another, and nobody has to re-approve a word.

Adding Images (Genuinely Optional)

You need zero artwork to build this deck. Every one of the twenty molds works from words alone — and the Builder will never ask you for a photo, a logo, or a diagram.

That's deliberate. Nothing stalls a launch like being told you need artwork before you can present. If you don't supply an image, the slot is removed and the slide reflows — it doesn't sit there as an empty grey box.

But a few slides do lift with one: your intro, story-bits, your $Million Moment™, and the send-off. If you want images, here's how they work.

An image can go behind any slide

Every one of the twenty molds carries a background layer, so you're not limited to a few "image slides." Want a photo behind your value stack, your framework, or your price? Just say so. Two things happen automatically: a veil is laid over the image so your text stays readable — pale on a light slide, dark on a dark one, tinted in your brand colour on an accent slide — and if you supply no image, the layer is deleted rather than left as an empty box.

Restraint is the whole skill

A background on every slide is exhausting to sit through and makes your deck harder to read, not easier. Three or four across a full deck is plenty. If an image is only filling space, cut it — a clean text slide in your brand beats a decorative photo every time.

✦ Six copy-paste prompt templates

image-prompts.md ships with your kit. It's organised by what the image is doing — background, hero, make-a-point, object, texture, people — so once you know the job you can put it on any slide. Each one is a block you copy, fill in the brackets, and paste into any generator. It also carries the four-part formula, the style suffix that makes a set look like one deck, and the ratios. Six ready-made backgrounds ship in images/, desaturated to work under all six themes.

Never generate a person

Your founder photo should be you. A generated face on your own intro slide is the fastest way to lose a room that later meets you in person. Your Event Magnet™ should be the real asset you built in Step 3 — that's what filled the room, so put the actual thing on screen. And a generated "client" on a proof slide is a fabricated testimonial, whatever the intention.

Anonymous human presence — seen from behind, face not visible — is the one legitimate exception, and the template covers how to ask for it.

The rule is hybrid: the text stays in HTML — crisp, on-brand, perfectly spelled — and the AI generates only the picture behind it. Never let AI put words inside an image; it misspells and goes off-brand. Text in HTML, picture from AI.

In Cowork or Claude Code, use the project's kie-image-gen skill. It asks for your Kie.ai key once, lets you preview and approve, and downloads the picture for you. On the web, generate the image in Kie.ai's web app and tell the Builder which one to use on each slide — same hybrid result, a couple more clicks.

✔ Cost is tiny

Images run about ten cents each through your own Kie.ai account. A whole deck is usually a dollar or two — and you only image-up the few slides that earn it.

Changing It Later

Because your words and your design live in separate files, you can change either one without touching the other. There are only two kinds of change, and they work differently on purpose.

A

New look, same words

Name another theme and say "rebuild." Crimson to Sovereign, Ember to Voltage — every mold reads from one token block, so the whole deck changes character at once. Minutes, not an afternoon, and not a single word re-approved.

B

New words

Edit your Script — or re-run Part 2 — then tell the Builder what changed. It rebuilds only the affected slides. It won't edit copy for you, which is exactly why you can trust that what's on the screen is what you approved.

Tips for Great Decks

Asset first, always. The deck is only as good as the asset. Get your Sniper Presentation™ Script tight in Step 4 before you build slides. The script is the spec; slides just visualize it.

Feed it your real Brand Foundations. The more it knows your colors, fonts, and voice, the more the deck looks like you and not a template.

Fewer, richer slides beat more, thinner ones. Let each slide carry one real idea, and talk through it. The teaching is in your mouth, not crammed on the wall.

Use your presenter view. Rehearse with S; present with P on a second screen. Your script is right there — you never have to memorise or read off the wall.

Fix fit flags in the Script, not the slide. When the Builder says a beat overruns its mold, that's your copy telling you it's carrying too much for one screen. Cutting it in the Script usually makes the spoken version better too.

Common Mistakes

✗ Don't

Try to write your Sniper Presentation™ inside this tool. It builds slides — it doesn't write the pitch.

✓ Do

Build your Script first with The Sniper Close™ Builder (Step 4), then bring the finished Script here.

✗ Don't

Expect it to invent testimonials, stats, or results to fill a proof slide. It won't make those up — and you wouldn't want it to.

✓ Do

Put your real proof in the Script. If there's none there yet, it drops the proof slide and tells you — rather than quietly filling it with something that isn't true.

✗ Don't

Ask it to "tighten slide 6" or reword a headline that doesn't quite fit. It will refuse, and it should.

✓ Do

Change the line in your Script, then say what moved. It rebuilds those slides. Your approved words stay approved.

✗ Don't

Dump your whole talk track onto the slide so the room reads ahead of you.

✓ Do

Keep the slide to one idea and let your speaker script live in the presenter notes, where only you see it.

✗ Don't

Treat every deck as a from-scratch design project — the PowerPoint habit.

✓ Do

Reuse the molds. A new event is a new Script through the same twenty molds; a rebrand is one token block.

Heads up — keep it one file

The single-file Slide Viewer opens with a double-click and works offline. If you ever split slides into a separate folder, a plain double-click can hit browser security — so stick with the single file, or preview it inside your AI tool. For almost everyone, the one file is the answer.
