What The Deposit Close Is — and What It Isn't

The Deposit Close is one of three ways your Sniper Presentation™ can end. You ask the room to put a deposit down against your high-ticket offer, right there.

No booked session. No follow-up call. No conversation of any kind. They pay the deposit, they're enrolled, and the rest of the money follows on the terms you set.

Why a deposit at all?

Because sometimes people want in and don't have the full amount in front of them today.

The deposit is a commitment fee. It's not a discount, not a trial, and not a smaller version of your offer. It's the part of the price they pay now to hold their place, and it comes off the total.

This is the hardest close to earn — and the simplest to run.

Asking a room to hand over money for a $10K–$25K offer, with no call to reassure them, only works when everything upstream is already doing the persuading: a proven event, a proven offer, and real results behind you.

That's why it sits at the top of the ladder. You don't start here.

What it is NOT:

It's not a booking fee for a call. There is no call. If a conversation is what closes them, you're running the Strategy Close.

It's not a way to make a big price sound small. The full price is stated plainly. The deposit is how they start paying it, not a way to hide it.

It's not a deposit on a maybe. They are enrolling. The deposit is the first payment of a decision that's already been made.

The Ask — Closing From the Stage

The value stack is walked, the anchor is set, the price is out. Now the ask — and it's the shortest of the three.

Beat 1

State the full price first

The whole number, out loud, before you mention the deposit.

Lead with the deposit and it reads as a trick — they'll assume you're hiding something. Lead with the price and the deposit becomes a courtesy.

Beat 2

Name the deposit and what it does

Say the amount, say it comes off the total, and say what it buys them: their place.

A deposit has to hold something. A spot in the cohort, this price before it rises, the fast-action bonus. "It starts your payments" is not a reason to pay today.

Beat 3

Say the terms plainly — including that it's non-refundable

When the balance is due, how it's paid, and that the deposit is non-refundable.

Say it out loud, in the room. It sounds like it should cost you sales. It doesn't — it's what makes the commitment real, and the people it filters out were never going to pay the balance.

What you must never do is leave it vague and let them discover it later.

Beat 4

Give the steps, then stop talking

Numbered. On screen. Pay the deposit, you're in, here's what happens next.

Then stop. No call to book, no session to sell, nothing to soften it with. This close lives or dies in the silence.

How it sounds

"The full investment is [full price]. That's what it costs to do this with me."

"You don't need all of it today. [Deposit amount] holds your place in the cohort, and it comes straight off the total — so it's not extra, it's the first part of it."

"The balance is due [terms]. And to be straight with you: the deposit is non-refundable. It's a commitment fee — it's what makes the place yours instead of someone else's."

"Link's on the screen. Put your deposit down and you're in. I'll see you in there."

Setting the Deposit

How much?

$997 is the recommended default, and your own number comes from your Money Model.

The shape that works is roughly 5–10% of the full price. Big enough that paying it means something. Small enough that someone who wants in can find it this week.

Too small and it stops filtering — you'll collect deposits from people who never pay the balance. Too big and it's just the price with extra steps.

Non-refundable — and why that helps you both

A refundable deposit isn't a commitment, it's a placeholder. People put it down to stop thinking about the decision, then change their mind in a quiet moment three days later.

Non-refundable makes the decision real in the room, which is exactly where you want it made. It also protects the person who did commit — their place isn't being held open by someone who's still deciding.

State it clearly, honour it consistently, and never bury it in the small print.

It comes off the total. Always.

The deposit is part of the price, not on top of it. Say so every time. The moment a deposit looks like an extra fee, this close stops working.

Why There's No Call — and What Replaces It

In the other two closes, a conversation does some of the work. Here, nothing does. So the work has to happen somewhere else.

The presentation carries everything.

Every objection a call would have handled has to be handled on stage — the price, the time, the "will this work for me," the "I need to talk to my partner." If your Sniper Presentation™ doesn't raise and answer them, nobody will.

The proof has to be undeniable.

People hand over money without a conversation when the results in front of them make the decision obvious. Real numbers, real people, real transformations.

The enrolment has to be flawless.

They pay, and then what? A confirmation, a clear statement of the terms, the balance date, and exactly what happens next. That's the moment a call would have reassured them — so the process has to do it instead.

Getting this wrong is where deposit closes leak. Somebody pays, hears nothing for four days, and starts wondering what they've done.

The urgency has to be real.

A deposit buys scarcity. If nothing is genuinely limited — places, this price, a bonus that expires — the deposit has nothing to hold and there's no reason to pay today rather than think about it.

Never manufacture it. A room at this level can smell a fake deadline, and it costs you more than the sale.

A Worked Example (Illustrative)

A made-up coach, to show the difference — not a real client, no real numbers.

Before — the call bottleneck.

A consultant runs a strong two-day event and ends by inviting people to book a call about her $18,000 programme. Nine book. Six show up. Three buy.

The calls take a fortnight to work through. By the time she reaches the last one, the energy from the event is long gone and the answer is "let me come back to you."

After — the Deposit Close.

Same event, different ending. She states the full price, then offers a place held by a deposit that comes off the total — non-refundable, said plainly from the stage, balance due in 30 days. Eight places, and she means it.

Five people pay before they leave the room. Each gets an immediate confirmation with the terms, the balance date, and their start date.

Four pay the balance within the month. The fifth asks for a payment plan and she agrees.

The offer never changed and neither did the price. She just stopped putting a two-week gap between the decision and the payment.

Hard Rules

Full price first, deposit second. Lead with the deposit and it reads as a trick.

The deposit must hold something real. A place, a price, a bonus. "It starts your payments" is not a reason to pay today.

Say non-refundable out loud. In the room, in the ask. Never in the small print.

It always comes off the total. A deposit that looks like an extra fee kills the close.

No call. Not even a friendly one. The moment you add a conversation, you're running a different close — and you've reintroduced the bottleneck you removed.

Real scarcity only. If nothing is genuinely limited, the deposit has nothing to hold.

Confirm immediately. Terms, balance date, and what happens next — within minutes, not days.

Earn it first. This close needs a proven event and real proof. Run it too early and you'll conclude it doesn't work.

Your numbers are yours. Full price, deposit, and terms come from your Money Model.

Common Mistakes That Kill the Close

Running it too early

The most common failure by a distance. A Launch-phase coach with a small audience and no results asks a cold room to put down $997, sight unseen. Nobody moves, and they decide the close is broken.

The fix: earn it. Use the Strategy Close while you're building proof. This one works when the event and the results are already doing the convincing.

Hiding the non-refundable term

You don't mention it on stage because it feels awkward. Someone asks for their money back a week later, you say no, and now you have an angry person telling everyone.

The fix: say it in the ask, plainly, as a normal part of the terms. Stated clearly it's fair. Discovered later it's a betrayal.

Fake scarcity

"Only five spots!" — and then six people pay and everyone gets in. The room notices, and every other number you've quoted all day becomes suspect.

The fix: pick a real limit and hold it. If you can genuinely take everyone, use a real price deadline or a real bonus instead.

Silence after the payment

They pay and hear nothing for days. There was no call to reassure them, and now there's no confirmation either. Buyer's remorse fills the gap.

The fix: immediate confirmation with the terms, the balance date, and the start date. Automate it so it can't be forgotten.

Adding "just a quick call" when the room goes quiet

Nobody moves, so you soften it — "or book a call if you'd rather chat." You've now got the worst of both: a close nobody trusts and a diary full of undecided people.

The fix: pick your close before you're on stage and hold it. If you wanted the conversation, that's the Strategy Close — run it deliberately, not as a rescue.

A deposit that's too small to mean anything

You drop it to $97 to get more takers. You get them — and most never pay the balance, because $97 wasn't a commitment, it was a tip.

The fix: 5–10% of the full price. Enough that paying it changes how they think about the decision.

Is It Ready?

No score out of 25 here. Pass or fail — and this close is the least forgiving of the three.

  • ✔ Earned — your event and your offer are proven, with real results to show.
  • ✔ Priced — full price, deposit, and balance terms all set in your Money Model.
  • ✔ Proportionate — the deposit is roughly 5–10% of the full price.
  • ✔ Stated — non-refundable is said out loud in the ask, not buried.
  • ✔ Credited — the deposit comes off the total, and you say so.
  • ✔ Holding something — a real place, price, or bonus that the deposit reserves.
  • ✔ Objection-complete — your presentation answers everything a call would have.
  • ✔ Confirmed — an immediate confirmation carrying the terms and the balance date.

All eight? Your Deposit Close is ready — and you've taken the sales call out of your business without losing the sale.
