What The Membership Close Is — and What It Isn't

The Membership Close is one of three ways your Sniper Presentation™ can end. You ask the room to join your membership, right there, on the spot.

Then — and this is the part that makes it work — you get on a call with every person who joined.

That call is the onboarding call. It isn't a sales call. The sale already happened. Its only job is to get them started.

The one thing to understand before anything else:

A membership doesn't die from bad closing. It dies from month-two churn.

Someone joins, feels good about it, then gets busy, never logs in, sees a second payment leave their account, and cancels. You didn't lose them in the room. You lost them in the fortnight afterwards.

So the Membership Close doesn't end when they say yes. It ends when they've started.

What it is NOT:

It's not a low-ticket offer. Your membership is a high-ticket offer paid over time — monthly, or annually at a saving. It's priced like a serious commitment because it is one.

It's not a course with a subscription bolted on. People pay monthly for ongoing access to you and to a room of people doing the same thing. If the value stops after month one, so does the membership.

It's not a consolation prize for people who wouldn't buy the big thing. It's a genuine front door — often the easiest first yes you'll ever get.

What breaks without it:

You run a great event, the room is warm, and the only thing on offer is a $15,000 commitment. Most of them can't say yes to that today — not because they don't want it, but because it's too big a first step. They leave, and you never hear from them again.

The Ask — Closing From the Stage

Your Sniper Presentation™ has done its work. You've made the offer, walked the value stack, anchored, and revealed the price. Now the ask.

Beat 1

Name what they're joining

Not "my membership." Name it, and say what being in it means.

People aren't buying access to content. They're buying a room, a rhythm, and you. Say that plainly.

Beat 2

Give two ways to pay — and make the annual obviously better

This is the mechanic that separates this close from the others.

Monthly lowers the barrier. It's the easiest yes in the room, and it's how most people will join.

Annual saves them real money — a 25% saving is the shape that works. And it does two things for you that monthly can't: it brings the cash forward, and it removes the month-two decision entirely.

Show both. Let the saving do the persuading. Don't push.

Beat 3

Tell them exactly what happens next

Here's the beat most people skip, and it's the one that protects your retention.

"When you join, you'll get a call from me in the next few days to get you set up."

Say it in the ask, not after. It de-risks the decision — they're not buying a login, they're buying a start. And it sets the expectation that they'll turn up for it.

Beat 4

Give the steps, then stop talking

Numbered. On screen. Pick monthly or annual, join, done.

Then stop. Don't re-explain the two prices. Don't justify. Let it sit.

How it sounds

"So here's what I'd like to do. I'd like you inside [membership name] — that's where we do this together, every month, with the people already doing it."

"Two ways in. You can join monthly, or you can take the annual and save 25% — same thing, you just pay once and save yourself a chunk."

"And when you join, you'll get a call from me in the next few days to get you set up properly. You're not just getting a login."

"Link's on the screen. Pick the one that suits you. I'll see you on the inside."

Why Two Prices, and How to Set Them

Every membership close offers the same choice: pay monthly, or pay annually and save. Understanding why stops you fiddling with it.

Monthly gets the yes.

It's a smaller number, so more people say it. That's the whole job. Monthly is how you fill the room.

Annual keeps the member.

An annual member has already decided. There's no second payment to reconsider, no month-two wobble, no card that expires in March. They're in for the year, which means they've given themselves time to actually get a result.

They also hand you the cash up front — which is what pays for the next event.

The saving is the lever, and 25% is the shape that works.

Too small and nobody switches. Too big and you've discounted your own offer for no reason. A quarter off — roughly three months free — is enough to move the people who were always going to stay, without bribing the ones who weren't.

Your actual numbers come from your Money Model. Don't invent them on stage, and don't discount them in the room because it went quiet.

The Onboarding Call — Where Retention Is Won

Book it within a few days of them joining. Keep it short — twenty to thirty minutes is plenty.

It has one job: get them started before the enthusiasm wears off.

Beat 1

Welcome and reassure

Tell them they made a good call, and mean it. A little buyer's remorse is normal — this is where you settle it.

Beat 2

Find out where they actually are

One question: what made you join, and what do you want in the next 90 days?

You need this in their words, because it tells you where to point them first.

Beat 3

Point at ONE thing

Not the whole library. One thing to do first, chosen for them, based on what they just told you.

Overwhelm is the number one cause of a member going quiet. The cure is a single next action.

Beat 4

Get them into the room

Make sure they're in the community, and that they've posted or said hello once while you're with them.

A member who has spoken to another member stays far longer than one who hasn't. Do it on the call — don't leave it to them.

Beat 5

Book the next touchpoint

The next live call, the next session, whatever your rhythm is. Put it in their diary before you hang up.

Never end an onboarding call without a specific next date.

What this call is NOT:

It is not a sales call. Don't upsell, don't pitch the high-ticket, don't mention anything they could buy. They just paid you. Selling now tells them the membership wasn't the real offer.

It is not a coaching session. You're orienting, not solving. Twenty minutes, one direction, done.

The Real Enemy: Month Two

Every membership has the same shape. A cohort joins, and a chunk of them leave at the second payment.

They didn't dislike it. They just never started, and now they're paying for something they aren't using. Cancelling is the rational move.

What actually keeps them:

A win in the first fortnight. Not a big one. Any one. A member who has done something and seen it work has a reason to stay.

A face they know. One conversation with you, one with another member. Belonging beats content every time.

A reason to show up next week. A rhythm they can feel — a call, a thread, a deadline. Membership without rhythm is just a folder of videos.

Watch it monthly:

Track how many join and how many are still there at payment three. That number tells you whether your membership works, and no amount of good closing will fix it if it's bad.

A Worked Example (Illustrative)

A made-up coach, to show the difference — not a real client, no real numbers.

Before — the big ask only.

A marketing consultant ends her masterclass with one option: her 12-month programme at $12,000. The room has been warm all day. Three people book calls, one buys.

Everyone else liked her, learned something, and left. There was nothing they could say yes to.

After — the Membership Close.

Same event, different ending. She invites the room into her membership — monthly, or annual at 25% off. She tells them plainly that everyone who joins gets a call from her that week to get set up.

Fourteen join. Eleven monthly, three annual.

She runs fourteen short onboarding calls. On each one she asks what they want in 90 days, points them at one thing, gets them to say hello in the group while she's there, and books them onto the next live call.

At payment three, twelve are still in.

The offer never changed. She just gave the room a door they could actually walk through — and then walked them through it.

Hard Rules

Always offer both prices. Monthly gets the yes, annual keeps the member. Showing only one costs you either volume or retention.

Promise the onboarding call in the ask. Not afterwards. It de-risks the decision and sets the expectation they'll attend.

Run the call within a few days. Enthusiasm has a short shelf life. A call three weeks later is a rescue mission.

Never sell on the onboarding call. They just paid you. Pitching now says the membership wasn't the real offer.

One next action, not the whole library. Overwhelm is what makes members go quiet.

Never end the call without a booked next touchpoint.

Don't discount in the room. The annual saving is your discount. It's already built in.

Your numbers are yours. The membership price and the annual saving come from your Money Model.

Common Mistakes That Kill the Close

Treating the membership as the cheap option

You present it apologetically, as the thing for people who can't afford the real offer. The room hears exactly that, and joining feels like admitting you couldn't.

The fix: your membership is a high-ticket offer paid over time. Present it like the front door it is, not the bargain bin.

Skipping the onboarding call when a lot of people join

Fourteen people join, that's fourteen calls, and you're tired. So you send a welcome email instead. Then you watch half of them cancel at payment two and conclude memberships don't work.

The fix: the calls ARE the close. If the volume is genuinely too high, shorten them to fifteen minutes or run them in small groups — but do not replace them with an email.

Pitching the high-ticket on the onboarding call

They're warm, they just paid, so you mention the bigger programme. Now the membership feels like a tripwire, and they trust you slightly less than they did an hour ago.

The fix: nothing for sale on that call. Ascension happens later, from results — not from a pitch in week one.

Handing over everything at once

"Here's the portal, there are 60 modules, dive in." They open it, feel behind before they've begun, and never come back.

The fix: one thing. Chosen by you, for them, based on what they said they wanted.

Only offering monthly

It feels friendlier, and it fills the room. But every member now faces a fresh decision every 30 days, and your cash arrives in dribs.

The fix: always show the annual with a real saving. The people who were serious will take it, and they're the ones who stay.

Never looking at churn

You count joins and feel good. Nobody counts who's left by payment three, so a leaky membership looks like a growing one for months.

The fix: check retention monthly. Joins tell you the close works. Retention tells you the membership does.

  • ✔ Bookable — you have a way to get every new member onto a call within days.

  • ✔ Rhythmic — there's a next touchpoint to book them onto.
