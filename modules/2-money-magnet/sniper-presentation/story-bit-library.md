# The Story-Bit Library

Ten speaker bits — timeless, repeatable stories you slot into a Sniper Presentation™
to raise emotion and move a room.

A bit isn't filler. It's a story that does **one job at one moment**: build engagement,
create hype, break a limiting belief, break an objection, or prime the close.

Story is the driver. Emotion makes the sale. These are the units that carry it.

---

## HOW A BIT IS BUILT — the six parts

Every bit in this library follows the same shape, and so should any you write yourself:

1. **HOOK** — the engagement setup. Gets hands up or answers in the chat before the story starts.
2. **STORY** — the metaphor or narrative, with call-and-response built in.
3. **TURN** — the bridge from the story to THEM.
4. **TARGET** — the belief or objection it breaks. This is why the bit exists.
5. **SOUNDBYTE** — the one line they'll repeat afterwards.
6. **TRANSITION** — the hand-off into the next beat.

---

## WHERE THEY GO

**PRE-PITCH bits** — the front half of your event (Phases 1–2, WHO and WHAT).
Jobs: engagement, hype, empowering belief. These warm the room and open minds BEFORE the offer.

**RE-PITCH bits** — the back half, around the close (Phases 3–4, WHY and WHEN).
Jobs: break a belief, break an objection, prime the decision. These fill your Belief / Bust-It
and Objection / Handle-It rows.

There is no bit for the pitch itself. The pitch IS your Sniper Close™ — engineered for your
specific offer, not a swappable story.

---

## THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS

**Any story you tell as your OWN must be true and yours.**

The bits below are classic teaching parables. Retell them in your own words and credit the
source where one is known — that's fair game and always has been. What you must never do is
pass a borrowed story off as something that happened to you.

A live room can feel a fake. And when they catch it, they don't just discount the story —
they discount everything you said before it.

---

## PICK BY TARGET, NOT BY VIBE

Don't choose the bit you like best. Choose the one whose **Target** matches a belief or
objection your Golden Avatar™ actually holds.

A brilliant bit aimed at a belief the room doesn't have is three wasted minutes at the exact
moment you can least afford them.

# PRE-PITCH BITS — Phases 1–2 (WHO / WHAT)

## Take the Shot

- **Placement:** Pre-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Serendipity+Connection · 1 WHO
- **Job:** empowering-belief
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "I'm scared to fail, so I don't start." (Fear of failure keeps them frozen before they ever try.)
- **Soundbyte:** "You miss every shot you don't take."
- **Source:** Original. Cites the verified Michael Jordan line from the 1990 Nike "Failure" ad (written by Jamie Barrett); Edison's lightbulb attempts are common public lore — told loosely, no hard count.
- **Runtime:** ~3 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"Let me ask you something. How many of you have held back from something — a launch, an
offer, a post — because a little voice said *what if it flops?* Be honest. Type **me**."

### 2 · STORY
"Yeah. Me too. So let me tell you about the best there ever was.

Michael Jordan did a Nike ad back in 1990. And he doesn't brag in it. He counts his
failures. He says: *I've missed over 9,000 shots. I've lost almost 300 games. 26 times
I was trusted to take the game-winning shot — and missed.* And then he says the line:
*I've failed over and over and over again in my life. And that is why I succeed.*

Read that again. The greatest player alive says he's great **because** he failed more
than anyone.

Thomas Edison, same thing. Story goes he ran through thousands of tries before the
lightbulb worked. Somebody asked how it felt to fail that many times. He said he didn't
fail — he found thousands of ways that don't work. Every miss was a step."

### 3 · TURN
"Here's the thing. That voice telling you *don't try, you might look stupid* — everybody
in this room has it. The winners just stopped obeying it.

You're not behind because you failed. You're behind because you haven't taken enough
shots yet. Missing isn't the enemy. Sitting still is."

### 4 · TARGET
Dissolves fear-of-failure paralysis early, so that when the offer comes later, "what if
it doesn't work for me?" has already been reframed as a normal, necessary part of
winning. Turns hesitation into permission to act.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"You miss every shot you don't take."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So today, you're taking the shot. Type **taking the shot** if you're done sitting on
the bench. Good. Let me show you where to aim."

---

### Placement notes
Best early, while building belief and rapport (WHO/Connection). Also works as a
courage bit right before an action step. The Jordan quote is verified — keep it
accurate. Don't add invented numbers to the Edison line; "thousands" is enough.

## The Bamboo

- **Placement:** Pre-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Opportunity · 2 WHAT
- **Job:** hype / empowering-belief
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "It's not working fast enough, so it must not be working." (They quit right before the payoff because early effort is invisible.)
- **Soundbyte:** "The growth was happening the whole time — underground."
- **Source:** Adapted from the widely-told Chinese bamboo tree parable (common public analogy). Told without exact year/height figures to avoid overstating.
- **Runtime:** ~2–3 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"How many of you have ever started something — eating right, building a business,
learning a skill — done everything right for a while, saw nothing, and wanted to quit?
Type **yep**."

### 2 · STORY
"There's a plant called the Chinese bamboo tree. And it does something strange.

You plant the seed. You water it. You give it sun. And for a long time… nothing. No
sprout. No shoot. Nothing you can see.

A year goes by. Still nothing. You'd swear the seed was dead. Most people would dig it
up and give up.

But the ones who keep watering — who trust it — one day the bamboo breaks the surface.
And then it doesn't crawl. It **shoots** up, tall, fast, further than you'd believe a
plant could grow in a season.

So here's the question. Did that bamboo grow in those few weeks? Type your answer.

No. It was growing the entire time. Down. Building a root system deep and strong enough
to hold everything that was coming. You just couldn't see it."

### 3 · TURN
"How many of you are in your underground season right now? You're putting in the work
and the surface still looks like dirt.

That's not failure. That's roots. The people who make it aren't the ones who grow
fastest — they're the ones who keep watering when they can't see a thing yet."

### 4 · TARGET
Reframes slow early progress as necessary root-building, not proof of failure. It
inoculates against the "I've tried before and nothing happened" quit-reflex and builds
belief that the system pays off if they stay in — which sets up committing to the offer.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"The growth was happening the whole time — underground."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So don't dig up your bamboo. Let me show you the roots we're going to build today —
and what breaks the surface when you do."

---

### Placement notes
Great in the Opportunity act to create belief in the system's payoff, or as a
mid-event re-energizer. Pairs naturally with a Heaven-with-the-system beat.

## The Crossroads

- **Placement:** Pre-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Opportunity · 2 WHAT
- **Job:** empowering-belief
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "I'll decide later / I need more time to think." (Chronic indecision, dressed up as being careful, is the real thing keeping them stuck.)
- **Soundbyte:** "Not deciding is a decision — it just picks the worst option for you."
- **Source:** Original
- **Runtime:** ~2–3 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"Be honest with me. How many of you have something in your life you've been *thinking
about* doing for way too long? Months. Maybe years. Type **guilty**."

### 2 · STORY
"Let me paint you a picture. Imagine you're standing at a crossroads. One road is the
life you've got now. The other is the life you actually want.

And you stand there. And you think about it. You research it. You ask your cousin, your
neighbor, three strangers on the internet. You wait for the perfect moment, the perfect
sign, the perfect amount of money.

Now here's the part nobody tells you. While you're standing there thinking — the sun is
going down. Time is passing. You're not staying still. You're getting older, standing at
the same crossroads, watching the light fade.

Standing still feels safe. It feels careful. But it's not. You didn't avoid a choice.
You chose the road you're already on — by default."

### 3 · TURN
"How many of you have talked yourself out of a decision so many times you forgot you
even wanted the other road?

That's the trap. It doesn't feel like quitting. It feels responsible. But indecision is
just quitting in slow motion."

### 4 · TARGET
Exposes "I'll think about it" as a decision that defaults to the status quo. It plants
the belief that action beats endless deliberation — so that when the offer and the
close arrive, "let me think about it" has already been named as the enemy.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"Not deciding is a decision — it just picks the worst option for you."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So today, you're a decider. Type **decider**. Because what I'm about to show you only
works for the people who actually choose a road."

---

### Placement notes
Strong in the Opportunity act to build a decisive frame early. It also seeds the close:
the "I'll think about it" objection is far weaker later if you've run this up front.

## Train the Room

- **Placement:** Pre-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Serendipity+Connection · 1 WHO
- **Job:** engagement
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "I'll just sit back, watch, and take notes." (Passive attendees don't buy — a quiet room is a dead room.)
- **Soundbyte:** "You don't watch this. You do this."
- **Source:** Original
- **Runtime:** ~2 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"Quick question before we start. How many of you actually want to walk away from today
different — not just with more notes, but changed? Type **I'm in** in the chat right now."

### 2 · STORY
"Here's the deal. I'm not a lecturer. Have you ever sat through a lecture and forgotten
it by lunch? Drop a **yes** if that's you.

That's not your fault. That's how watching works. You watch, you nod, you leave, and it's
gone by dinner.

So we're not going to do that today. We're going to do this together. I'm going to ask
you questions, and when I do, I want you typing in the chat. Sometimes I'll start a
sentence and let you finish it. So if I say the more you put in, the more you get
______ — you type it. Go ahead, type it now: **out.**

See that? You're already doing it."

### 3 · TURN
"Here's why this matters. The people who show up quiet, arms crossed, just watching —
they leave the same way they came. Nothing changes.

The people who lean in, who answer, who get their hands dirty — those are the people
things actually shift for. Every single time."

### 4 · TARGET
Breaks passive-attendee mode at the very top. A room that's answering, typing, and
saying words out loud is a room that's awake, bonded to you, and primed to say yes
later. This bit sets the rule for the whole event and makes every later engagement
prompt land.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"You don't watch this. You do this."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So that's the deal for today — you show up as a doer, I'll show up with everything I've
got. Fair? Type **fair**. Good. Let's get into it."

---

### Placement notes
Run this near the very start, right after the hook and welcome — before any teaching.
It's the "train the chat" moment. Works for every phase and every close. Keep it fast
and high-energy; it sets the tone. Note: it deliberately avoids the disputed
"retain 10%/70%" learning-pyramid stats — the point stands on its own without them.

# RE-PITCH BITS — Phases 3–4 (WHY / WHEN)

## Down Before Up

- **Placement:** Re-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Remove Risk · 3 WHY (into Execution · 4 WHEN)
- **Job:** belief-break
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "Growth should feel comfortable, and if it's hard or I dip first, something's wrong." (Fear of the dip stops them from committing to real change.)
- **Soundbyte:** "You have to go down before you go up."
- **Source:** Adapted from two public natural-world archetypes — the seed that must break to become a tree, and the eaglet that learns to fly by falling. Retold original.
- **Runtime:** ~3–4 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"Quick one. How many of you have quit something the moment it got uncomfortable — told
yourself *this shouldn't be this hard, maybe it's not for me*? Type **been there.**"

### 2 · STORY
"Let me show you a law nobody talks about. What goes up… must come ______. Type it.
Down. Right — Newton.

But here's the part they leave out. What goes up must go **down first.** Watch. Before
you jump, your body dips. Before a plane climbs, it races *down* the runway. Before a
skyscraper goes up, they dig a hole for the foundation. Down first. Always.

Now look at a seed. This little seed wants to be a big tree. What does it have to do
first? Type it. It has to go **down** — into the dirt, the dark, the cold. And it has to
stop being a seed. It cracks open. It looks like it's dying. That's the exact moment
it's becoming a tree.

Same with an eagle. You know how a mother eagle teaches her eaglet to fly? She carries
it up high on her back… and then she drops it. And that little eaglet falls — feathers
and fluff tumbling through the sky — sure it's the end. And right before it hits, she
swoops under and catches it. Again. And again. Until one day the wind catches its wings,
and it flies.

Eagles learn to fly by **falling first.**"

### 3 · TURN
"How many of you have been standing at the edge of a real change — and pulled back the
second it started to feel like falling?

That's most people. They go all in on themselves, they start to dip, and it's so
uncomfortable they grab the nearest branch and climb back into the same old nest. Safe.
Stuck. Forever.

We're not afraid of falling. We're afraid of hitting the ground. So get yourself a
mother eagle — someone who's going to catch you before you hit."

### 4 · TARGET
Reframes the discomfort and short-term dip of real change as the mechanism of growth,
not a warning sign. It pre-empts "this feels risky/hard" at the close and positions the
offer as the mother eagle — the one who catches them through the dip.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"You have to go down before you go up."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So don't grab the branch this time. Let me show you who's going to catch you while you
learn to fly."

---

### Placement notes
Belief-break for the fear-of-the-dip / fear-of-hard objection — strong just before the
offer. Positions high-touch support (the "mother eagle") as the risk-remover, so it
pairs naturally with the Strategy and Deposit closes.

## Get in the Room

- **Placement:** Re-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Execution/Re-Pitch · 4 WHEN (also Remove Risk · 3 WHY)
- **Job:** belief-break
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "I can figure this out on my own with enough information / free content." (Info-hoarding as a substitute for guidance and proximity.)
- **Soundbyte:** "You don't need more information. You need to get in the room."
- **Source:** Original. Uses the common, true fact that Everest climbers summit with experienced guides (Sherpas) — no invented numbers.
- **Runtime:** ~3–4 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"Raise a hand — or type **me** — if you've bought the courses, watched the videos, saved
the posts… and you still haven't gotten the result you wanted."

### 2 · STORY
"Yeah. A lot of hands. So let me ask you something.

If we got rich from reading books, librarians would be the wealthiest people on earth.
Have you ever seen a librarian pull up in a Ferrari? They've got access to every book
ever written. So why not?

Because information alone doesn't do it.

Let me make it real. Imagine I hand you a book — *How to Climb Mount Everest.* Great
book. You read the whole thing. Everybody say **book.**

Now, good idea or bad idea for you to fly out and climb Everest tomorrow? Type it.

Right. Bad idea. Okay, so you don't stop there. You buy the boots, the oxygen, the gear.
You've got the info AND the equipment now. Better idea or still a bad idea? Type it.

Still bad. You'd die on that mountain and you know it.

Now — what if you climbed it next to a guide who has summited it many times? Someone who
knows every ridge, every storm, when to move, when to stop, and reaches back and grabs
your hand at the parts that kill people. Are your chances better now? Type **yes.**

Not a little better. A thousand times better. Because they know the things the book
can't tell you — the tiny stuff, the timing, the *don't step there.*"

### 3 · TURN
"How many of you have been trying to climb your mountain from a book?

Information alone doesn't get the result. Information plus action, all by yourself,
still doesn't get it. The people who actually make it are the ones who get in the room
with someone already standing on top."

### 4 · TARGET
Breaks the DIY / "just need more free content" belief that stops people from investing
in guidance. It reframes the offer (coaching, mentorship, the program) as the guide on
the mountain — the missing variable — right before the close.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"You don't need more information. You need to get in the room."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So the only question is which room you get in. Let me show you the one I built for you."

---

### Placement notes
A core close-primer whenever the offer is high-touch (coaching/consulting) — pairs with
the Strategy and Deposit closes. Run it just before the Value Stack / offer reveal.

## Release the Grip

- **Placement:** Re-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Execution/Re-Pitch · 4 WHEN
- **Job:** objection-break (money / letting go)
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "I don't want to let go of my money." (Clinging to what they have is the very thing keeping them stuck.)
- **Soundbyte:** "Release the grip."
- **Source:** Adapted from the "South Indian monkey trap," made famous by Robert Pirsig in *Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance* (1974) — a public parable about value rigidity. Retold in original words and credited.
- **Runtime:** ~3–4 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"How many of you have ever wondered how you actually catch a monkey? Stay with me —
there's a big point coming. Type **tell me.**"

### 2 · STORY
"There's an old parable — a writer named Pirsig made it famous — about how villagers
trap a monkey.

They don't use a net. They don't chase it. They take a coconut, hollow it out, and
chain it to the ground. Then they cut one hole — just big enough for a monkey's open
hand to slide in. And inside, they put a little rice, or a banana. Everybody say
**banana.**

The monkey smells it. Reaches in. Grabs a fistful. And now here's the trap — the hole
is big enough for an open hand, but not for a **fist.** With the banana in its grip, the
monkey is stuck.

Now watch. There's no net. Nothing holding that monkey but its own hand. It could open
its fingers and walk free any second. But it won't. It pulls, it screams, it fights —
and it will sit there until the trappers come, because the one thing it will not do…
is **let go.**"

### 3 · TURN
"Now, we're all smarter than a monkey. Type **smarter than a monkey.**

But how many of you are holding onto something with a death grip — some money, an old
way of doing things, a comfort — so scared to release it that it's got you trapped in
the exact spot you want out of?

Here's what the monkey can't see. There are infinite bananas in the jungle. It's
squeezing one, and the one it's squeezing is the only thing standing between it and all
the rest."

### 4 · TARGET
Breaks the money-clinging objection at the point of decision. It reframes the price as a
grip they're refusing to open — and the thing they're gripping as the very reason
they're stuck. Makes releasing the money feel like freedom, not loss.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"Release the grip."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So the choice is simple. Keep squeezing the banana you've got — or open your hand and
reach for the whole jungle. Let me show you what's waiting when you let go."

---

### Placement notes
A money/price-objection bit for the close — pairs with any of the three closes at the
price-reveal moment. High-participation; lean into the call-and-response. Keep the
Pirsig credit in so it's clearly a retold parable, not lifted verbatim.

## The Crab Bucket

- **Placement:** Re-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Execution/Re-Pitch · 4 WHEN
- **Job:** objection-break (external / social)
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "I need to check with the people around me / what will they think?" (The people closest to them talk them out of the leap.)
- **Soundbyte:** "Don't let the crabs in the bucket set your ceiling."
- **Source:** Adapted from the widely-known "crab mentality" / crabs-in-a-bucket folk metaphor (public domain). Retold original.
- **Runtime:** ~2–3 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"How many of you have gotten excited about something — a big move, an investment in
yourself — and the first person you told talked you right out of it? Type **yes.**"

### 2 · STORY
"Let me tell you about crabs. If you catch one crab and put it in a bucket, you have to
put a lid on it — because one crab will climb right out.

But if you put a bunch of crabs in the bucket? No lid needed. Everybody say **no lid.**

Why? Because the second one crab starts climbing toward the top — toward freedom — the
others reach up and pull it back down. Every time. Not one of them gets out. They'd all
rather stay in the bucket together than watch one of them escape."

### 3 · TURN
"How many of you have crabs in your bucket?

The people who mean well. Who love you. But every time you reach for something bigger,
they say *who do you think you are? That's risky. That's not for people like us. Stay
down here where it's safe with the rest of us.*

They're not evil. They're just reporting the view from inside the bucket. But if you let
people at the bottom set your ceiling, you'll never climb out."

### 4 · TARGET
Breaks the external / social-approval objection ("I have to run it by my spouse, my
friends, my circle") at the close. It gives them permission to stop letting other
people's fear dictate their decision — without insulting the people they love.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"Don't let the crabs in the bucket set your ceiling."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So here's the real question — are you deciding this, or are you letting the bucket
decide it for you? Let me show you what climbing out actually looks like."

---

### Placement notes
The go-to bit for the "what will people think / I need to ask someone" objection.
Runs late, near the close. Keep the tone compassionate — the crabs are loved ones, not
enemies; the point is *their view isn't your ceiling,* not *cut everyone off.*

## The Real Price

- **Placement:** Re-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Execution/Re-Pitch · 4 WHEN
- **Job:** close-prime (cost of inaction)
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "This costs too much." (They're weighing the price of the offer while ignoring the far bigger price they're already paying to stay the same.)
- **Soundbyte:** "You're already paying. The only question is for what."
- **Source:** Original
- **Runtime:** ~3 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"Let me ask the question that's in the back of some of your minds right now. *What's this
going to cost me?* Fair question. Type **fair.** So let's actually do the math — the real
math."

### 2 · STORY
"Most people, when they look at an offer, they see one number. The price. And they go,
*ooh, that's a lot.*

But there's a number nobody puts on the table. It's invisible. It doesn't send you a
bill. But you pay it every single year, on time, without fail.

It's the cost of staying exactly where you are.

Think about it. What did last year cost you? Not in dollars you spent — in the result
you didn't get. The clients you didn't sign. The income you left sitting there. The
year of your life you traded doing it the hard way, alone, guessing.

Put a number on that. Go on — what's it actually worth? That number is enormous. And
here's the brutal part: you already paid it. Last year. And you'll pay it again this
year. And next year. Quietly. Automatically. For as long as nothing changes."

### 3 · TURN
"So the question was never *can I afford this?* You've been affording the expensive
option this whole time — the do-nothing option.

The real question is: are you willing to pay that invisible bill one more year? Or are
you ready to trade it for a number that's a fraction of the size and actually gets you
out?"

### 4 · TARGET
Reframes price as a comparison — not "spend vs. save," but "small known cost vs. huge
recurring hidden cost." It makes the cost of inaction the biggest number in the room, so
the offer's price feels like the cheap option. A close-primer for the price reveal.

### 5 · SOUNDBYTE
"You're already paying. The only question is for what."

### 6 · TRANSITION
"So let's stop paying for standing still. Here's exactly what it looks like to switch
which bill you're paying — starting today."

---

### Placement notes
A close-primer for the price-reveal moment — pairs with every close. Run it right before
you name the price. Do not invent specific dollar figures for the "invisible bill" — make
the attendee name their own; that's what makes it land and keeps it honest.

## The Time Bank

- **Placement:** Re-Pitch
- **SCORE™ act / phase:** Execution/Re-Pitch · 4 WHEN
- **Job:** objection-break (time)
- **Target (the belief/objection it breaks):** "I don't have time for this right now." (Waiting for a free window that never comes — while the days burn either way.)
- **Soundbyte:** "You're spending the day no matter what. Spend it on purpose."
- **Source:** Adapted from the widely-circulated "86,400 seconds / bank of time" parable (public, loosely attributed to Marc Levy). Retold original.
- **Runtime:** ~2–3 min

---

### 1 · HOOK
"How many of you want this, you believe it works, but the thing in your head is *I just
don't have the time right now*? Be honest. Type **that's me.**"

### 2 · STORY
"Okay. Imagine a bank. And every single morning, this bank drops **86,400** into your
account. Everybody say **86,400.**

But there are rules. You can't save it. You can't roll it over to tomorrow. Whatever you
don't spend by midnight… it's gone. Burned. And the next morning, fresh again — 86,400.

Now — what would you do? Type it. You'd spend every last bit of it, right? You'd never
let a dollar burn.

Here's the thing. You have that account. It's real. It's not dollars — it's the 86,400
**seconds** in every day. Deposited each morning. Gone each night. No savings, no
rollover, no refunds.

You're not deciding *whether* to spend today's 86,400. That's already spent by
midnight, guaranteed. You're only deciding **what you spent it on.**"

### 3 · TURN
"How many of you keep spending today's seconds on the exact same day you had last year —
telling yourself you'll start when there's more time?

There is never going to be more time. There's just the same 86,400, over and over, spent
on the old life or the new one. 'I don't have time' really means 'I'm choosing to spend
it on something else.'"

### 4 · TARGET
