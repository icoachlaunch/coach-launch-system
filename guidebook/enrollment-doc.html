01

Your Offer, Written Out

Your Red Diamond Offer™ is the one document that sells your complete high-ticket offer for you.

We call it your Enrollment DocThe long-form offer: a sales letter that carries your whole offer, stacks the value and makes the next step obvious.. It's a sales letter that works around the clock, without you in the room.

You've already built the model and written the words. What you don't have yet is the one document that packages your whole offer.

Think of where it gets used. You drop it in the chat at your live event. You email it to the people who didn't buy in the room. You send it to anyone who says "let me think about it."

It keeps working after you've logged off. That's exactly what a real offer document is supposed to do.

7

The 7X Red Diamonds. Seven elements that make a high-ticket offer so desirable the price feels small. Stack them honestly and the yes gets easy.

What it is not

It's not a piece of content. You don't post it. You send it to warm people who are close to a yes.

It's not a lead magnet. The free thing that fills your events is the Event Magnet™, a separate Step 3 asset with a different job.

It's not for one module or one step. It always carries your complete offer, the whole journey from where they are to where they want to be.

And it doesn't replace your event. It backs up the offer you make live.

The principle

The value is built in, not tacked on. Build an offer worth far more than you charge, and the letter only has to show it.

04

The 7X Red Diamonds

This is the heart of a high-ticket offer. It answers the question every buyer is really asking: why is it worth this much?

You don't need all seven. But every one you add honestly raises the value and makes the price feel smaller.

1

OpportunityA door they can't open alone

Access to something unique they couldn't get on their own: a high-value person to meet, a podcast spot, a list of contacts.

You're opening a door, not just teaching a skill.

2

ProximityCloseness to you and the room

Closeness to you, and to a group of people who've already done it.

The room they get into is often worth more than the training.

3

ExclusivityNot for everyone

Selective access, like a mastermind or a VIP networking group.

Being hard to get into is valuable all by itself.

4

Expert AccessWho they get to talk to

Direct access to you, your team and any experts you bring in, like partners or consultants who add to the results.

5

Turn-KeyPlug in, don't figure it out

Done-for-you and done-with-you systems and templates.

You're selling the shortcut, not the homework.

6

ExperienceSomething they feel

Delivered as an experience: events, community, retreats, a VIP dinner.

People pay more to feel something, not just learn something.

7

AccountabilityStructure that gets it done

Structure that helps them actually do the work, and get there faster.

The result they came for depends on this as much as on the information.

✕ Not that

"Community and support." That's a shrug.

✓ Do this

"A monthly mastermind call capped at 12 founders, plus a private group where I answer questions daily." That's a reason to buy.

The trap: stacking a Red Diamond you can't really deliver. A promised retreat that doesn't exist gets found out on day one. If you want one you don't have yet, build toward it. Don't write it as if it's live.

05

Your Levels, Your Stack, Your Bonus

Once the reader wants the offer, these blocks make the price feel like a bargain.

The 3 Levels

Your offer has three ways to buy it, each a higher price than the last. Each level has a fixed form. You name it and you price it.

Level 1Confidence

Always a membership: your trainings, your system and your community, paid monthly or yearly. Both are always shown. Usually $5K+ a year.

Level 2Acceleration

Done-for-you, or a 3-day accelerator where they build it with you. Results faster. Usually $10K–$15K+.

Level 3Proximity

1-on-1, a retreat or a mastermind. Direct access to you, the top tier. $20K+, and as much as $100K.

The yearly membership is priced at a saving on twelve months: 25% is the standard, or the saving you set in Offer Pricing.

The levels print as the very last block of the letter, after the P.S. The body sells the one core offer your Money Model priced. The ladder shows the range.

In the letter, your reader sees your names for the levels, never "Confidence," "Acceleration" or "Proximity." Those labels are for you. Only have one level today? Name the ladder anyway and mark the rest as where you're headed.

The principle

Proximity shows up twice on purpose. Access to you adds value at any level, and at the top level, access to you IS the offer.

The Value Stack

The Value StackEvery deliverable in the offer, each with a real dollar value, totalled next to the price so the gap is impossible to miss. lists everything they get, each with a real value, then the total, then the price.

Your whole Magic Formula™ system is one line. Each real Red Diamond is a line. The Fast Acting Bonus is a line. Then the bridge: "All of that, today, for [price]."

✕ Inflated

"My whole system, worth $60,000+, plus coaching, plus tons of bonuses!" One fake number taints every honest one.

✓ Real values

Each piece at what you'd charge for it alone, or what the alternative actually costs. A believable gap sells.

The Fast Acting Bonus

A real bonus for acting now, with a real deadline: join by a date, be one of the first few, decide by the end of the event.

It gives the fence-sitter a reason to move today instead of "someday." The deadline has to be true. A fake one is the fastest way to lose a smart buyer.

06

The Blocks That Close It

Because your Enrollment Doc is a long letter, it adds the blocks that turn wanting into doing. They all sit in the Execution act, around your call to action.

BlockWhat it does
Fork in the RoadSays who this is NOT for, then who it's a great fit for. It filters out bad-fit buyers and makes good-fit ones lean in.
Confidence Filter"If you aren't confident this will produce at least [X] return, don't sign up." It puts the only variable on the reader's commitment.
Objection CrusherJustifies the price: the cost per day, and what they've already spent on things that didn't work.
Call to actionThe one action, built to the close you locked in your Money Model, with your exact price and a link placeholder.
P.S. / P.P.S.The P.S. restates the whole offer in short. The P.P.S. gives fence-sitters a way to reach you. The P.S. is the second-most-read part of any letter.

The Risk Reversal (your guarantee) and any scarcity go in only if they're true. If you have none, they're left out, never made up.

The principle

Telling the wrong people not to buy builds trust. The reader has braced for a hard sell the whole way, so willingly turning some away breaks the pattern right when they're deciding.

Your call to action follows your close

The call to action isn't one-size-fits-all. It's built to the close you locked in your Money Model. The Builder works with the Four Closes, and never invents another.

CloseWhat the call to action does
The ASK™ CloseThey apply for the free application call: application form + calendar, and the sale happens on the call. The call to action is "Apply now."
The JOIN™ CloseThey join your membership from the room, with monthly and yearly shown side by side, then an enrollment call and the upsell from there.
The PASS™ CloseThey book the paid session, a Paid Audit or Strategy Session, and pay on the spot. The upsell or downsell comes from the session.
The SEAL™ CloseFull offer → non-refundable deposit → onboarding call → balance paid before it starts. $997 is the recommended deposit default, and you can set your own.

When the sale happens before any call, as with JOIN™ and SEAL™, there's no conversation to handle the objections. So your Red Diamonds, your Value Stack, your guarantee and your FAQ do that job.

07

What You Get, and How to Use It

The Builder hands you the letter. That's it.

Six lines of file details at the top, then your headline, the letter in the SCORE™ order, your P.S. and P.P.S., and your 3 Levels last. Everything below those six lines goes onto a page exactly as it stands.

Expect roughly 2,500–4,000 words once every block is in. Well short of that, a block is missing. Far past it, you're teaching instead of selling.

The letter carries none of our names

No act labels, no "Value Stack," no "Red Diamond" anywhere in it. Just your words, your system's name and your level names.

A reader who sees "Serendipity" is reading a template, and the spell breaks. You need the map. Your prospect needs the letter.

The build notes come to you in the chat instead: every flag to clear, every link to swap, and which Red Diamonds are live. Clear them before the letter leaves your hands.

Then package it

The .md file is your master copy. The Builder then asks how you want to share it: a PDF for the event chat and email, a Word file, a Google Doc, or a single web page.

Give it your BRAND KIT block and the export uses your fonts and brand colour. When the letter changes, change the .md and export again. Never edit an export.

Present live, then drop the doc

This is where most people leave money on the table. Your live offer catches the decisive buyers. Your Enrollment Doc catches everyone else.

  1. Make your offer at the event. Stack the value, show the price, handle objections, give the next step.
  2. Drop the doc. "I put my whole offer into one document. Link's in the chat."
  3. Tell them what to do with it. "Type 'got it' to confirm you have it. Read it carefully."
  4. Let it work after. Everyone who didn't buy in the room now has what they need to decide on their own time.
What breaks if you get this wrong

Hide the price and send people to "go read the doc," and your event loses its climax. The people who'd have bought on the spot never get the chance.

Even with The SEAL™ Close, where the deposit comes before any call, the doc runs alongside a real event, never instead of one.

09

The Hard Rules and the Gate

These keep your Enrollment Doc honest, complete and ready to use. The Builder holds you to every one.

  1. Follow the SCORE™ arc. S, C, O, R, E, in order. The offer lands only after trust and desire.
  2. Arrange, don't rewrite. Your SCORE™ Card blocks go in word for word.
  3. Only stack what's real. Fake value is worse than less value.
  4. The complete offer, always. Never a single module, never the Event Magnet™.
  5. Your prices stay your prices. No "improving" $5,000 into $4,997.
  6. Real scarcity only. Genuine limits and real deadlines, or none at all.
  7. Present live, back it up with the doc. The event makes the offer. The doc catches the rest.
  8. Your numbers, not Matthew's. Your doc uses your own real figures.

Why it isn't scored

Your SCORE™ Card was already checked, and your Money Model already passed its profit check. So this is a gate, not a score out of 25.

First, the completion gate: the Builder won't build the letter until every required block exists and you've approved it. A letter with the story but no offer layer looks finished and sells poorly.

Then the Ready Check, section by section. Every line has to pass.

7X Red DiamondsOnly the real ones, each with a specific detail you can deliver
3 LevelsIn their fixed forms, your names and real prices, lowest to highest, printed last
Value Stack and bonusReal values, the whole system as one line, a genuine bonus deadline

Check Your Work

Almost every weak Enrollment Doc fails in one of these six ways. Check yours against each one.

✕ A thin offer with no Red Diamonds

"My program plus some calls." Information is cheap, and the buyer knows it.

✓ Real value stacked in

Add the Red Diamonds you can truly deliver: access, experience, accountability.

✕ The offer before the arc

Opening with the price and the deliverables reads as a pitch.

✓ The SCORE™ order kept

Problems, story and system first. The price comes after the reader wants it.

✕ Faked value

A promised retreat that isn't real, or a "$97,000 value."

✓ Only what you can deliver

A smaller honest offer outsells a big fake one.

✕ A buried price

The reader has to hunt for it, or "book a call to find out."

✓ The price stated plainly

Build the Value Stack, then state the price clearly right after it.

✕ Trying to close everyone

No Fork in the Road. It chases anyone with a pulse.

✓ Strong filters

Say who it's not for. Better clients, fewer refunds.

✕ The doc replacing the live offer

"I won't cover pricing today, it's all in the doc."

✓ Present live, then drop the doc

The doc backs up your event. It never stands in for it.
