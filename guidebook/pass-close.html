01

When You Use It

The PASS™ Close is the close for Phase 3, Growth. It sells your Accelerator Offer, at $10K–$15K+, through a paid session.

PASS™ stands for Paid Audit or Strategy Session. It's a name, not a set of letter steps. An Audit finds the gaps. A Strategy session delivers an outcome. You choose which one you run, and either way you upsell or downsell from it.

This is the phase where you pitch from the stage. Your event is a single day of 180–240 minutes, every fortnight, and paid entry becomes the default.

It's the right close when your offer costs more than a stranger will commit to without talking to you first. That's not weakness. It's the honest shape of a bigger decision.

Signs you're ready

  • You've graduated Phase 2: across one 28-day cycle, 10% or more of your live attendees JOIN™ from the room.
  • Your Accelerator Offer is built and priced in your Money Model.
  • You've run enough calls that you're not nervous, and enough of them converted that you believe a session is worth paying for.
  • You stop hoping they say yes, and genuinely don't mind either way.

Signs you're not

  • Your JOIN™ rate hasn't held at 10% across a full cycle yet.
  • You'd feel the need to apologise for the fee.
  • Your Magic Formula™ isn't locked, so there's nothing to audit against.

What stays available

Closes from earlier phases don't go away. The ASK™ Close, the free application call, is still there. So is The JOIN™ Close, which sells your Membership Offer from the room. The PASS™ Close is the one you add.

A free call is not a PASS™. A free call is The ASK™ Close. The PASS™ is always paid.

02

Why It Works

The closes differ by where the conversation sits next to the sale. With The PASS™ Close, it sits before the sale. You talk in the session, then they buy. It's the only close where a paid conversation comes first.

A room can't say yes to a five-figure decision on the spot. Make that pitch and ask them to buy, and they go quiet. The PASS™ gives them a smaller yes that leads to the bigger one.

Here's the shift. You're not booking a sales call. You're booking a diagnosis. They sit with you for an hour, you score their business against your Magic Formula™, and they leave with a written plan.

The law it rides

The PASS™ Close rides the second law: people who pay, pay attention. A fee filters. People who pay show up, arrive ready, and take the hour seriously. People who don't pay treat it as optional.

The first law, Immersion = Conversion, does the work before it. A 180–240 minute event gives the room long enough with you to want the next hour.

The principle

You're not selling coaching. You're delivering a diagnosis. Enrolment is what happens when the diagnosis is thorough and the expert is obviously credible.

04

The Ask

Your Sniper Presentation™ runs its course. You've made the offer, walked the value stack, anchored and revealed the price. Now comes the ask.

With The PASS™ Close, the ask is not "buy this." It's "book a session with me." It has four beats.

  1. Name the session. Nobody wants "a call." Give the session a real name that says what they get. Use the shape [Timeline] + [Outcome] + [Session Type]: "90-Day Client Flow Mapping Session," or "The Studio Owner Growth Audit." A named session sounds like a deliverable.
  2. State the fee, and where it goes. Say the number plainly. Then take the sting out straight away: it comes off the first payment if you work together.
  3. Make the promise that removes the risk. They leave with a written plan whether they hire you or not. This is the beat most people miss, and it's what makes a paid session make sense.
  4. Give the steps, then stop talking. Numbered, on screen, simple. Click the link, pick a time, pay, done. Don't fill the silence, re-explain or apologise for the fee. The pause is doing work.
How it sounds (illustrative)

"I run a 90-Day Client Flow Mapping Session. It's [your fee], it takes about an hour, and we go through your business step by step against the exact system I just showed you. You'll leave with a scored assessment and a written plan. That's yours whether you work with me or not. And if we work together, the [fee] comes straight off your first payment. Link's on the screen. Pick a time that works."

05

The Call

The PASS™ Session is the call that follows this close. It's about an hour, and it has five beats that always run in this order.

1

The GoalAbout 5 minutes

Three questions. Where do you want to be in 90 days? Where are you now, on a scale of 1 to 10? What are the top two or three things in the way?

Write down their exact words. You'll use them at the end.

2

The Audit15–20 minutes

Nine steps, one question each. Score every step out of 5, for a total out of 45.

Capture only. No coaching, no solving. They'll say something broken and you'll want to fix it. Say "got it," write it down and move to the next step.

The trap: every fix you hand over here is a reason not to hire you.

3

The Gap3–5 minutes

Now you talk. Give the total, say how many areas are solid and how many need attention, and name the phase where the gaps cluster.

Tie it back to what they told you in Beat 1. That's the moment the audit stops being a quiz and becomes a mirror. Finish on hope: these gaps are specific, and they're fixable.

4

The Plan25–35 minutes

This is the part they paid for. For every step scoring 3 or below, give a root cause, a recommendation and one piece of homework they can start on their own. Steps scoring 4 or 5 get one sentence: that's solid, keep doing it.

Direction, not delivery. Tell them where to go. Don't walk them there. The homework is real, doable and deliberately incomplete.

5

The Close3–5 minutes

Two questions and an ask. Don't improvise this part.

"Based on everything we've mapped out, how confident are you that this plan gets you to [their goal] if it's done properly?" They'll say high. You just spent forty minutes proving it.

"And how confident are you that you can do all of this on your own?" They'll say low, because it's a lot and they know it.

Then: "Would you like my help?" Stop talking and let the silence sit. Silence reads as confidence, and you've earned it.

After the ask

If they say yes
Move into onboarding like it was always going to happen. "Great. I'll put the [fee] you paid today toward your first payment, so you're not paying twice. Want to get started?"
If they want to think
Take it gracefully. Send the full report today and follow up in a day or two. Then actually do it. Don't discount and don't push. The report is your follow-up.

How a session total reads, from 40–45 down to below 20, is in The Closes chapter.

Your PASS™ Instrument

You can't audit someone against nothing. You need an instrument: a fixed set of things you check, in the same order, every time. Yours already exists. It's your Magic Formula™, three phases and nine steps.

That's why nobody can copy this session. Anyone can run a generic business audit. Only you can audit someone against your nine steps.

Step Currency
The measure that tells you where they stand on a step. A number, a rate or a confidence level.
Questions
One to three things you'd actually say out loud to get that measure.
Anchors
What a 5 looks like and what a 1 looks like. The middle takes care of itself.

Your Step Currency is the measure for one step. Don't confuse it with your Core Currency, the single outcome your $Million Promise™ delivers.

Common Mistakes

Most PASS™ Closes that fail, fail in one of these ways.

✕ Coaching during the audit

They describe something broken and you fix it right there. It feels generous. It's also why they don't hire you.

✓ Capture only

Say "got it," write it down, move on. Every time. Save it all for The Plan.

✕ A sales call with an audit stapled on

You rush the nine steps to get to the pitch. They feel it, and the audit becomes a formality.

✓ Give the audit its full time

15–20 minutes, and mean it. If the honest answer is that they don't need you, say so.

✕ Apologising for the fee

"It's only a small amount, just to cover my time…" Now they think it isn't worth it.

✓ Say it like it's normal

The fee, what they get, and that it comes off the first payment. Then move on.

✕ Filling the silence after the ask

Two seconds after "would you like my help?" you're listing bonuses. You've answered your own question, and the answer was no.

✓ Ask, then stop

Close your mouth. Count to ten in your head if you have to. Let them think.

✕ Staying free too long

Free feels safer, so you never switch. Your calendar fills with no-shows and people who were never going to buy.

✓ Charge for the PASS™

Use The ASK™ Close to find your feet, then charge. The fee isn't a barrier. It's the filter that makes this close work.

Is It Ready?

No score here. It's pass or fail: you either have the pieces or you don't.

  • Named. Your session has a real name, not "a call."
  • Priced. The fee comes from your Money Model, and you can say it without flinching.
  • Credited. The fee comes off the first payment, and you say so in the ask.
  • Promised. They leave with a written plan whether they buy or not.
  • Calibrated. All nine Magic Formula™ steps have a measure, questions and anchors.
  • Disciplined. You know the audit is capture-only, and you can hold that line.
  • Scripted. Two questions and the ask, word for word, then silence.
  • Delivered. You have a report you can send within the hour.

All eight? Your PASS™ Close is ready to run.
