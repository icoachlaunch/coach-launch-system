01

Your Look, Decided Once

Your Visual Style Guide is how your brand looks: decided once, held in one place, and read by everything you build after it.

It used to mean a document. A list of colours, a couple of fonts, some rules you hoped you'd remember.

Now it means something more useful: a Brand KitA complete look shipped as a real file: your hero colour, text tones, two fonts, corners, shadows, and a light and a dark ground, all tuned to work together..

Six Kits ship with this tool. You pick one. And because it's a file and not a memo, every builder downstream reads it instead of asking you.

Your Sniper Presentation™ Slides, your Cash Flow Engine™ funnel pages, your Magic Formula™ Visual, your ads and your posts all load the same look from the same place.

1

One Kit. One file. Everywhere. Change it there and everything follows. Change it anywhere else and you've started to drift.

It matters because recognition is built on repetition. When your slides, pages and posts share one colour and one pair of fonts, people learn to spot you. That builds trust, and trust is what makes a stranger comfortable enough to buy.

When the look wanders, you read as smaller, newer and less certain than you are.

What it is not

It's not a logo. A logo is one small piece of it, and a clean wordmark works fine to start.

It's not artwork or a finished website. It's the look those things follow, held in a file a designer, a tool or an AI can read.

It's not six options you keep open, or a theme you pick per project. A deck theme, a funnel theme and a social theme is three brands, not one.

It's not your Brand Voice. That's Foundation 3, how you sound. This is only how you look.

The principle

Consistency stops being something you have to remember and becomes something you can't avoid.

03

How You'll Build It

You build your look in seven moves, each one a decision you lock and reuse forever. The Builder takes you through them one at a time, always tied back to your personality.

Name the feeling, pick your Brand Kit (the big one), confirm your typography, document your logo or wordmark, define your imagery, see the components your Kit already built, and write your consistency rules.

Notice how much of that is confirming rather than deciding. That's deliberate. The Kit carries the design decisions: the colour maths, the font pairing, the corners, the contrast.

One feeling → one Brand Kit → everything you build, automatically.

05

The Seven Moves, In Full

Here's each move: what it is, why it matters, how to choose yours, and the trap that quietly makes brands look messy.

1

Your Look in a WordThe feeling

Before you pick anything, you name the feeling your visuals should give: bold, warm, premium, playful, clean.

Why it matters. The feeling is the filter every other choice passes through, and it points you at the right Kit.

How to choose yours

Pull it straight from your personality. A Challenger feels bold and high-contrast. A Friend feels warm and welcoming. An Expert feels premium and calm. One lead feeling has to drive.

Can't say how it should feel? That's normal. Open the gallery first, pick the Kit that looks like your business, and the feeling will name itself.

✕ Not that

"Premium but playful and minimal but bold." Four feelings fighting. No Kit can satisfy all of them.

✓ Do this

"Warm and welcoming." One clear feeling that matches a Friend personality.

The trap: a feeling that contradicts your personality. If they disagree, one of them is wrong. Fix it here, before it picks the wrong Kit.

2

Your Brand KitThe one choice that spreads

You pick one of the six Kits, and it carries your colours, fonts, buttons and corners into everything you build.

Why it matters. It's the only choice in this guide that reaches everywhere. Get it right and the rest of your look takes care of itself.

How to choose yours

Open the gallery, narrow to the two or three your feeling points at, and pick the one that looks like your business. Then there are two paths.

Path A: take the Kit as it is. Fastest, and guaranteed to hold together. Most people should do this. Never had a brand colour? Then you're on Path A, and that's the best place to start.

Path B: you already have a brand colour. A colour your audience knows you by. Keep it. You pick the closest Kit for its fonts and feel, swap in your own hero colourYour brand's signature colour. It's the accent: buttons, one highlighted word, a badge. The loudest thing you own., and the Builder records the swap.

The contrast check: Path B only, every time

Your colour has to carry the text that sits on it. Golds, oranges, greens and cyans can't carry white text. That's not a judgement, it's physics. So the Builder checks it and sets the text to your dark tone if white would be faint.

✕ Not that

"Sovereign for the slides, Meadow for the funnel." That's two brands. The Builder will refuse it.

✓ Do this

"Sovereign, but with my navy as the hero." One Kit, one deliberate swap, written down.

The trap: asking for a seventh look, or tweaking every value until the Kit is unrecognisable. The flex is your hero colour. Everything else stays as the Kit set it.

3

TypographyConfirm, don't decide

Two fonts, two jobs. A display font for headings that carries personality, and a body font for paragraphs that has one job: be easy to read.

Why it matters. Fonts set tone before a word is read, and more than two is the fastest way to look amateur.

How to choose yours

You mostly don't. Your Kit already picked a pairing, both free on Google Fonts and already loaded. It also sets the size ladder, so a headline on a slide and a headline on your funnel page stay in proportion.

Attached to fonts you already use? You can swap them in and keep everything else. The Builder checks they load on the web first.

✕ Not that

Three fonts, plus a script for paragraphs nobody can read, plus a desktop-only font that won't load on a web page.

✓ Do this

"Happy with the pairing." Locked in five seconds, and it works with your colours.

The trap: swapping fonts because the Kit's fonts feel unfamiliar on day one. Give them a week on real work. Familiarity isn't the same as fit.

4

Logo & WordmarkThe mark and its rules

Your logo, or a clean wordmark (your name set in your Kit's display font), plus the simple rules for using it.

Why it matters. A consistent mark signals you're established. You don't need a designed logo to start.

How to choose yours

Have a logo you like? Document its colours and check it works on your Kit's light and dark grounds. Don't have one? Start with a wordmark.

Either way, set three rules: clear space around it, a minimum size, and a version for light backgrounds and one for dark.

✕ Not that

Waiting months for the "perfect" logo while your brand looks different on every asset.

✓ Do this

A clean wordmark in your Kit's display font, with clear space and light and dark versions.

The trap: stalling the whole brand on a logo. A simple wordmark used everywhere beats a great logo used inconsistently.

5

Imagery & TextureThe feel

The style of your photos and graphics, plus any one simple recurring element: a shape, a line, a grid.

Why it matters. This is the one thing your Kit can't decide for you. Two brands on the same Kit can look nothing alike because of their imagery.

How to choose yours

Match it to your feeling: bright and candid for warm brands, moody and minimal for premium ones, bold and graphic for high-energy ones.

No idea yet? That's fine. Imagery is the one thing you learn by publishing. The Builder marks it to decide after your first ten posts, and that still passes.

✕ Not that

Generic stock handshakes on a Sovereign brand, with five graphic styles competing.

✓ Do this

Bright, real, human photos on a warm Ember brand, plus one signature shape used sparingly.

The trap: imagery that fights the feeling. Cheap stock on a premium Kit quietly undercuts everything the Kit got right.

6

ComponentsAlready built

The small repeating pieces: buttons, headings, cards, callouts, lists, quotes, price blocks. Your Kit built all of them.

Why it matters. Every piece already matches your colours and fonts, so you never style a button again. You just use it.

What ships in every Kit

Solid and outline buttons, headings on your size ladder, a highlighter for one word in a headline, cards and callouts, tick and cross lists, a before/after block, stats, testimonials, price blocks, funnel-page pieces, and a photo layer that keeps text readable over any image.

And two grounds, light and dark. Same blocks, two moods. Your hero colour isn't a third ground. It's the accent, which keeps it the loudest thing you own.

✕ Not that

A new button shape and colour on every page because "this one needed something different."

✓ Do this

The Kit's standard set, used as shipped. One button style on every asset.

The trap: restyling a component on one page. If it genuinely needs to change, it changes in the Kit, for everything, or not at all.

7

Consistency RulesThe guardrails

A short do/don't list that keeps you on brand when you're moving fast.

Why it matters. Rules you can recite are rules you'll actually follow. Five lines beat a fifty-page brand bible nobody opens.

How to choose yours

Start from the standard set and trim to what you'll remember: one hero colour, neutrals do the heavy lifting, two fonts, the logo gets clear space, images match the feeling, and when in doubt, more white space.

✕ Not that

Twenty rules covering every case, which nobody reads twice.

✓ Do this

Five short lines you could say from memory.

The trap: rules so long you never read them again. Short and firm beats thorough and ignored.

06

Your BRAND KIT Block

When the Builder puts your guide together, the most important part isn't the colour table or the font names. It's three short lines at the end.

BRAND KIT: sovereign
FILE: Reference/brand-kit/tokens/sovereign.css
OVERRIDES: none

Figure 06 · A BRAND KIT block. An illustrative example for a business on the Sovereign Kit, as shipped.

That block is what every visual builder reads. When your slides, your funnel pages or your Magic Formula™ Visual need to know what your brand looks like, you give them those three lines and they do the rest.

LineWhat it says
BRAND KIT:Your Kit, one of the six, by its name
FILE:The real file your look lives in, in the Reference folder of your workspace. You save it there yourself, once.
OVERRIDES:Anything you kept from your existing brand. For most people it says none.

If you kept your own hero colour (Path B), the last line lists exactly that and nothing else, like this: OVERRIDES: --brand: [your colour] · --ink-on-brand: [the text colour that passed the check]. Kept your own fonts? They're listed there too.

The Builder writes this line for you. You never type a colour yourself.

Where it lives

The block sits at the end of your Visual Style Guide, saved as visual-style-guide.md in your Reference folder. Your Kit's file sits beside it, in Reference/brand-kit/tokens/, a folder that holds exactly one file.

There is exactly one of these blocks in your business. If you ever find two, one of them is wrong.

Three ways you'll use it

Running a builderPaste the block

When a builder asks what your brand looks like, give it the three lines. You never answer a colour question again.

Building a pageLoad three files

Your Kit, then blocks.css, then the surface for your medium, like surfaces/page.css. The blocks are already your colours.

Changing laterChange one line

Change the line naming your Kit and everything follows. The next section shows how, without ending up with two.

07

Changing Your Kit Later

Your Kit isn't a tattoo. Businesses reposition and rebrands happen. You're allowed to change it. What you're not allowed to do is end up with two.

The changeHow it works
Keeping your own colour or fontsThis is the Path B swap, done inside the Builder. Only the hero colour, its text colour, or the two fonts can change. Never the corners, spacing, shadows or grounds.
Moving to a different KitRe-run the Builder's Kit step and pick the new one. Take the old file out of tokens/, save the new one, and replace the old block everywhere you pasted it. Then rebuild anything you made on the old Kit.
Editing a colour on one pageDon't. That page has left your brand. If a colour needs to change, it changes in the Kit, through the Builder, so it changes everywhere at once.

Rebuilding sounds heavy, and it isn't. A slide deck rebuilt in a new Kit takes minutes, because the words and structure are separate from the look.

One Kit installed at a time

Replace, never add. Your guide carries one BRAND KIT block. Your workspace holds one Kit. Every builder reads the same one.

The moment there are two, you're back to guessing which one is true, and so is every tool you use.

09

The Hard Rules

These keep your look consistent and repeatable. The Builder holds you to every one.

  1. One Kit, everywhere. Not one for slides and another for the funnel.
  2. One Kit installed at a time. When it changes, it's replaced. Never a second block alongside the first.
  3. Never type a colour code outside your Kit. A hand-picked colour on one page is the start of drift.
  4. Six Kits, not seven. You choose from the six. You don't invent one or tweak one until it's unrecognisable.
  5. The flex is your hero colour and your fonts. Corners, spacing, shadows and grounds stay as the Kit set them.
  6. Text on your hero must read. Swap in your own colour and the contrast check runs.
  7. Two fonts, never more. A characterful heading and a readable body.
  8. Pick for the business, not for you. The Kit matches your personality, not your favourite colour.
  9. A mark with rules. Clear space, minimum size, and light and dark versions.
  10. Imagery matches the feeling. No cheap stock on a premium Kit.
  11. When in doubt, white space. More room, not more stuff.
