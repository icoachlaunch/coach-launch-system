01

Your System, As a Picture

The Magic Formula™ Visual is your Magic Formula™, three phases and nine steps, turned into one graphic you can put in front of buyers.

You've already built the system. The Visual makes it something people can see in four seconds.

That matters more than it sounds. A system nobody can picture is a system nobody can repeat back to a friend.

What you get is one HTML file. Double-click it and it opens in any browser, animates once, and sits there looking like you paid a designer for it.

No software to buy. No account to make. No designer to brief.

The principle

A list of nine steps reads like homework. The same nine steps in a graphic read like a method.

Where you'll use it

On the sales page, so people see what they're buying before they read a word.

In the ad, as the image that stops the scroll. In your masterclass deck, on the slide where you reveal the system.

In the proposal, the onboarding pack, the pitch. Anywhere someone needs to understand your method fast.

What it is not

It's not where you build your system. The Magic Formula™ Builder does that. This tool draws a system that's already locked, and it stops if you haven't got one.

It's not a new version of your system. Every word on the graphic comes from your locked Magic Formula™. Nothing gets rewritten or "improved."

It's not a Coach Launch-branded asset. It carries your colour, your fonts, your mark and your words. Our name appears nowhere on it.

And it's not a slide deck. It's one graphic. Your deck comes later, with the Sniper Presentation™ Slides.

The Three Styles

Same system, same words, three different looks. You pick one, and you can rebuild in another any time without redoing the interview.

Want to see them with real content in them? Open the style gallery ↗

1

BlueprintPrecise and technical

An engineering drawing. Fine grid, hairline boxes, technical labels, and a title block in the corner like a real architect's sheet.

The signature. A dimension line runs across the top carrying your promise timeline, like "14 days, start to finish." It's the one style where your timeline is drawn as a measurement.

✕ Think twice if

Your brand is warm, playful or emotional. Blueprint will make it feel clinical.

✓ Pick it for

Work where being exact is the selling point. Proposals, PDFs, printed handouts.

2

MarqueeLoud and high contrast

A broadcast title card. Your system name set huge in capitals, solid colour bars for each phase, and the steps on bright cards underneath.

The signature. On load, a bar of your colour sweeps across the frame and each phase wipes in behind it. It's built to be watched, not just read.

✕ Think twice if

You sell quiet, expensive and understated. Marquee is the loudest of the three by a distance.

✓ Pick it for

Stopping a scroll. Ads, social posts, video thumbnails, the top of a registration page.

3

MonolithQuiet and premium

Three slabs standing side by side, each with a coloured seam down its edge, on a dark ground or a soft light one.

The signature. A line runs under all three slabs, and your $Million Promise™ sits beneath it. The three phases literally stand on the promise.

✕ Think twice if

You need maximum impact in a crowded feed. Monolith is calm on purpose.

✓ Pick it for

High-ticket work where the price does the talking. Sales pages, pitch decks, your about page.

Not sure? Start with Monolith

It's the most forgiving of the three. It works for almost any niche, and you can rebuild in another style later in one sentence.

Light or Dark?

Every style comes in both, so that's six looks, not three. The style decides the shape. The theme decides the room it stands in.

Both grounds come straight from your Brand Kit, so a light graphic and your light pages are the same white.

ThemePick it whenWhy
DarkIt's going on a screen: an ad, a social post, a landing page, a dark deckIt holds attention, and hides a busy background inside someone else's page
LightIt's going on paper, into a document, or onto a white pageA dark graphic on a white sales page fights the page. A light one sits in it
Print

The printing test. If it's going to be printed, pick light. A dark graphic eats ink, and most printers make the background muddy rather than black.

You can have both. Ask for the other theme at the end and keep two files: the dark one for ads, the light one for documents. Same answers, one word changed.

07

The Five Things You Decide

Most of the graphic comes straight from your Magic Formula™. These five are the choices you make in the build, and each one changes how the finished file works.

1

The short display linesWhat people actually read

Your step outcomes were written for a document, where a full sentence is right. On the graphic, each one gets a box about 60 characters wide. That's roughly eight words.

Why it matters. The graphic is a fixed size. Text that overruns its box doesn't wrap. It gets cut off, with no warning.

How to get it right

The Builder shortens each line and shows you all nine beside your originals. It won't build until you've signed off, and it never trims anything quietly.

Keep the noun and the verb. Cut the articles, the qualifiers and the setup. Then ask: could someone read it in one glance and know what they get?

✕ Too long

"Know exactly what to do at every night waking, no guessing, no half-asleep forum scrolling."

✓ Right

"Know what to do at every night waking." Same promise, half the words.

The trap: cutting so hard it goes vague. "Better sleep for everyone" is short and says nothing. Short and specific is the goal.

2

Your system markTop left, for the method

The mark at the top left stands for the system, not your business. Your business name already sits in the corner.

Why it matters. Plenty of coaches have both: a company logo and a small mark for their signature method. If you only have one, use the one that suits the system.

How to get it right

SVG is best, because it stays sharp at any size. PNG is fine. Wide logos and square marks both fit.

Haven't got one? Say so. The Builder makes you a simple monogram from your system's initials, in your brand colour.

✕ Not that

Leaving the slot empty while you hunt for the perfect logo.

✓ Do this

Take the monogram now. You can swap in a real mark later.

The trap: putting your company logo in the system slot, so your business name shows up twice and the method gets no mark of its own.

3

Your step iconsNine, all the same style

Every step gets a small icon to its left. They give the eye somewhere to land, and they make nine text rows feel like nine distinct things.

Three ways to get them

Your own. Already use an icon set? Send the files. SVG preferred, PNG fine.

An icon service. Plug in your own account key for a service like Streamline. The Builder searches it and shows you what it found first.

The built-in set. Plain line icons that sit quietly next to any brand. Nothing to buy. This is the default.

✕ Not that

Mixing line icons with solid ones because each one looked good on its own.

✓ Do this

Check the list of nine, swap any that miss, and keep them all one style.

The trap: mixed icon styles. It's the fastest way to make a good graphic look cheap, and the Builder won't do it.

4

The hot stepExactly one

Your Magic Formula™ flagged one hot stepThe hottest ingredient in your system. It creates the "I need that" moment, and it seeds your Event Magnet™.. On the graphic it's marked, so the eye goes there first.

Why it matters. When someone looks at your system, you want them landing on the piece that makes them think "I need that." That's the step that later becomes your Event Magnet™.

How to get it right

The Builder takes it straight from your Magic Formula™. If it isn't flagged there, it asks. It never picks for you.

✕ Not that

Two or three steps marked, because you're proud of all nine.

✓ Do this

One step: the one that gets the fastest "I need that" from a stranger.

The trap: highlight everything and you've highlighted nothing. The eye has nowhere to land.

5

The buttonBottom right, and live

Every style has a live button. Click it and it goes wherever you point it. That's what turns a diagram into an asset that does a job.

You give it three things

The line: a short nudge above the button, like "Ready for full nights?" The label: what the button says, under about 30 characters. The link: a booking calendar, a checkout, an application, a waitlist.

✕ Not that

"Learn more." It doesn't say what happens next.

✓ Do this

"Book a free sleep call." It names the thing they're about to get.

The trap: a placeholder link that ships. A dead button on a sales page looks broken. If you don't have a destination yet, point it at your main booking page.

The Hard Rules

These keep the graphic true to your system. The Builder holds you to every one.

  1. Your Magic Formula™ is locked first. This tool draws a system. It doesn't write one. If it isn't built, the Builder stops and sends you back.
  2. Three phases, nine steps. Always. A different count isn't padded or trimmed here. It gets fixed in the Magic Formula™ Builder.
  3. Your words go on unchanged. Phase names and step names are never rewritten. Only the outcome lines get shortened, and only with your approval.
  4. Exactly one hot step. Not two, not none.
  5. Your Brand Kit, never a new colour. The colour, fonts, corners and shadows all come from your Kit.
  6. Nothing external. The file carries its own icons and code, so it works offline and inside anyone else's page.
  7. ™ is yours to claim. The Builder only puts ™ on your system or step names if you already use it.

Check Your Work

Most weak graphics fail in one of these five ways. Check yours against each one.

✕ Fixing the system mid-build

You spot a weak step and start rewriting it here. Now the graphic doesn't match your locked Magic Formula™.

✓ Note it, finish, go back

Fix it in the Magic Formula™ Builder, then rebuild the Visual. That takes minutes.

✕ Waving through the short lines

"Yep, fine," without reading them. These nine lines are what everyone actually reads.

✓ Read all nine out loud

If one doesn't sound like you, change it there and then.

✕ The style you like, not the style for the job

A loud dark graphic in a printed proposal, or a quiet one buried in a busy feed.

✓ Decide where it's going first

Feed and ads, Marquee. Print and proposals, Blueprint. Sales pages and decks, Monolith.

✕ More than one hot step

Every step feels important, so three get marked.

✓ One step

The one that creates the fastest "I need that" in a stranger.

✕ A button pointing nowhere

A placeholder link ships, and every click the graphic earns is lost.

✓ Decide the destination first

No destination yet? Point it at your main booking page and update it later.

Is it ready?

  • Three phases and nine steps, all present.
  • Every phase name and step name matches your Magic Formula™ word for word.
  • All nine display lines read cleanly, and you approved each one.
  • Exactly one step is marked as the hot step, and it's the right one.
  • Nine icons, all the same style, one per step.
  • Your system mark is top left and looks sharp.
  • Your Brand Kit is used throughout, and the text sitting on your colour is readable.
  • The theme suits where it's going: light for print, dark for screens and ads.
  • Your business name is in the corner, spelled right.
  • The button says what happens next, and the link works when you click it.
  • No leftover placeholder text, and it opens by double-clicking with no internet connection.

The last check: show it to someone who doesn't know your business and ask what you do. If they can tell you back in one sentence, it's working.
