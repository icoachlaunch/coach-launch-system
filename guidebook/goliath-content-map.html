01

Your Whole Content Plan, Already Titled

The Goliath Content™ Map is your complete content plan: every video, email and hook you could make, already titled and organised step by step.

It's built from two things you've already locked: your SCORE™ Card and your Magic Formula™.

Your SCORE™ Card holds five acts of proven copy. Your Magic Formula™ holds nine steps worth teaching. The map puts them side by side.

9 steps + the whole system × 5 SCORE™ acts × 3 hooks per act = 210 working titles

Figure 01 · The map's maths. Plus 21 more titles for every Event Magnet™ you've built.

Every step can be taught through every act. And every act can be opened with three different hooks.

210

Enough for a year and a half to two years. At 2–3 pieces a week, 210 titles last 70 to 105 weeks, before you add a single Event Magnet™ section.

Why it matters

Most coaches don't struggle with content because they're lazy. They struggle because nobody gave them a system.

So they lose half an hour deciding what to make, go quiet when life gets busy, or ask an AI for ideas and get "5 Tips to Unlock Your Potential."

When the ideas are already mapped, a busy week doesn't break the chain. You just pick the next row.

What it is not

It's not the content itself. The map gives you titles, not scripts or emails.

It's not a posting calendar. It tells you what to make, not when or where it goes out.

And it's not a new message. If a title doesn't sound like your SCORE™ Card, it doesn't belong on the map.

The principle

Same message. Different front doors. You don't have a content problem, you have a map problem.

04

Step Promises and Struggles

Your $Million Promise™ covers the whole transformation. That's perfect for Section 1, but it's too big for a video about Step 4.

So every step gets two things of its own. They're the raw material for every title in its section.

1

The Step PromiseThe pitch for one step

A step promiseWhat one step gets them, how much or how fast, and the one pitfall it saves them from. Your $Million Promise™, scoped down to one step. says what this step gets them, with a number or a timeframe, and the one pitfall it saves them from.

Why it matters. Every title in a section is built on its step promise. A vague one means 21 vague titles.

How to find yours

Start with the step's outcome in your Magic Formula™. Then ask how they'll know it worked, and what one mistake this step stops them making.

There's no audience callout. By the time someone's watching content about Step 4, they already know it's for them.

✕ Not that

"Get organised and become more productive."

✓ Do this

"Plan your whole week in 30 minutes on a Sunday, without a to-do list that's longer by Tuesday." (Illustrative.)

The trap: rewording your big promise nine times. Each step promise is about what this step delivers, and no two steps share a pitfall.

2

The Step StruggleThe friction they hit on their own

A step struggleThe friction your avatar hits when they try one step on their own. The thing that happened to them last Tuesday. isn't a big-picture pitfall. It's the moment that happened to them last Tuesday. You need one to three per step.

Why it matters. Step struggles power your Connection Stones and a third of your hooks. They're how your content makes someone feel seen.

How to find yours

Look in your Golden Avatar™: their pains, their limiting beliefs and the exact phrases they use. Then bring it down to one step.

What do they try? What goes wrong? What do they say to themselves when it does?

✕ Not that

"They struggle with consistency." A category.

✓ Do this

"They batch a month of posts on Sunday, then run out by Wednesday." An experience.

The trap: writing it in your words. They don't say "I lack a content strategy." They say "I never know what to post."

07

The Title Rules

Every title has to be market-ready: good enough to use as a video title, a subject line or a hook without another edit. The Builder checks each one against these seven rules before you see it.

  1. Specific beats general, and specific means true. Your real numbers, step names and avatar's phrases. No confirmed number? A concrete detail, never an invented stat.
  2. Open a gap. Hint at the answer. Don't hand it over in the title.
  3. Their words, not yours. "Stop chasing clients in your DMs" beats "Optimise your client acquisition strategy."
  4. One idea per title. The act or the hook picks the angle. Never cram two.
  5. Break the pattern. If any AI could have written it, rewrite it.
  6. Short enough to text. Aim for 6 to 12 words.
  7. Match the act or the hook. A Myth Hook debunks. A Proof Hook proves.

Watch for the tells of AI writing: "How to" three times in a row, words like unlock, master and elevate, and vague benefits like "next level."

And run the niche-swap test. If you could swap in a different audience and the title still works, it's too generic.

09

The Hard Rules

The Builder holds you to every one of these.

  1. Built from what's locked. Every title comes from your $Million Promise™, Golden Avatar™, Magic Formula™ and SCORE™ Card. Nothing is invented to fill a gap.
  2. No number you can't back up. A concrete detail or a flag instead.
  3. Your words stay your words. Your system name, step names and promise are used exactly as you locked them.
  4. Titles, not content. The map plans. It never writes the scripts or emails.
  5. Every piece matches its act. Five different openings, not one idea said five times.
  6. Every Event Magnet™ gets its own section. Every title in it sends people to the tool.
  7. Your numbers, not Matthew's. Matthew White's $24.6M and $5.2M are his credentials. They never go in your titles.

The map isn't scored out of 25. The assets it's built from were already scored and locked, so every title passes a check instead, and the whole map passes a Ready Check before you get the file.
