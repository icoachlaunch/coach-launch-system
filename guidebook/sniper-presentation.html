01

Your Live Event, Written Down

The Sniper Presentation™ is your live event: the masterclass where you deliver, move people and make your offer.

It ends with the Sniper Close™, the closing sequence that turns an audience into clients.

Here's the part that changes everything. You've already written this. Your SCORE™ Card is the master copy. The Red Diamond Offer™ turned it into a written sales letter. The Sniper Presentation™ turns the same card into a spoken pitch for a live room.

So the Builder isn't writing your message. It's arranging it, and adding the live layer:

  • Engagement. A room that answers, types and raises hands.
  • Story bits. Timeless stories that raise emotion at the right moment.
  • The close. The exact beats that ask for the sale without being weird.

What it is not

It's not a lecture. A lecture teaches everything and sells nothing.

It's not slides droning at a silent audience. It's a live event where the room is part of the show.

It's not a pitch bolted onto the end. By the time you make the offer, it's the obvious next step, not a surprise.

The principle

The whole presentation is the pitch. Every act sets up the close.

If you're at Growth or Mastery · the VIP Hour

A VIP HourA bonus session just for VIP ticket holders, where you over-deliver, build rapport and reveal a big secret. Growth and Mastery only. is what makes the VIP worth paying for. The VIP itself was built in your Cash Flow Max™.

At the start is the fuller experience, when you expect a solid VIP turnout. At the end is the smart fallback when live attendance might be thin.

Either way, you never make the offer in the VIP Hour. The close happens in Phases 3 and 4, with the whole room.

Then · the Material Check

Before it writes a single beat, the Builder checks your material against six things a room has to believe before anyone buys:

  1. This is for me.
  2. What I'm doing now won't work.
  3. You get my problem better than I do.
  4. There's a real reason I'm stuck, and it isn't me.
  5. Your system will work for someone like me.
  6. I need to do this now, not someday.

If any of the six is thin, you shore it up first. It's far easier than fixing thirty beats later.

05

The Four Phases, Beat by Beat

This is the part to read slowly. Here are the beats that decide the outcome: what each one does and the trap that ruins it.

1

Phase 1 · WHOOpen Loop · Serendipity + Connection

By the end of this phase the room should think: this person gets me, and I need to hear the rest.

The hook and your $Million Promise™

Open with a question they answer with a hand or a YES, then land your $Million Promise™ word for word. You have about ninety seconds before they decide whether to give you the hour.

✕ Not that

"Hi everyone, before we start, a few quick housekeeping items…"

✓ Do this

"How many of you have a business that works but can't explain it in one sentence? Drop a YES."

Who this is for, and who it isn't

Name your Golden Avatar™ out loud, and name who should leave. Saying who it's not for is what makes "this is for you" believable.

The open loop

Promise something valuable at the end, while they're still deciding whether to stay. Then actually deliver it.

The enemy and the guide

Name the real thing to blame, and it must not be them. You're the guide with the map. The room is the hero.

✕ Not that

"You just haven't been consistent enough."

✓ Do this

"You were taught to sell like a shopfront when you're actually a specialist."

Your $Million Moment™

The discovery story, told as a scene: where you were, who was there, what was said, what it cost. It must be true and yours.

The trap: compressing your story into a summary. "I struggled for years before I figured it out" loses a room that was just starting to lean in.

2

Phase 2 · WHATToken · Opportunity

You reveal the system and make them want it. You are not teaching it.

The Big Shift

One reframe, stated like a law: "The reason [problem] keeps happening isn't [what they think], it's [your insight]." Say it, pause, let it land.

The System Reveal

Your Magic Formula™, three phases and nine steps, shown whole. Name each step by what it gets them, not what they'd do.

Hell → Heaven

For your core steps, life without the system and life with it, in concrete scenes. Then ask for a response.

✕ Not that

"You'll feel more confident and in control."

✓ Do this

"Right now you check your phone at 7am hoping someone booked."

The Confidence Check

"Drop a YES if you can see how this changes [their outcome]." Then wait. If the room is quiet, slow down, re-land the struggle in their words and ask again.

The trap: teaching. If someone could leave the room and do a step from what you showed, you taught it, and turned a buyer into a do-it-yourselfer.

3

Phase 3 · WHYSniper Close™ · Remove Risk → the offer

You dissolve what's holding them back, then you ask.

Belief / Bust-It

The reasons they believe it won't work for them. Take them from your Golden Avatar™, in their words, and bust each one with logic or proof.

Broken Models

Two or three approaches they've already tried, and why each one fails by design. Be fair to each. They've blamed themselves enough.

The Pitch Primer

The one line where you cross from teaching into selling: "You've seen the what and the why. Let me show you the how, together." Then go, without flinching.

Value Stack → Anchor → Bridge → Price

Walk the stack line by line from your Red Diamond Offer™. Anchor high and honestly. Bridge. Then the price, plainly, and stop talking.

The close

Your chosen close, with clear numbered steps on screen. Then let the silence sit.

The trap: price before value. Say the number first and every good thing after it sounds like justification.

4

Phase 4 · WHENRe-Pitch · Execution

Most of the room won't move on the first ask. This phase is for them, and it's where a lot of the money is.

Objections, raised before they're asked

Name the three to five biggest outside objections yourself: time, money, "tried before," "my niche is different." Then answer them.

The Tour

Walk them through their first two weeks inside. Every sentence starts with "you'll," never "you get."

The Fork in the Road

Two honest futures: where they'll be in 90 days if they act, and if they don't. Price the "don't" side with arithmetic, not adjectives.

Urgency, the re-pitch and the send-off

Urgency must be true, like a cohort that starts or a bonus that expires. Then your second ask, and a send-off that delivers the Token and leaves them lifted.

The trap: only asking once. Two asks, with content between them that earns the second, is the minimum.

06

Making the Words Land

Run it in order, always. Move a beat and you're asking people to buy before they're ready.

Running long? Cut depth, never beats. Fewer proof points, a tighter story, three steps walked instead of nine. Protect your $Million Moment™ and the Fork in the Road at any length.

Six checks on every line you'll say

  1. Would you actually say it? Read it aloud to one person across a table. Contractions on, simple words.
  2. Can they see it? A number or a name beats a category every time.
  3. One job per line. Cut every word that isn't doing work.
  4. Their words, not yours. Reuse your avatar's real language. "You" should outnumber "I," except in your $Million Moment™.
  5. Rhythm. Short. Then longer, so the short one lands.
  6. The AI sweep. Cut words like delve, unlock, unleash, elevate, seamless, game-changer and next level.

Then read the whole script out loud, once. Your mouth catches what your eyes miss. It takes about twenty minutes, and it's the highest-value twenty minutes in the build.

What if you haven't got proof yet?

Borrowed proof is legitimate. Invented proof never is.

Industry figures from a credible source you can point to are real proof. Your own story, and your first client, are proof too. What you must never do is make a number up or imply someone else's result was yours.

If you drop in a figure you haven't checked, mark it [VERIFY BEFORE DELIVERING] and go back to it. Never let an unchecked figure reach a live room.

07

Story Bits, the Emotional Engine

Facts inform. Stories move. And it's emotion, not logic, that makes the sale.

So every act gets a story bitA timeless, repeatable story that does one job at one moment: build engagement, create hype, break a belief, break an objection or prime the close..

Every bit has the same six parts

  1. Hook. An engagement setup: "how many of you…?"
  2. Story. The metaphor, with call-and-response built in.
  3. Turn. The bridge from the story to their life.
  4. Target. The belief or objection it breaks. This is why the bit exists.
  5. Soundbyte. The one line they'll repeat afterwards.
  6. Transition. The hand-off into the next beat.

Where they go

Pre-pitch bits warm the room in Phases 1 and 2. Re-pitch bits break beliefs and objections in Phases 3 and 4.

Count acts, not phases. Five acts means at least five bits, and Phase 1 carries two: an engagement bit early and an empowering-belief bit after your $Million Moment™.

09

The Hard Rules

These keep the presentation yours, and keep it closing. The Builder holds you to every one.

  1. Arrange, don't rewrite. Every beat pulls from your locked assets.
  2. Never skip or reorder an act. Longer events add depth and story, never a new order.
  3. One of the three closes, matched to your Money Model.
  4. A story in every act. An act with no bit is a lecture slide, not a moment.
  5. Spoken, not written. Short sentences, direct address, engagement built in. Never read your sales letter at the room.
  6. Slide and speech stay apart. Every beat has a slide line and a speaker line, so your talk track never lands on the wall.
  7. Only your real numbers and true stories. Borrowed parables get credited. Unchecked figures get marked.

11

Check Your Work

Almost every presentation that doesn't close fails in one of these six ways.

✕ Teaching instead of moving

The room leaves "satisfied," with no reason to buy.

✓ Reveal the what and the why

Save the how for the offer. Put a story where the extra teaching was.

✕ A quiet room

No hands, no chat. Drifted people don't buy.

✓ Train the room early

Then keep asking for a YES, a fill-in-the-blank, a hand.

✕ Apologising for the pitch

"Sorry, here comes the salesy part." Now they think it's something to be ashamed of.

✓ Own the transition

"Let me show you how we do this together." No apology, no flinch.

✕ Skipping the Fork in the Road

Without both futures, they default to "I'll think about it."

✓ Paint it honestly

Where they'll be in 90 days if they act, and if they don't.

✕ Wrong length for the phase

A 3-day Challenge while you're still validating, or a big close crammed into a short Masterclass.

✓ Match the format to your phase

Take the format your Money Model points to, and earn your way up.

✕ Borrowing a story as your own

It works until someone in the room has heard it before.

✓ Your stories in the personal slots

Library parables told as parables, credited.
