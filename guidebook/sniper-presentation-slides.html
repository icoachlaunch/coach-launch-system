01

Your Script, On Screen

The Sniper Presentation™ Slides take the script you approved in Part 2 and turn it into a full slide deck, in your brand, in minutes.

Most people treat slides as a design project. You fight templates for hours and end up with a deck you use once. Next launch, you start over from a blank file.

Here, the design lives in reusable moldsDesigned slide layouts. Each one is the structure of a slide: which boxes are on it and what fills them. The skill copies these and never writes slide code of its own. instead. So your slide work finally compounds. A new event is a new script through the same molds. A rebrand is one block of colour and type.

And your script already comes as slides in disguise. Every beat has a slide line (what's on screen) and a speaker line (what you say). The skill turns each beat into a real slide and keeps your speaker line as private notes.

The principle

You don't have a slide problem. You have a template problem.

04

The Rule That Protects Your Script

This tool never writes or changes a single word of your copy.

Your script was built, coached and passed an 8-point Ready Check in Part 2. If a slide tool quietly reworded a headline to make it fit, your approved script would no longer be approved, and nobody would know.

So the line is hard. Its only freedom is where a line breaks. It never tightens a phrase, fixes a typo, reorders a sentence or trims a list.

When copy doesn't fit

It stops and shows you a fit flagA note that a beat has more copy than its mold can hold, like "slide 22 has nine value-stack rows, the mold holds eight." You decide what to cut, in your script.. You decide what to cut, in your script, and it rebuilds that slide.

✕ Not that

"Tighten slide 6." It will refuse, and it should.

✓ Do this

Change the line in your script, then tell it what moved. It rebuilds those slides.

The principle

Your approved words in, your slides out. What's on the screen is exactly what you approved.

08

Images Are Optional

You need zero artwork to build this deck. Every mold works from words alone, and the skill never asks you for a photo, a logo or a diagram.

If you don't supply an image, the slot is removed and the slide reflows. It never sits there as an empty grey box.

When you do want one

Any slide can take a background. A veil is laid over it automatically so your text stays readable.

Restraint is the whole skill. Three or four across a full deck is plenty. A clean text slide in your brand beats a decorative photo every time.

The image-prompt framework in your resources has six copy-paste templates, sorted by what the image does: background, hero, make a point, object, texture and people. Any image generator works. Ready-made placeholder images ship with the kit too.

✕ Never generate a person

A generated face on your intro slide, or a generated "client" on a proof slide. That's a fabricated testimonial, whatever the intention.

✓ The real thing

A real photo of you, and your real Event Magnet™ from Step 3. Anonymous, face-not-visible people are the only exception.

The trap: letting AI put words inside an image. It misspells them and goes off-brand. Text stays live on the slide. The image is only the picture behind it.

09

Changing It Later

Your words and your design live in separate files, so there are only two kinds of change.

ChangeWhat you doWhat happens
New look, same wordsName another look and say "rebuild."The whole deck changes character in minutes. Not a word re-approved.
New wordsEdit your script, or re-run Part 2, then say what changed.It rebuilds only the affected slides.

Your first deck is the slow one. After that, a new event is a new script through the same molds.

Tips for great decks

  1. Script first, always. The deck is only as good as the script. The script is the spec.
  2. Feed it your real Foundations. The more it knows your brand, the more the deck looks like you.
  3. Fewer, richer slides. One real idea per slide. The teaching is in your mouth, not on the wall.
  4. Use your Presenter View. Rehearse with S, present with P on a second screen.
  5. Fix fit flags in the script. Cutting it there usually makes the spoken version better too.
  6. Keep it one file. The single file opens with a double-click and works offline.

Check Your Work

These are the mistakes that make a deck work against you.

✕ Writing your pitch inside this tool

It builds slides. It doesn't write the pitch.

✓ Script first

Build your script with the Sniper Presentation™ Builder in Part 2, then bring it here.

✕ Expecting it to fill a proof slide

It won't invent testimonials, stats or results, and you wouldn't want it to.

✓ Real proof in the script

If there's none yet, it drops the proof slide and tells you.

✕ Asking it to reword a headline

"Tighten slide 6." It will refuse.

✓ Change it in the script

Then say what moved. Your approved words stay approved.

✕ Your talk track on the slide

The room reads ahead of you.

✓ One idea per slide

Your speaker script lives in the notes, where only you see it.

✕ The blank-file habit

Treating every deck as a from-scratch design project.

✓ Reuse the molds

A new event is a new script. A rebrand is one block.

Is your deck ready?

  • Every slide is one idea, 16:9, with nothing overflowing. Readable from the back of the room.
  • Your talk track is in the presenter notes, not on the audience slide.
  • Every colour, font and logo matches your Visual Style Guide.
  • Every claim, number, price and quote traces to your locked assets. Nothing invented.
  • Proof slides use only your real testimonials and results.
  • Your $Million Promise™, offer and price appear exactly as you wrote them.
  • It plays in the Slide Viewer, and Presenter View shows your script and timer.
