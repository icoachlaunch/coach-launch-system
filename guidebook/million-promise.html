01

One Line Your Business Hangs On

Your $Million Promise™ is the one sentence your whole business hangs on.

It tells a stranger who you help, the outcome they get, how fast they get it, and the painful thing they get to skip. Usually in under 25 words.

It matters because everything else in your marketing flows out of it.

Every ad pulls from this promise. Every email opens with a version of it. Every post, sales page and event hook is this promise, dressed up for a different room.

When the promise is sharp, the work downstream gets easier. The ads write themselves, and sales conversations feel like agreeing, not convincing.

When it's fuzzy, the opposite happens, quietly and expensively.

You pay for ads that don't convert, post content nobody engages with, and sit through sales calls that drag. Most people who think they have a traffic problem actually have a promise problem.

60s

The test. If your ideal client doesn't feel read within a minute of hearing your promise, it isn't done yet. It's still too broad, too vague, or too much about you.

Matthew White built Coach Launch on this idea.

$24.6 Million in high-ticket sales generated and $5.2 Million in recurring client results delivered, all sitting downstream of promises that were clear before a single ad ran.

What it is not

It's not a slogan. Slogans are built to sound nice, not to make one person feel understood.

It's not your mission or your "why." A cold prospect doesn't care about your values yet.

It's not a list of everything you do, and it's not a description of your method. The reader doesn't care how you do it until they believe you can get them the result.

The principle

If you could paste your line onto a competitor's website and nobody noticed, it isn't a promise yet.

03

How You'll Build It

You don't write a $Million Promise™ by staring at a blank page. You find five parts, in order, and then snap them together.

The five parts are your Ideal Audience, your Core CurrencyThe one result your audience wants most. The thing they'd trade money to get., your Measurable MetricThe number or milestone on that result, like "5 new clients" instead of "more clients.", your Target Timeline and your Painful PitfallsThe things they've tried that failed, or dread having to do, to get the result..

Then you run the line through four quick filters and polish it into three versions.

"I help [audience] [get metric + currency] in [timeline], without [pitfall]."

04

The Five Parts, In Full

Here's each part: what it is, why it matters, how to find yours, and the trap that quietly kills it.

1

Ideal AudienceWho you help

The one type of person you most want to reach. Not everyone you could help, but the one you build the whole promise around.

Why it matters. The audience is the first thing a stranger hears. Name a fuzzy "everyone" and nobody recognises themselves.

How to find yours

Say who they call themselves: a profession, a group or a situation you could pick on an ad platform. "Real estate agents." "New dads." "SaaS founders in their first year."

Then check the market is big enough to advertise to: roughly 3–5 million+ people, or 1 million+ businesses.

Too small? Widen the area, add a couple of concentric avatarsRelated, overlapping groups, like "coaches, consultants and course creators." Never three unrelated audiences stapled together., or grow by referral instead of ads.

✕ Not that

"Growth-minded entrepreneurs who want freedom." That's a mindset, not a market.

✓ Do this

"Newly divorced women over 40 who want to rebuild their finances." A real person you can picture and target.

The trap: hiding behind a mindset ("motivated," "ambitious") because narrowing feels like turning money away. The narrower the person, the more of them say "that's me."

2

Core CurrencyThe one outcome

The single result your audience wants most. You can probably deliver several. This is the ONE that makes them lean forward.

Why it matters. People don't buy a bunch of benefits. They buy the one outcome that's been keeping them up at night.

How to find yours

Ask: "If I could get you just ONE of these in the next 90 days, which would it be?"

Most outcomes are something they gain (clients, revenue), something they keep (assets, health) or something they get through (a divorce, an exam, a launch).

Run the 3AM test: is this what they actually lie awake over? A plan or a blueprint is what you give them, not what they get.

✕ Not that

"Transformation." "Clarity." "Unlock your potential." Nobody searches for those at 3AM.

✓ Do this

"Booked sales calls." "Pounds lost." "Custody of your kids kept." Countable, and worth losing sleep over.

The trap: reaching for a big, inspiring word because it feels bigger. Vague feels safe and sells nothing.

3

Measurable MetricThe number on it

The number or milestone on your currency. "Lose weight" is forgettable. "Lose 20 lbs" stops the scroll.

Why it matters. A number turns a wish into a promise, because the reader can picture the finish line.

How to find yours

Use a real figure from client results, a solid industry benchmark or your best honest estimate. If it's an estimate, say "up to."

Keep the range tight, no wider than about 2x. "5–10 clients" is believable. "5–20" sounds like a guess.

✕ Not that

"More clients." "Better sleep." A direction, not a metric.

✓ Do this

"5 new clients." "Debt-free in 18 months." A number they can picture.

The trap: staying vague so you can't be pinned down. A promise with no number never gets believed in the first place.

4

Target TimelineHow fast

How long it realistically takes your audience to hit the metric. One number, not a range: "in 90 days," not "in three to six months."

Why it matters. A clear clock makes the outcome feel near instead of "someday."

How to find yours

Pick the honest time most clients take to get their first real win. That's usually 30–90 days for a core program.

If part of it is out of your hands, promise the part you control. If the full journey is long, sell the first win, not the whole road.

✕ Not that

"Quickly." "Soon." "In 3–6 months." A range reads as "I'm not sure."

✓ Do this

"In 90 days." "In your first 30 days." One number you can stand behind.

The trap: giving a range to feel safe. Commit to the single number most clients actually hit.

5

Painful PitfallsWhat they get to skip

The things they believe they'd have to do, or have already tried and failed at, to get the result. Your promise lets them skip one or two.

Why it matters. Naming the exact thing they hate proves you understand them. That's what turns "nice outcome" into "finally, someone gets it."

How to find yours

Reach for real failed attempts, not feelings. What did they try that didn't work? What do they dread having to do?

Pick one or two, and say them the way they'd say them out loud: "without burning a dollar on ads."

✕ Not that

"Without feeling overwhelmed." A feeling happens to them. It isn't something they tried.

✓ Do this

"Without cold-calling." "Without a big ad budget." A real thing they tried, hated or dread.

The trap: naming a frustration ("stuck") instead of the concrete thing that flopped.

05

Putting It Together

Once you have all five parts, snap them into one line. The shape flexes to your outcome.

"I help [audience] [get / keep / get through] [metric + currency] in [timeline], without [pitfall]."

Then test the draft against four filters. If one fails, fix it before you polish.

  1. Mechanism check. Is it about their outcome, not your system? Cut "using my method."
  2. The 3AM test. Would someone actually wake at 3AM over this?
  3. Plain language. Would they say it out loud, in these words? A stranger should get it instantly.
  4. Painkiller, not vitamin. Does it relieve real pain, or is it just nice to have?
The principle

Keep the audience in every version. The person you help is the first thing that makes a stranger stop.

06

The Copy-Chief Polish

The last move is to sharpen the line until every word earns its place. The Builder gives you three versions, because the same promise can be cut three ways.

Version ATight

The shortest, punchiest cut. Under 20 words, nothing wasted.

Version BComplete

Every part in a natural flow, around 20–25 words.

Version CBold

The strongest verbs and the most vivid words, audience still in.

Good polish means strong verbs (land, hit, add, end, keep), words they'd actually use, and no method sneaking back in.

Read all three out loud. The one you'd be least embarrassed to say to a stranger's face is usually the one.

08

The Hard Rules

These keep a promise specific enough to sell. The Builder holds you to every one.

  1. A real audience, never a mindset. A person you could target: a profession, a group, a situation.
  2. Big enough to sell to. Roughly 3–5M+ people or 1M+ businesses, or grow by referral on purpose.
  3. One currency, not a list. Every extra outcome waters the promise down.
  4. A real number, tight range. No wider than about 2x. "Up to" if it's an estimate.
  5. One timeline, the part you control. A single number, never more than 180 days up front.
  6. Failed attempts, not feelings. Two pitfalls at most, in plain words.
  7. Their outcome, not your method. No "using my system."
  8. Under 25 words, plain enough to say out loud.

Check Your Work

Almost every weak promise fails in one of these six ways. Check your line against each one.

✕ An audience that's really "everyone"

"I help entrepreneurs." A promise for everyone lands on no one.

✓ One person you could target

"First-year online coaches." Narrower always lands harder.

✕ A big, vague outcome

"Transformation," "freedom." You can't measure it, so you can't sell it.

✓ The real thing underneath

"Freedom" becomes "10 hours back a week."

✕ No number on the outcome

"More clients." Nobody can picture "more."

✓ A tight, real number

"5 new clients," with "up to" if it's an estimate.

✕ A timeline that's a range

"In 3–6 months." A range sounds unsure.

✓ One number you control

"In 90 days."

✕ Pitfalls that are feelings

"Without feeling overwhelmed." That's not something they tried.

✓ The failed attempt underneath

"Without juggling five funnels."

✕ Your method sneaking in

"Using my 7-step framework." It makes the line about you.

✓ Their result only

Cut the method. It gets its moment in Step 2, the Magic Formula™.

Is it ready?

  • The audience is a specific, targetable person, not a mindset.
  • The market is big enough to advertise to, or you're growing by referral on purpose.
  • There's ONE core currency they lose sleep over, with a clear number on it.
  • There's one timeline, not a range, and it's the part you control.
  • The pitfall is a real failed attempt, in plain words.
  • It's about their outcome, under 25 words, and a stranger gets it instantly.
  • It scored 18 or more, with no part under 3.
