01

Your Story, Held Up As a Mirror

Your $Million Moment™ is the true story of how you discovered the way you help people.

It's the honest account of the struggle you were in, the moment everything shifted, and how that moment became the method you use now.

At the heart of it is one pivotal moment: the moment your system was born. That's the $Million Moment™.

And here's the part most people miss. This story isn't really about you.

Told right, your ideal client doesn't hear a story about a coach. They see themselves. Your old struggle looks like theirs, and the fear you had is the fear they carry today.

That's the moment they start to trust you, before you've sold a single thing.

Your ads grab attention and your promise names the outcome. But a stranger buys from someone they believe understands them.

Matthew White built Coach Launch on stories that connect, not credentials that impress.

$24.6 Million in high-ticket sales generated and $5.2 Million in recurring client results delivered, all downstream of one simple truth: people follow the person whose story they see themselves in.

What it is not

It's not a resume or a bio. Nobody sees themselves in your titles and years.

It's not a highlight reel. The everything-went-great version is exactly what people scroll past.

It's not bragging. The results matter, but they're proof of what's possible for the reader, not a victory lap.

And it's not really about you. You're the guide who's walked the road, not the hero of the tale.

The principle

If your story makes people think "wow, impressive" instead of "wow, that's me," it isn't a $Million Moment™ yet.

03

How You'll Build It

You don't write a discovery story by staring at a blank page trying to sound inspiring. You get interviewed.

The $Million Moment™ Builder sits across from you and asks the right questions, one at a time, until the real story comes out. Then it shapes your answers into the finished story.

Every question comes with two helpers: why it's being asked, and a small example of the kind of answer that works. You're never left guessing.

The interview follows P.I.V.O.T.Matthew's story-interview framework: five stages that follow the natural emotional arc of a real change, from the struggle to the proof., wrapped with a short warm-up at the start and a quick refinement at the end.

StageWhat it captures
P · Pain PointThe struggle that kicked off your journey, and the one your ideal client is living now
I · Identity CrisisHow that struggle changed who you thought you were, and who you feared you'd become
V · Vital MomentThe exact moment everything shifted. This is your $Million Moment™
O · Overcoming ProcessHow you turned that insight into a repeatable method, and gave it a name
T · Transformation EvidenceThe proof it works, framed as what's now possible for them

Along the way you name your method. At the end it all becomes one flowing story that lands on a single line.

04

Why It's an Interview

The gold is buried, and questions dig better than a blank page. Your best material lives in the moments you'd normally skip.

The Builder uses a handful of proven techniques to pull the real story out. Knowing them helps you give better answers.

TechniqueWhat it means for you
Easy first, deep laterSafe, simple questions come before anything tender. The warm-up is doing real work, so don't rush it.
"What" and "how," not "why""Why" puts you on the defensive. "What happened?" and "how did it feel?" just ask you to describe.
Your senses and your body"My stomach dropped when I opened the bank app" lands harder than "it was a stressful time."
Before versus afterYou'll compare then and now again and again. The gap between the two is the story.
Specifics over summariesA moment, a place, a real number, the actual words someone said. "I was struggling" is a summary, not a story.
About them, not youEvery stage ties back to your Golden Avatar™: their pain, their fear, the belief that keeps them stuck.
A word on impostor syndrome

A lot of people freeze here and think, "my story isn't special enough." That's impostor syndrome talking, not the truth.

Your story doesn't have to be dramatic. It has to be real and specific. The small, honest moments connect hardest, so just be honest and go a little deeper than feels comfortable.

05

The P.I.V.O.T. Stages, In Full

Here's each stage: what it is, why it matters, how to find yours, and the questions you'll be asked.

Warm-up: Rapport

Before any digging, the interview starts easy. It builds the safety you need for the harder stuff, and it finds your real "why," which often becomes the warmest line in the story.

  • In a sentence or two, what do you do now, and who do you help?
  • How did this all start? Where were you at the very beginning of this journey?
  • What made you want to share your story and help these people? What's your real why?
P

Pain PointThe struggle that started it all

The specific challenge you were living with that kicked off your journey. The worst moment of it, what you'd already tried, and what was at stake.

Why it matters. This is the mirror. Your old pain is almost certainly the exact pain your Golden Avatar™ is living right now.

How to find yours

Go back to before you figured any of this out. Find one bad moment and describe it like a scene: where you were, what was happening, how it felt.

The questions you'll be asked

  • What specific challenge or frustration were you living with that kicked off this whole journey?
  • Take me to a moment when it was at its worst. Where were you? What was happening? What were you feeling?
  • What had you already tried to fix it, and why didn't those work?
  • If nothing had changed, where would you have ended up? What was really at stake?
✕ Not that

"I faced some challenges early in my business and had to overcome adversity."

✓ Do this

"I was posting every single day, getting two likes and no clients, watching my savings drain."

The trap: leading with credentials. The struggle connects. The resume repels.

I

Identity CrisisWho you were afraid you'd become

The inside of the struggle. How the pain changed the way you saw yourself and your future, and what felt most at risk.

Why it matters. It's the part almost everyone skips, and the part that connects deepest. Naming your fear gives your reader permission to admit theirs.

How to find yours

Ask what the struggle was costing you beyond money or time. What would it have meant about you if you'd quit? If it feels tender to write, you're in the right place.

The questions you'll be asked

  • How did this challenge change how you saw yourself, or your future?
  • What were you afraid might never be possible for you because of it?
  • What felt most at risk in that season, the thing you couldn't bear to lose?
✕ Not that

"It was a difficult period professionally." Still outside. Where's the fear?

✓ Do this

"I started to think maybe I was just kidding myself, that I wasn't a real coach, just someone playing at it."

The trap: telling the events and leaving out the inner life. The story stays on the surface.

V

Vital MomentThe turn, and your $Million Moment™

The exact moment something shifted: the realisation, the second the fog lifted. This is the moment the whole story is named for.

Why it matters. Done right, the reader has the realisation with you. That's what makes them believe change is possible for them too.

How to find yours

Pinpoint it. Where were you, what did you think, what did you feel in your body? If there were several turning points, pick the ONE that seeded what you do now.

The questions you'll be asked

  • Can you pinpoint the exact moment something shifted for you? Where were you when it happened?
  • What was the insight or realisation that changed everything?
  • What were you thinking in that moment?
  • What did you feel in your body when it hit you?
✕ Not that

"Eventually, things started to click and I figured out a better way." Which moment?

✓ Do this

"A mentor looked at my calendar and said, 'You're giving away the most valuable hour of your day for free.' I felt my face go hot."

The trap: a vague "it clicked." Slow this part down. It deserves more detail than anything else in the story.

O

Overcoming ProcessHow you built your system

Where the insight becomes a method. Your first action, what it cost you to figure out, the approach you built and the obstacles you hit. And this is where you name it.

Why it matters. A one-off win could be luck. A system you built, tested and can hand to someone else is what makes you a guide worth following.

How to find yours

Walk through what you actually did after the Vital Moment. Then give it a working name: "the ___ System" or "the ___ Method."

The questions you'll be asked

  • What was the very first thing you did after that realisation?
  • What did it cost you to figure this out: the support, the learning, the trial and error?
  • What system or approach did you start building out of that insight?
  • What unexpected obstacles hit as you put it into practice, and how did you handle them?
  • Let's give it a working name. What do you call your system?
✕ Not that

"I just changed my mindset and everything got better." What did you build? What's it called?

✓ Do this

"I started charging for every call and built a three-question filter to qualify people first. I called it the Paid-First Method."

The trap: skipping the name because it doesn't feel perfect yet. A working name is enough.

T

Transformation EvidenceThe proof

The real results your system produced, for you and for the people you've helped. The before and after, what's possible now, and the feedback that meant the most.

Why it matters. Your struggle earned trust and your Vital Moment gave hope. This stage proves the hope is real.

How to find yours

Gather the real numbers and real changes, yours first, then your clients'. Only use numbers you can stand behind, and balance the feeling with the facts.

The questions you'll be asked

  • What real results have you, or your clients, gotten using your system?
  • How does daily life or work look now compared to before?
  • What can you (or they) do now that felt impossible before?
  • How are you helping others get the same transformation?
  • What's the most meaningful piece of feedback you've ever gotten about this work?
✕ Not that

"I became incredibly successful and now I'm a seven-figure industry leader." A trophy, about you.

✓ Do this

Your real result, then a client's real words about what changed for them. Proof of what's possible for the reader.

The trap: bragging. Same facts, humble angle.

Wrap-up: Refinement

The interview closes with a few reflective questions. They often surface the single best line in the whole story.

  • If you could go back and tell your struggling self one thing, what would it be?
  • What's the most important part of your story that your audience needs to hear?
  • Anything I didn't ask that would help me understand your journey better?

The sample answers come from two made-up coaches: a fitness coach for busy dads and a business coach who used to give away free calls. They show the shape of a good answer. If yours just echoes the example, the Builder asks, "That's the example. What's yours?"

Putting It Together

Once the interview is done, your answers are woven into one flowing story, in P.I.V.O.T. order and in your real words.

Pain → Identity Crisis → Vital Moment → the [Name] System is born → Proof → a warm turn to the reader

Figure 06 · The arc of a $Million Moment™. Every beat sets up the one in the middle.

  1. It opens on the pain, not your credentials, because the pain is what your reader recognises first.
  2. It carries the inner shift, not just the events, so the reader feels it.
  3. It keeps the Vital Moment front and centre. Everything else exists to set it up.
  4. It balances emotion with real proof.
  5. It talks to one person, your Golden Avatar™, the whole way through.

Naming your system

A named system feels real and repeatable in a way "my approach" never does. It's what people point to, remember and tell their friends about.

Don't agonise over it. Name it after what it does, who it's for, or how it feels to use. You'll formalise this exact system in Step 2, the Magic Formula™.

08

The Hard Rules

These keep a discovery story real and connecting. The Builder holds you to every one.

  1. It has to be true, and it has to be yours. Never borrowed, never invented. People can feel a fake story.
  2. Lead with struggle, not credentials.
  3. Every P.I.V.O.T. stage is there, all five, in order.
  4. Carry the outside and the inside. The events AND the shift in who you were.
  5. One clear Vital Moment. One specific turn that seeded your system, not five scattered ones.
  6. Real numbers only. An inflated result loses the trust the story just earned.
  7. Keep it about them. Every stage mirrors your Golden Avatar™.
  8. Name your system. The [Name] System is born in the Overcoming Process.

09

The Ready Check

Your $Million Promise™ and Golden Avatar™ are scored out of 25. Your $Million Moment™ is not scored. On purpose.

You can't give vulnerability a 4 out of 5. A story either rings true and makes someone see themselves, or it doesn't.

So instead it passes a Ready CheckEight plain yes-or-no checks. The story is ready only when every one is a yes. No points, no total..

CheckIt's a yes when…
True & theirsEvery part really happened to you. Nothing borrowed or invented.
Pain-led & mirroredIt opens on the struggle, and that struggle reflects your Golden Avatar™.
Identity shift presentIt carries who you were afraid you'd become, not just the events.
Vital Moment is specificOne clear moment the reader can stand inside.
System named & bornThe [Name] System is discovered and named, and it's repeatable, not luck.
Real proof, not braggingThe results are there, framed as what's possible for them.
Emotion and facts balancedIt makes someone feel something AND gives them evidence to believe.
Plain, human, about themIt sounds like you talking, and your Golden Avatar™ sees themselves in it.

If any check is a no, the story isn't ready. Go back to that stage and dig for the truth until it turns into a yes.

No Golden Avatar™ locked yet? Then the mirror checks are flagged unverified. Build Part 2, then come back and confirm the story reflects them.

Almost every weak discovery story fails in one of these eight ways. Check yours against each one.

✕ Leading with your resume

"I'm a certified coach with 10 years of experience…" It builds distance, not trust.

✓ Lead with the struggle

Start where your reader is right now: stuck, frustrated, afraid.

✕ A highlight reel

Everything went smoothly and you were always on the way up.

✓ The low point and the doubt

The scar builds the bond, not the trophy.

✕ Skipping the identity shift

The events, but none of the inner life.

✓ Name what it did to you

What you were scared it meant about you.

✕ A vague Vital Moment

"Over time I figured it out."

✓ One moment, as a scene

Where you were, what was said, what you felt.

✕ Making it about you

Impressive, detailed and entirely self-focused. It earns admiration, and admiration doesn't buy.

✓ A mirror for them

At every stage, ask: will my Golden Avatar™ see themselves here?

✕ Bragging in the proof

Big numbers, status, a victory lap.

✓ Proof of what's possible

Client changes and real feedback first. Same facts, humble angle.

✕ Borrowing or inventing

Stretching the truth because yours feels too ordinary.

✓ Your real story

The small, honest moments connect hardest.

✕ No named system

A lovely story with nowhere to land.

✓ A working name

"The ___ System" or "the ___ Method." You'll sharpen it in the Magic Formula™.
