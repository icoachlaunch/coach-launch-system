01

The One Buyer Your Promise Is For

Your Golden Avatar™ is the one real buyer your whole promise is built for, profiled deep enough that you know exactly who to target and what to say.

Your $Million Promise™ named who you help in a few words. The Golden Avatar™ takes that name and goes deep.

Who this person really is. Where they're stuck right now. What they've already tried that failed.

The one outcome they'd pay for today, the objections that stop them, the places they hang out, and the way they actually talk about all of it.

It matters because everything you do to reach people runs on this.

Your ads are only as good as your picture of who to target. Your content only lands if it's written to one person with one pain. Your sales calls only feel easy when the prospect already feels understood.

When the avatar is vague or invented, the opposite happens, quietly and expensively.

Most people who think they have a targeting problem or a content problem really have an avatar problem. They never pinned down the real human, so every message is aimed at a ghost.

1

The rule that changes everything. Every field comes from one real source: a call, a review, a DM, a survey, a client you've served. If you're making it up, you're writing fiction.

Matthew White built Coach Launch on messages aimed at real, specific people.

$24.6 Million in high-ticket sales generated and $5.2 Million in recurring client results delivered, all downstream of knowing exactly who the buyer was before a single ad ran.

What it is not

It's not a made-up character. "Marketing Mary, 34, likes yoga and podcasts" feels productive and targets no one.

It's not a demographic slice on its own. Age and job title tell an ad platform who to show, not what makes the person care.

It's not your dream client. It's a portrait of who you actually serve now.

And it's not one giant "everyone I could help" blob. Describe your whole market as one person and the profile stops being useful.

The principle

If you can't point to a real call, review or client behind a line in your profile, that line isn't research. It's a guess.

03

How Many Avatars You Need

Before you profile anyone, you answer one question. And it's a math question, not a matter of taste.

The answer comes from market size, using the same sizing rule as your $Million Promise™. You want a market big enough to run paid ads to: roughly 5 million+ people for a B2C offer, or about 1 million+ businesses for B2B.

  • Your Golden Avatar™, the exact buyer your promise names, always comes first.
  • If that one audience already clears the bar, one avatar is all you need. One sharp avatar is the strongest thing you can have.
  • If your bullseye is smaller, you add one or two overlapping avatarsRelated circles who want the same core outcome as your Golden Avatar™, just from a slightly different starting point. Also called concentric. until the combined market clears the bar.
  • You never pad the number with unrelated audiences. Three is the ceiling, not the goal.

Why does size drive the count? Run ads to a market that's too small and the platform runs out of people to show. Costs climb and results stall, however good your avatar is.

Overlapping vs unrelated

Overlapping avatars share a Core Currency. They all want the same result.

✕ Unrelated

Busy parents, single professionals and retirees. They want different things, so they'd need different promises. That's three businesses wearing one coat.

✓ Overlapping

Course creators, coaches and consultants. They all want more paying clients: same outcome, different person.

The test before you add one: does this person want the same core outcome as my Golden Avatar™, just in a different situation? If not, stop.

The Builder walks you through this first. It asks for your estimate of the market and never makes a number up.

04

How You'll Build It

You don't build an avatar by imagining a person and writing down what you assume. You pull from evidence you already have.

That means sales calls, reviews, DMs, surveys and real clients. You sort that evidence into seven fields, one avatar at a time.

Don't have the evidence yet? Then anything you build is a hypothesis, not a profile. The Builder marks those fields UNVERIFIEDA field with no real evidence behind it. It can't score above 3, and the report tells you what to go collect. and gives you homework: talk to five real people in your market.

One avatar = Snapshot + Situation + Pains & Failed Attempts + The One Outcome + Objections + Watering Holes + Their Real Words

In the Builder you can answer one field for one avatar, or give the answer for all of them at once if you already know it. Either way, every avatar has to pass a score before it locks.

05

The Seven Fields, In Full

Here's each field: what it is, why it matters, how to find yours, and the trap that quietly turns a profile into fiction.

1

Snapshot & LabelName the person

A short, memorable label plus one line on who this person is. The way you'd describe them to a friend over coffee.

Why it matters. A name makes the avatar usable. When you sit down to write, "who am I talking to?" has a one-word answer you can hold in your head.

How to find yours

Think of a real client, or a real pattern of clients. Name them after their stage or defining trait, then add one line on where they are right now.

The test: could you introduce them to a colleague in one sentence and have them picture who you mean?

✕ Not that

"Busy professional." "My ideal client." "Marketing Mary, 34, loves yoga." A stock caricature with nothing real behind it.

✓ Do this

"Second-Year Sarah: a real estate agent two years in, closing a few deals, stuck under six figures."

The trap: naming your dream buyer instead of the people who've actually paid you or shown up on calls.

2

Situation & DemographicsWhere they really are

The practical facts: rough age, role, B2B or B2C, income, solo or with a team, the tools they use. Plus the situation: just starting, plateaued or scaling, and what a normal week looks like.

Why it matters. Demographics tell an ad platform who to show. The situation is what makes the message land once they see it.

Demographics without situation is a mailing list. Situation without demographics is a feeling you can't target.

How to find yours

Look at who actually books calls and buys: their real ages, roles and stage. If you catch yourself writing what you assume, mark it to verify and check it against a few real people.

✕ Not that

"25–65, anyone in business, wants to grow." No stage, no reality.

✓ Do this

"35–45, real estate agent, 2–4 years in, solo, works 6 days a week, plateaued and frustrated."

The trap: stopping at age and income. Two people with the same job title at different stages buy completely different things.

3

Pains & Failed AttemptsWhat hurts, and what flopped

The pain that keeps them up at night around your core outcome, and what they've already tried that failed: a course, a coach, a tool, a tactic, a hire.

Why it matters. People don't buy because they hurt. They buy because they tried to fix the hurt and it didn't work.

Name the exact thing they tried and hated, and something clicks: this person gets my situation.

How to find yours

Sales calls are the goldmine. People describe exactly what they tried when they explain why they're talking to you.

Reviews often open with the failed path. Collect the two or three most painful, and use their words.

✕ Not that

"Overwhelmed." "Stuck." "Lacks confidence." Feelings, not attempts. Or "they've tried everything."

✓ Do this

"Bought a $2K course, watched two modules, quit." "Ran boosted posts for months and got nothing."

The trap: naming a feeling. Take each one and ask, "what did they actually do about this that didn't work?"

4

Desires & the One OutcomeWhat they'd pay for today

A few desires, then the ONE outcome they'd pay for right now. It has to match the Core Currency in your $Million Promise™.

Why it matters. One clear outcome is a laser. Five competing desires are a fog.

If the avatar's real desire doesn't match your promise, that's a signal. Either this isn't your bullseye, or your promise needs another look.

How to find yours

Pick the outcome that makes them reach for a card, not the one you're proudest of. It's often more concrete and more selfish than you'd like.

Sell the outcome they'd pay for today. Deliver the deeper change once they're in.

✕ Not that

"Freedom." "Confidence." "To finally feel successful." Or three end goals at once.

✓ Do this

"Their first 50 paying students." "5 new listings a month." One outcome, matched to the promise.

The trap: drifting into big, vague words. If you can't measure it, you can't build content and offers around it.

5

Objections & BeliefsThe brakes on the sale

Why they hesitate when they're this close to buying. Their objections are what they say out loud. Their limiting beliefs are the false things underneath.

Why it matters. Every objection you can name in advance is one your marketing answers before they raise it.

A limiting belief is even more valuable. One post that breaks "you need a huge audience to launch" can do more than ten posts about your offer.

How to find yours

Listen to the second half of sales calls and the reasons people give when they say no. You can't guess objections. You have to have heard them.

Price is usually the last mask. Dig past "I can't afford it" to the real fear, usually "it won't work for someone like me."

✕ Not that

"They'd probably say it's too expensive." A guess, with no belief behind it.

✓ Do this

Objection: "I've bought courses before and never finished them." Belief: "I need a big following before ads make sense."

The trap: inventing objections. An invented one sends your marketing after a fear nobody has.

6

Watering HolesExactly where they gather

The specific watering holesThe named places your buyer already spends time: groups, podcasts, creators, tools, events, newsletters. The places you can point an ad at. you could point an ad or a piece of content at. Names, not categories.

Why it matters. This is the field that turns a profile into a targeting plan. "Online" is everywhere, which is nowhere.

How to find yours

Push past your own list and followers. Those people already know you. The places that matter most are where the cold ones gather.

Don't know? Ask five real people in your market one question: "where do you go to learn about this?" Their answers become your targeting map.

✕ Not that

"Social media." "Online." "LinkedIn." Which part? Whose content? Or only "my own email list."

✓ Do this

"The [named] Facebook group for new agents." "[Specific] real estate podcast." "Uses [named CRM]."

The trap: guessing. A guessed watering hole points your ad spend at an empty room.

7

Voice of CustomerTheir real words

Three to five real quotes, word for word, the way they said them. Straight from a call, a review, a DM or a survey.

Why it matters. The fastest way to write copy that converts is to use the customer's own language back to them. It feels like recognition, not selling.

This becomes a swipe file you'll reuse for the life of your business.

How to find yours

Pull them from your evidence and don't clean them up. Keep their grammar, their word choices, their emphasis.

If you don't have real quotes yet, don't fake them. Mark the field to collect and go get three.

✕ Not that

"Struggles with lead generation." "Desires financial stability." You wrote that, not them.

✓ Do this

"I've spent more on courses than I've made back." Raw, specific, theirs.

The trap: polishing. If a quote makes you wince a little because it's so raw, it's perfect.

07

The Hard Rules

These keep a profile honest and usable. The Builder holds you to every one.

  1. Market size decides the count. As many avatars as it takes to reach roughly 5M+ people or 1M+ businesses, and no more.
  2. Evidence, not imagination. Every field comes from a real source, or it's flagged UNVERIFIED.
  3. Avatar #1 is the person your promise names. No quietly swapping in someone easier to imagine.
  4. Three avatars at most, and only if they overlap. Every extra one shares the same core outcome.
  5. Pains are failed attempts, not feelings.
  6. One outcome per avatar, matched to the promise. A mismatch means the avatar or the promise is off.
  7. Watering holes are specific and named. "Social media" is not a targeting plan.
  8. Voice of customer stays in their real words. Never polished into consultant-speak.
  9. Every avatar passes the score to lock. 18 out of 25 or more, with a 3 or better on every field.

08

How It's Scored

Each avatar is scored on five fields, 0 to 5 points each, for 25 in total. Your Snapshot and your real quotes feed these scores rather than getting their own.

Any field you can't back with real evidence caps at 3. So a profile full of guesses simply can't reach 18. That's by design.

Field52
Situation & DemographicsReal age, role, stage and daily reality, from actual clients"Anyone in business who wants to grow"
Pains & Failed AttemptsTwo or three real things they tried that flopped"Overwhelmed and stuck" (feelings)
Desires & the One OutcomeOne countable outcome that matches your promise"Freedom," or three goals at once
Objections & BeliefsReal objections from calls, plus a limiting belief to break"They'd say it's too expensive" (a guess)
Watering HolesNamed groups, shows, creators, tools, events"Social media" or "online"

What your score means

ScoreStatusWhat to do
18–25LockedEvidence-backed and specific. Move on to the $Million Moment™
13–17AlmostRaise every field under 3, then push the weakest up until you clear 18
0–12RebuildYou're likely guessing. Go get real calls, reviews and DMs first

Both conditions count. A single field sitting at 2 fails the whole avatar, even if the total looks fine, because one weak spot is where your marketing leaks.

Check Your Work

Almost every weak avatar fails in one of these eight ways. Check yours against each one.

✕ Inventing the customer

Ten minutes, seven fields, no one talked to. It points your marketing at a person who doesn't exist.

✓ Research every field

Pull from real calls, reviews and DMs. Mark anything unproven so you know what to go check.

✕ Three avatars when one would do

Every extra avatar splits your message and your budget.

✓ Size the market first

If your bullseye clears the bar, build one deeply and stop.

✕ Demographics with no situation

"35–50, business owner, $100K+." Who to show, not what to say.

✓ Add the stage and the week

Starting, plateaued or scaling, and what a normal week really looks like.

✕ Feelings instead of failed attempts

"Overwhelmed." That could be said to anyone.

✓ The attempt underneath

"Bought three courses and finished none."

✕ Too many outcomes, or a vague one

"Freedom, confidence, more clients and better systems."

✓ One outcome they'd pay for

Concrete, countable, and matched to your promise's Core Currency.

✕ Guessing objections

"They'd probably think it's too expensive."

✓ Objections you've heard

From real calls, with the deeper fear under the price.

✕ Vague watering holes

"Social media." "LinkedIn." You can't run an ad to "online."

✓ Named places

The specific groups, podcasts, creators, tools and events.

✕ Polishing their words

"Struggles with work-life balance and income."

✓ Word for word

"I'm working 60 hours a week and still broke."
